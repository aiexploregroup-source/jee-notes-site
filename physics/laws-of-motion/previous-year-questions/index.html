Laws of Motion (NLM) questions in JEE Main test whether you can draw a correct free-body diagram and then apply Newton’s second law, ΣF = ma, along sensible axes. This page has two parts. First come four questions with step-by-step solutions. They are marked “source verification pending” while we check them against the official papers. Then come 15 original JEE Main-style practice questions written by JEE Notes, covering friction, connected bodies and pulleys, inclined planes, pseudo forces in accelerating frames, and circular motion with friction. Every practice answer is fully worked using g = 10 m/s². Use it after revising the chapter notes and formula sheet.

PYQ · Source verification pending

The time taken by a block of mass $m$ to slide down from the highest point to the lowest point on a rough inclined plane is 50 % more compared to the time taken by the same block on identical inclined smooth plane. Both inclined planes are at $45^\circ$ with the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the rough inclined surface and block is _____.

Show solution

Both planes cover the same slope length $L$ starting from rest, so $L = \tfrac{1}{2}a t^2$, which gives $t \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a}}$.

Accelerations ($\theta = 45^\circ$, so $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$):

$$a_{\text{smooth}} = g\sin\theta, \qquad a_{\text{rough}} = g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta$$

Time ratio (rough time is 50 % more, i.e. $t_{\text{rough}} = 1.5\,t_{\text{smooth}}$):

$$\frac{t_{\text{rough}}}{t_{\text{smooth}}} = \sqrt{\frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}}} = 1.5 \implies \frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}} = \frac{9}{4}$$

Solve for $\mu$ (dividing by $g\cos\theta$ with $\tan 45^\circ = 1$):

$$\frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta} = \frac{1}{1-\mu} = \frac{9}{4}$$

$$1 - \mu = \frac{4}{9} \implies \mu = \frac{5}{9}$$

Answer: μ = 5/9

PYQ · Source verification pending

At $t = 0$, a body of mass 100 g starts moving under the influence of a force $\left(5\hat{i} + 10\hat{j}\right)\,\mathrm{N}$. After 2 s its position is $\left(2x\hat{i} + 5y\hat{j}\right)\,\mathrm{m}$. The ratio $x : y$ is __________.

Show solution

Mass $m = 100\ \text{g} = 0.1\ \text{kg}$. The body starts from rest at the origin.

Acceleration from $\vec{a} = \vec{F}/m$:

$$\vec{a} = \frac{5\hat{i} + 10\hat{j}}{0.1} = \left(50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}\right)\ \text{m/s}^2$$

Displacement after $t = 2$ s, $\vec{s} = \tfrac{1}{2}\vec{a}\,t^2 = 2\vec{a}$:

$$\vec{s} = 2\left(50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}\right) = \left(100\hat{i} + 200\hat{j}\right)\ \text{m}$$

Match components with $\left(2x\hat{i} + 5y\hat{j}\right)$:

$$2x = 100 \implies x = 50, \qquad 5y = 200 \implies y = 40$$$$x : y = 50 : 40 = 5 : 4$$

Answer: 5 : 4

PYQ · Source verification pending

A block takes $t$ time to slide down a plane inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. If the surface is made smooth (frictionless), the block takes time $\dfrac{t}{2}$ to slide down the plane. The coefficient of friction between the block and the inclined plane is $\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{100}\right)$. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

Show solution

Same slope length $L$ from rest, so $t \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a}}$.

Accelerations ($\theta = 45^\circ$):

$$a_{\text{rough}} = g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta, \qquad a_{\text{smooth}} = g\sin\theta$$

Time ratio ($t_{\text{smooth}} = t/2$, so rough time is twice the smooth time):

$$\frac{t_{\text{rough}}}{t_{\text{smooth}}} = \sqrt{\frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}}} = 2 \implies \frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}} = 4$$

Solve for $\mu$ (with $\tan 45^\circ = 1$):

$$\frac{1}{1-\mu} = 4 \implies 1 - \mu = \frac{1}{4} \implies \mu = \frac{3}{4} = 0.75 = \frac{75}{100}$$

Hence $\alpha = 75$.

Answer: 75

PYQ · Source verification pending

A car moving with a speed of 54 km/h takes a turn of radius 20 m. A simple pendulum is suspended from the ceiling of the car. Determine the angle made by the string of the pendulum with the vertical during the turning. (Take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)

Show solution

Convert the speed: $v = 54\ \text{km/h} = 54 \times \dfrac{5}{18} = 15\ \text{m/s}$.

In the car’s (non-inertial) frame, the bob experiences a pseudo (centrifugal) force $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ horizontally outward and gravity $mg$ downward. At equilibrium the string tilts by angle $\theta$ from the vertical:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{mv^2/r}{mg} = \frac{v^2}{rg}$$

Substitute values:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{(15)^2}{20 \times 10} = \frac{225}{200} = 1.125$$$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.125)$$

Answer: θ = tan⁻¹(1.125)

JEE Main-style practice questions (original)

These are original practice questions written by JEE Notes, not from any past paper. Take g = 10 m/s² throughout.

P1. A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal floor (μs = 0.4, μk = 0.3). A horizontal force of 15 N acts on it. What is the friction force?

(A) 20 N (B) 15 N (C) 12 N (D) 0

Show solution

The maximum static friction is μs·mg = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N. Since 15 N < 20 N, the block stays at rest and static friction balances the applied force: f = 15 N.

Answer (B).

P2. The same block is now pushed with 30 N. Find its acceleration.

(A) 2 m/s² (B) 3 m/s² (C) 4 m/s² (D) 6 m/s²

Show solution

30 N > 20 N, so the block slides and kinetic friction applies: f = 0.3 × 50 = 15 N. Then a = (30 − 15)/5 = 3 m/s².

Answer (B).

P3. Masses of 3 kg and 2 kg hang over an ideal (light, frictionless) pulley. Find the acceleration and the string tension.

(A) 2 m/s², 24 N (B) 2 m/s², 20 N (C) 4 m/s², 24 N (D) 2 m/s², 30 N

Show solution

a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂) = 10/5 = 2 m/s². For the 2 kg mass, T − 20 = 2 × 2, so T = 24 N. Check with the 3 kg mass: 30 − T = 3 × 2, which also gives T = 24 N.

Answer (A).

P4. A 4 kg block on a smooth table is tied by a string over a light pulley at the table’s edge to a hanging 1 kg mass. Find the tension.

(A) 10 N (B) 8 N (C) 2 N (D) 5 N

Show solution

a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂) = 10/5 = 2 m/s². For the block on the table, T = 4 × 2 = 8 N. Check with the hanging mass: 10 − 8 = 1 × 2.

Answer (B).

P5. A block slides down a 30° incline with μk = 1/(2√3). Find its acceleration.

(A) 5 m/s² (B) 2.5 m/s² (C) 3.75 m/s² (D) 1.25 m/s²

Show solution

a = g(sinθ − μcosθ) = 10(½ − (1/(2√3))(√3/2)) = 10(0.5 − 0.25) = 2.5 m/s².

Answer (B).

P6. What is the minimum coefficient of static friction needed to keep a block at rest on a 37° incline? (Use sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8.)

(A) 0.6 (B) 0.8 (C) 0.75 (D) 1.33

Show solution

At the limit, mg sinθ = μ mg cosθ, so μ = tanθ = 0.6/0.8 = 0.75.

Answer (C).

P7. A 60 kg person stands on a scale in a lift accelerating upward at 2 m/s². What does the scale read?

(A) 480 N (B) 600 N (C) 720 N (D) 120 N

Show solution

N − mg = ma, so N = 60(10 + 2) = 720 N.

Answer (C).

P8. A small block is pressed against the vertical front face of a cart, on the outside. The cart accelerates forward (horizontally) and pushes the block ahead of it. μs between the block and the face is 0.5. What minimum acceleration keeps the block from sliding down?

(A) 5 m/s² (B) 10 m/s² (C) 20 m/s² (D) 15 m/s²

Show solution

The face pushes the block forward, giving a normal force N horizontally. The block moves with the cart, so N = ma (ground frame). Equivalently, in the cart’s frame the backward pseudo force ma presses the block onto the face. Static friction acts upward and must balance gravity: f ≤ μN, so mg ≤ μma and a ≥ g/μ = 10/0.5 = 20 m/s². Check: at a = 20 m/s², N = 20m and μN = 10m = mg, which is exactly the limit.

Answer (C).

P9. A bob hangs from the roof of a train accelerating horizontally at 7.5 m/s². What angle does the string make with the vertical? (Use tan37° = 0.75.)

(A) 30° (B) 37° (C) 45° (D) 53°

Show solution

In the train’s frame, tanθ = a/g = 0.75, so θ = 37°.

Answer (B).

P10. A car takes a flat (unbanked) turn of radius 50 m with μs = 0.2. What is its maximum safe speed?

(A) 10 m/s (B) 20 m/s (C) 5 m/s (D) 14 m/s

Show solution

Friction supplies the centripetal force: mv²/r ≤ μmg, so v ≤ √(μrg) = √(0.2 × 50 × 10) = √100 = 10 m/s (36 km/h).

Answer (A).

P11. A coin sits 10 cm from the centre of a horizontal turntable, with μs = 0.4. What is the maximum angular speed before it slips?

(A) 2 rad/s (B) 2√10 rad/s (C) 4 rad/s (D) 20 rad/s

Show solution

mω²r ≤ μmg, so ω ≤ √(μg/r) = √(4/0.1) = √40 = 2√10 ≈ 6.32 rad/s.

Answer (B).

P12. Blocks of 2 kg and 3 kg touch each other on a smooth floor. A 20 N horizontal force pushes the 2 kg block. What force does the 2 kg block exert on the 3 kg block?

(A) 8 N (B) 20 N (C) 12 N (D) 4 N

Show solution

a = 20/5 = 4 m/s². For the 3 kg block alone, N = 3 × 4 = 12 N.

Answer (C).

P13. A 3 kg box lies on a 9 kg plank, and the plank rests on smooth (frictionless) ice. μs between the box and the plank is 0.3. A horizontal rope pulls the box, not the plank. What is the largest pull for which the box and the plank move together without slipping?

(A) 9 N (B) 12 N (C) 27 N (D) 36 N

Show solution

Now the plank is accelerated only by friction from the box, so the limit is set by the plank, not the box. Maximum friction is μs·m(box)·g = 0.3 × 30 = 9 N, so the plank’s maximum acceleration is 9/9 = 1 m/s². Moving together, F = (3 + 9) × 1 = 12 N. Check with the box: F − 9 = 3 × 1, so F = 12 N.

Answer (B).

P14. A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds along the same line at 8 m/s. Contact lasts 0.02 s. What is the average force from the wall?

(A) 50 N (B) 100 N (C) 450 N (D) 900 N

Show solution

The change in momentum is 0.5 × (8 − (−10)) = 9 kg·m/s, so F = 9/0.02 = 450 N.

Answer (C).

P15. At what banking angle can a car take a 40 m radius turn at 20 m/s with no friction needed?

(A) 30° (B) 37° (C) 45° (D) 60°

Show solution

tanθ = v²/(rg) = 400/400 = 1, so θ = 45°.

Answer (C).
