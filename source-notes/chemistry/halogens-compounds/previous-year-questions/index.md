PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

**Statement I:** 3-phenylpropene reacts with HBr and gives a secondary alkyl bromide having a chiral carbon atom as the major product.

**Statement II:** Aryl chlorides and aryl cyanides can be prepared by the Sandmeyer reaction as well as the Gattermann reaction.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> 3-phenylpropene is allylbenzene, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-}\text{CH}_2\text{-}\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$. Markovnikov addition of HBr places Br on the more substituted internal carbon:</p>$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CH}_2\text{-CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CH}_2\text{-}\underset{\text{Br}}{\text{CH}}\text{-CH}_3$$<p>The Br-bearing carbon carries four different groups ($\text{-Br}$, $\text{-H}$, $\text{-CH}_3$, $\text{-CH}_2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5$), so it is a chiral secondary bromide. <strong>True.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> Both routes start from a diazonium salt. Sandmeyer uses cuprous salts ($\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2$ for Ar-Cl, CuCN for Ar-CN); Gattermann uses copper powder with HCl (Ar-Cl) or with KCN/Cu (Ar-CN). Both give aryl chlorides and aryl cyanides. <strong>True.</strong></p><p>Both statements are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

**Statement I:** Benzyl chloride reacts faster in the $S_N1$ mechanism than ethyl chloride.

**Statement II:** The ethyl carbocation intermediate is less stabilized by hyperconjugation than the benzyl carbocation is by resonance.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The rate-determining step of $S_N1$ is carbocation formation, so the more stable the cation, the faster the reaction.</p><ul><li><strong>Benzyl cation</strong> $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-}\overset{+}{\text{CH}}_2$ is stabilized by <strong>resonance</strong> — the positive charge is delocalized into the aromatic ring (three additional resonance structures).</li><li><strong>Ethyl cation</strong> $\text{CH}_3\text{-}\overset{+}{\text{CH}}_2$ is a primary cation stabilized only by weak <strong>hyperconjugation</strong>.</li></ul><p>Since resonance stabilization $\gg$ hyperconjugation, the benzyl cation is far more stable, so benzyl chloride reacts faster in $S_N1$. <strong>Statement I is true.</strong> Statement II correctly states the reason and is also <strong>true</strong>, and it is the correct explanation of Statement I.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

Correct statements regarding alkyl halides (R-X) among the following are:

A. Alcohol being a less polar solvent than water, alcoholic KOH favours the elimination reaction with R-X.

B. Order of reactivity towards $S_N1$ is $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CH}_2\text{-Cl} > \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CHCl-C}_6\text{H}_5$.

C. Non-substituted aryl halides exhibit properties similar to alkyl halides.

D. Vinyl chloride is an example of a haloalkene and allyl chloride is an example of a haloalkyne.

E. R-Cl can be prepared by reacting R-OH with $\text{SOCl}_2$, but Ar-Cl cannot be prepared by reacting Ar-OH with $\text{SOCl}_2$.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Evaluate each statement:</p><ul><li><strong>A. True.</strong> Alcoholic KOH (a less polar medium) removes $\beta$-hydrogens and promotes <strong>elimination</strong> ($E2$), whereas aqueous KOH favours substitution.</li><li><strong>B. False.</strong> $S_N1$ rate follows cation stability. The benzhydryl cation $(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5)_2\overset{+}{\text{CH}}$ from $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CHCl-C}_6\text{H}_5$ is stabilized by <strong>two</strong> rings and is more stable than the benzyl cation, so the order is reversed: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CHCl-C}_6\text{H}_5 &gt; \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-CH}_2\text{-Cl}$.</li><li><strong>C. False.</strong> Aryl halides ($\text{C-X}$ has partial double-bond character, resonance) are far less reactive than alkyl halides; their properties are not similar.</li><li><strong>D. False.</strong> Allyl chloride $\text{CH}_2=\text{CH-CH}_2\text{-Cl}$ is a <strong>haloalkene</strong>, not a haloalkyne.</li><li><strong>E. True.</strong> Aliphatic alcohols give R-Cl with $\text{SOCl}_2$, but phenols (Ar-OH) do not give Ar-Cl with $\text{SOCl}_2$ because of the strong, partial-double-bond $\text{C(aryl)-O}$ linkage.</li></ul><p>Correct statements: <strong>A and E only.</strong></p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

n-Butane on monochlorination under photochemical conditions gives an optically active compound “P”. “P” on further chlorination gives dichloro compounds. The number of dichloro compounds obtained (ignore stereoisomers) is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Identify P.</strong> Monochlorination of n-butane $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH}_2\text{-CH}_2\text{-CH}_3$ gives 1-chlorobutane and 2-chlorobutane. Only <strong>2-chlorobutane</strong> $\text{CH}_3\text{-}\underset{\text{Cl}}{\text{CH}}\text{-CH}_2\text{-CH}_3$ has a chiral carbon, so it is the optically active product <strong>P</strong>.</p><p><strong>Second chlorination of 2-chlorobutane.</strong> Label the carbons $\text{C}^1\text{H}_3\text{-}\text{C}^2\text{HCl-}\text{C}^3\text{H}_2\text{-}\text{C}^4\text{H}_3$. A second Cl can replace an H at any of the four carbons:</p><ul><li>New Cl at $\text{C}^1$ → 1,2-dichlorobutane</li><li>New Cl at $\text{C}^2$ → 2,2-dichlorobutane</li><li>New Cl at $\text{C}^3$ → 2,3-dichlorobutane</li><li>New Cl at $\text{C}^4$ → 1,3-dichlorobutane (renumbering $\text{ClCH}_2\text{-CH}_2\text{-CHCl-CH}_3$)</li></ul><p>Ignoring stereoisomers, these are <strong>4 distinct</strong> dichloro compounds.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 4 (option B)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

**Statement I:** Due to increase in van der Waals forces, the order of boiling points is $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{I} > \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{I} > \text{CH}_3\text{I}$.

**Statement II:** As 1,4-dichlorobenzene (para) is more symmetric, its melting point is higher than 1,2-dichlorobenzene (ortho); however its boiling point is lower than that of 1,2-dichlorobenzene (ortho).

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Within a homologous series, increasing molecular size (surface area) increases van der Waals forces and hence boiling point. Since chain length increases $\text{CH}_3\text{I} &lt; \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{I} &lt; \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{I}$, the boiling-point order stated is correct. <strong>True.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> The <strong>para</strong> isomer packs into a more symmetric crystal lattice, giving a higher <strong>melting point</strong> than the ortho isomer (standard NCERT trend). For boiling point, the ortho isomer has a small net dipole moment while the para isomer’s opposing dipoles cancel; thus the para boiling point is slightly <strong>lower</strong> than the ortho. <strong>True.</strong></p><p>Both statements are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>
