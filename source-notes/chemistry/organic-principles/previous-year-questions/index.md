PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

Increasing order of electron withdrawing power of the following functional groups is:

a. $-\text{CN}$   b. $-\text{COOH}$   c. $-\text{NO}_2$   d. $-\text{I}$

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Electron-withdrawing power here is governed by the $-I$ (inductive) effect. The standard experimental order of $-I$ strength for these groups is:</p>$$-\text{NO}_2 \;&gt;\; -\text{CN} \;&gt;\; -\text{COOH} \;&gt;\; -\text{I}$$<p>The nitro group is the strongest electron-withdrawing group, followed by cyano, then carboxyl, and finally the iodo group is the weakest of the four.</p><p>Writing this in <strong>increasing</strong> order of electron-withdrawing power:</p>$$-\text{I} \;&lt;\; -\text{COOH} \;&lt;\; -\text{CN} \;&lt;\; -\text{NO}_2$$<p>Using the labels: $\;d &lt; b &lt; a &lt; c$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

**Statement I:** 2,6-diethylcyclohexanone and 6-methyl-2-n-propylcyclohexanone are metamers.

**Statement II:** 2,2,6,6-tetramethylcyclohexanone exhibits keto–enol tautomerism.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I — check molecular formulas.</strong> Cyclohexanone is $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$. Each ring substituent replaces one H.</p><ul><li>2,6-diethylcyclohexanone: add two $-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$ groups $\Rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_8\text{O}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2 = \text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{18}\text{O}$</li><li>6-methyl-2-n-propylcyclohexanone: add $-\text{CH}_3$ and $-\text{C}_3\text{H}_7 \Rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_8\text{O}(\text{CH}_3)(\text{C}_3\text{H}_7) = \text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{18}\text{O}$</li></ul><p>Same molecular formula, same functional group (ketone), but a different distribution of alkyl groups around the carbonyl — this is exactly <strong>metamerism</strong>. <strong>Statement I is TRUE.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II — check for $\alpha$-hydrogens.</strong> Keto–enol tautomerism requires at least one $\alpha$-hydrogen. In 2,2,6,6-tetramethylcyclohexanone, both $\alpha$-carbons (C-2 and C-6) are fully substituted by methyl groups, so <strong>there are no $\alpha$-hydrogens</strong>. Hence no enol can form. <strong>Statement II is FALSE.</strong></p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

Match List-I with List-II.

**List-I (Mixture of Compounds):** A. Diethylamine + Ethylamine; B. Acetaldehyde + Acetone; C. Ethanol + Phenol; D. Benzoic acid + Cinnamic acid

**List-II (Reagent used to distinguish):** I. Bromine water; II. $\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{KOH},\ \Delta$; III. Neutral $\text{FeCl}_3$; IV. Ammoniacal silver nitrate

Choose the correct answer.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Match each mixture with the reagent that distinguishes its two components:</p><ul><li><strong>A. Diethylamine (2°) + Ethylamine (1°):</strong> the carbylamine (isocyanide) test with $\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{KOH},\ \Delta$ is given only by the <strong>primary</strong> amine (foul-smelling isocyanide). Distinguishes them $\Rightarrow$ <strong>A–II</strong>.</li><li><strong>B. Acetaldehyde + Acetone:</strong> ammoniacal $\text{AgNO}_3$ (Tollens’ reagent) oxidises the <strong>aldehyde</strong> to give a silver mirror, but not the ketone $\Rightarrow$ <strong>B–IV</strong>.</li><li><strong>C. Ethanol + Phenol:</strong> neutral $\text{FeCl}_3$ gives a characteristic violet colour with <strong>phenol</strong>, not with ethanol $\Rightarrow$ <strong>C–III</strong>.</li><li><strong>D. Benzoic acid + Cinnamic acid:</strong> cinnamic acid has a C=C double bond and <strong>decolourises bromine water</strong> by addition; benzoic acid does not $\Rightarrow$ <strong>D–I</strong>.</li></ul><p>Therefore: A–II, B–IV, C–III, D–I.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

Match LIST-I with LIST-II.

**List-I (Reaction):** A. Williamson Synthesis; B. Friedel–Crafts Reaction; C. Bromination of vinylbenzene; D. Chlorination of toluene in light

**List-II (Mechanism):** I. Electrophilic addition; II. Free radical substitution; III. Nucleophilic substitution; IV. Electrophilic substitution

Choose the correct answer.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Classify each reaction by its mechanism:</p><ul><li><strong>A. Williamson ether synthesis:</strong> an alkoxide ($\text{R}-\text{O}^-$) attacks an alkyl halide by an $S_N2$ pathway $\Rightarrow$ <strong>nucleophilic substitution (III)</strong>.</li><li><strong>B. Friedel–Crafts reaction:</strong> an electrophile ($\text{R}^+$ or acylium) attacks the aromatic ring $\Rightarrow$ <strong>electrophilic substitution (IV)</strong>.</li><li><strong>C. Bromination of vinylbenzene (styrene):</strong> $\text{Br}_2$ adds across the C=C double bond of the vinyl group $\Rightarrow$ <strong>electrophilic addition (I)</strong>.</li><li><strong>D. Chlorination of toluene in light:</strong> $h\nu$ generates chlorine radicals that substitute at the benzylic $-\text{CH}_3$ (side chain) $\Rightarrow$ <strong>free radical substitution (II)</strong>.</li></ul><p>Therefore: A–III, B–IV, C–I, D–II.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

**Statement I:** In para-substituted $O_2N-C_6H_4-\overset{\oplus}{C}H-C_6H_4-OCH_3$, the carbocation is stabilised by the $+R$ effect of the $-OCH_3$ group.

**Statement II:** In para-substituted $O_2N-C_6H_4-\overset{\ominus}{C}H-C_6H_4-OCH_3$, the carbanion is stabilised by the $-R$ effect of the $-NO_2$ group.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I.</strong> The methoxy group $-\text{OCH}_3$ has a lone pair on oxygen and shows a $+R$ (electron-donating resonance) effect. Through the para position it pushes electron density toward the benzylic carbon, delocalising and <strong>stabilising the positive charge</strong> of the carbocation. <strong>TRUE.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II.</strong> The nitro group $-\text{NO}_2$ is a strong $-R$ (electron-withdrawing resonance) group. Through the para position it pulls electron density away from the benzylic carbon, delocalising and <strong>stabilising the negative charge</strong> of the carbanion. <strong>TRUE.</strong></p><p>Both statements correctly match the substituent’s electronic effect to the charge it stabilises.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

Shown below is the structure of methyl acetate with three different $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ carbon–oxygen bonds:

$$\text{H}_3\text{C}-\underset{\beta}{\overset{\overset{\textstyle O}{\|}\;\alpha}{\text{C}}}-\underset{\gamma}{\text{O}}-\text{CH}_3$$

The correct order of the bond lengths of these bonds is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Identify the three carbon–oxygen bonds:</p><ul><li>$\alpha$: the carbonyl $\text{C}=\text{O}$ double bond.</li><li>$\beta$: the single bond from the carbonyl carbon to the ester oxygen ($\text{C}-\text{O}$).</li><li>$\gamma$: the single bond from the ester oxygen to the methyl group ($\text{O}-\text{CH}_3$).</li></ul><p>The carbonyl $\text{C}=\text{O}$ ($\alpha$) is a genuine double bond, so it is the <strong>shortest</strong>.</p><p>The ester oxygen’s lone pair is delocalised into the carbonyl (resonance $\text{C}-\text{O}^+ = \text{C}$), giving the $\beta$ bond <strong>partial double-bond character</strong>, so it is shorter than a plain single bond.</p><p>The $\gamma$ bond ($\text{O}-\text{CH}_3$) is a normal single bond with the least double-bond character, so it is the <strong>longest</strong>.</p><p>Hence the increasing order of bond length is:</p>$$\alpha \;&lt;\; \beta \;&lt;\; \gamma$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

**Statement I:** On the basis of the inductive effect, the order of stability of alkyl carbanions is $CH_3^- > CH_3\text{-}CH_2^- > (CH_3)_2CH^- > (CH_3)_3C^-$.

**Statement II:** Allyl and benzyl carbanions are more stabilised by the inductive effect and not by the resonance effect.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I.</strong> A carbanion carries a negative charge, so it is <strong>destabilised</strong> by electron-donating groups. Alkyl groups are $+I$ (electron donating), and each added alkyl group intensifies the negative charge, making the carbanion less stable. Thus more alkyl substitution $\Rightarrow$ lower stability:</p>$$CH_3^- &gt; CH_3CH_2^- &gt; (CH_3)_2CH^- &gt; (CH_3)_3C^-$$<p>This order is <strong>correct</strong>.</p><p><strong>Statement II.</strong> Allyl and benzyl carbanions are primarily stabilised by <strong>resonance</strong> (delocalisation of the negative charge over the $\pi$-system / ring), not merely by an inductive effect. The statement claims the opposite, so it is <strong>incorrect</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>
