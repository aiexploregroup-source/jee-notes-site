PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

4.7 g of phenol is heated with Zn to give product X. If this reaction goes to 60% completion then the number of moles of compound X formed will be ________ $\times 10^{-2}$. (Nearest Integer) (Given molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$ : H:1, C:12, O:16)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Heating phenol with zinc dust reduces the C–OH bond, giving <strong>benzene</strong> as product X:</p>$$C_6H_5OH + Zn \longrightarrow C_6H_6 + ZnO$$<p><strong>Moles of phenol taken:</strong> molar mass of phenol $= 6(12)+6(1)+16 = 94\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$</p>$$n_{\text{phenol}} = \dfrac{4.7}{94} = 0.05\ \text{mol}$$<p>The stoichiometry is $1:1$, so at 100% completion $0.05$ mol of benzene would form. At <strong>60% completion</strong>:</p>$$n_X = 0.05 \times 0.60 = 0.03\ \text{mol} = 3 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 3</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

The descending order of acidity among the following compounds is: A. phenol; B. 4-nitrophenol; C. 4-methoxyphenol; D. 4-nitrobenzoic acid; E. benzoic acid.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Two effects decide the order:</p><ol><li><strong>Carboxylic acids are far stronger than phenols</strong> (a carboxylate spreads negative charge over two equivalent oxygens), so D and E sit at the top.</li><li><strong>Substituent effect:</strong> a $-NO_2$ group (electron-withdrawing) increases acidity, while $-OCH_3$ (electron-donating by resonance) decreases it.</li></ol><p>Approximate $pK_a$ values (lower $pK_a$ = stronger acid):</p><table><thead><tr><th>Compound</th><th>Species</th><th>$pK_a$</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>D</td><td>4-nitrobenzoic acid</td><td>$\approx 3.4$</td></tr><tr><td>E</td><td>benzoic acid</td><td>$\approx 4.2$</td></tr><tr><td>B</td><td>4-nitrophenol</td><td>$\approx 7.1$</td></tr><tr><td>A</td><td>phenol</td><td>$\approx 10.0$</td></tr><tr><td>C</td><td>4-methoxyphenol</td><td>$\approx 10.2$</td></tr></tbody></table><p>Descending acidity: $\mathbf{D &gt; E &gt; B &gt; A &gt; C}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

An organic compound ‘$x$’ where molar ratio of C, O and H are equal, on treatment with 50% KOH under reflux followed by acidification produced ‘$y$’. The most likely structure of ‘$y$’ is : [Molar mass of ‘$x$’ is 58 g mol$^{-1}$]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Since $C:O:H = 1:1:1$, the formula is $C_nH_nO_n$ with mass $29n$:</p>$$29n = 58 \;\Rightarrow\; n = 2 \;\Rightarrow\; C_2H_2O_2$$<p>This is <strong>glyoxal</strong>, $OHC\text{–}CHO$ — a dialdehyde with <strong>no $\alpha$-hydrogen</strong>.</p><p>With concentrated (50%) KOH, an aldehyde lacking $\alpha$-H undergoes the <strong>Cannizzaro reaction</strong>. Glyoxal undergoes an <em>intramolecular</em> Cannizzaro: one $-CHO$ is oxidised to $-COO^-$ while the other is reduced to $-CH_2OH$. After acidification:</p>$$OHC\text{–}CHO \xrightarrow[\text{then } H^+]{50\% \text{ KOH}} HOCH_2\text{–}COOH$$<p>Product $y$ is <strong>glycolic acid</strong> ($HOCH_2COOH$), matching option C.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

‘x’ is the product which is obtained by the hydrolysis of prop-1-yne in the presence of mercuric sulphate under dilute acidic medium at 333 K. ‘y’ is the product which is obtained by the reaction of ethane nitrile with methyl magnesium bromide in dry ether followed by hydrolysis. IUPAC name of product obtained from ‘x’ and ‘y’ in the presence of barium hydroxide followed by heating is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Find x:</strong> Markovnikov hydration of prop-1-yne ($CH_3\text{–}C\equiv CH$) with $HgSO_4/\text{dil. }H_2SO_4$ adds water at the internal carbon:</p>$$CH_3\text{–}C\equiv CH \xrightarrow{H_2O,\,HgSO_4} CH_3\text{–}\underset{O}{\overset{}{C}}\text{–}CH_3 \;=\; \textbf{acetone}$$<p><strong>Find y:</strong> A nitrile with a Grignard reagent gives a ketone after hydrolysis:</p>$$CH_3C\equiv N + CH_3MgBr \rightarrow CH_3\text{–}C(=NMgBr)\text{–}CH_3 \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3\text{–}CO\text{–}CH_3 \;=\; \textbf{acetone}$$<p>So both x and y are <strong>acetone</strong>. With $Ba(OH)_2$ (base), acetone undergoes <strong>aldol condensation</strong> with itself; heating dehydrates the aldol:</p>$$2\,CH_3COCH_3 \xrightarrow{Ba(OH)_2} (CH_3)_2C(OH)\text{–}CH_2\text{–}COCH_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta,\,-H_2O} (CH_3)_2C{=}CH\text{–}COCH_3$$<p>The product is <strong>mesityl oxide</strong>. Numbering to give the ketone the lowest locant:</p>$$\underset{1}{CH_3}\text{–}\underset{2}{CO}\text{–}\underset{3}{CH}{=}\underset{4}{C}(CH_3)\text{–}\underset{5}{CH_3}$$<p>giving <strong>4-methylpent-3-en-2-one</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 4 - Methylpent-3-en-2-one</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

An optically active alkyl bromide $C_4H_9Br$, reacts with ethanolic KOH to form major compound [A] which reacts with bromine to give compound [B]. Compound [B] reacts with ethanolic KOH and sodamide to give compound [C]. One molecule of water adds to compound [C] on warming with mercuric sulphate and dilute sulphuric acid at 333 K to form compound [D]. The functional group in compound D will be confirmed by:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Identify the starting bromide:</strong> an <em>optically active</em> $C_4H_9Br$ must have a stereocentre, so it is <strong>2-bromobutane</strong>, $CH_3\text{–}CHBr\text{–}CH_2\text{–}CH_3$.</p><p>Trace the sequence:</p><ul><li><strong>[A]</strong> — ethanolic KOH → dehydrohalogenation (Saytzeff) → <strong>but-2-ene</strong>, $CH_3CH{=}CHCH_3$.</li><li><strong>[B]</strong> — $Br_2$ addition → <strong>2,3-dibromobutane</strong>.</li><li><strong>[C]</strong> — ethanolic KOH then $NaNH_2$ → double dehydrohalogenation → <strong>but-2-yne</strong>, $CH_3\text{–}C\equiv C\text{–}CH_3$.</li><li><strong>[D]</strong> — $H_2O/HgSO_4/\text{dil. }H_2SO_4$ (333 K) hydrates the internal alkyne → <strong>butan-2-one</strong>, $CH_3\text{–}CO\text{–}CH_2\text{–}CH_3$.</li></ul><p>Butan-2-one is a <strong>methyl ketone</strong> ($CH_3CO\text{–}$ group), which gives a positive <strong>iodoform (haloform) test</strong>. Tollens’ (silver mirror) and Benedict’s are aldehyde tests; Lucas is an alcohol test — none apply here.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> Haloform test</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

Consider compounds A, B and C with following structural formulae
$A = CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-OH$
$B = CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3$
$C = HO-CH_2-CH_2-CH(OH)-CH_3$
For the conversion of B from A, reagent (D) required is ________ and structural formula of product (E) obtained when C undergoes same reaction using excess reagent (D) is ________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>A → B:</strong> pentan-1-ol loses a molecule of water to give a pentene. Removal of $H_2O$ from an alcohol is <strong>acid-catalysed dehydration</strong>, so reagent <strong>D = conc. $H_2SO_4$ (or $H_3PO_4$)</strong> — not an oxidant like PCC. This immediately eliminates the two PCC options.</p><p><strong>C → E (same reaction, excess D):</strong> $C$ is <strong>butane-1,3-diol</strong>, $HO\text{–}CH_2\text{–}CH_2\text{–}CH(OH)\text{–}CH_3$. With excess dehydrating acid, <strong>both</strong> $-OH$ groups are eliminated:</p>$$HO\text{–}CH_2\text{–}CH_2\text{–}CH(OH)\text{–}CH_3 \xrightarrow[-2\,H_2O]{\text{excess } H_2SO_4} CH_2{=}CH\text{–}CH{=}CH_2$$<p>giving the conjugated diene <strong>buta-1,3-diene</strong>. This matches option D.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D: Conc. $H_2SO_4$ or $H_3PO_4$; E: $CH_2=CH-CH=CH_2$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

One mole of phenol is treated with dilute $HNO_3$ at 298 K to give a mixture of products. The mixture is separated by steam distillation. The steam volatile compound (X) is separated. The increase in percentage of oxygen in (X) with respect to phenol is ________ $\times 10^{-1}$ %
(Given molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$ H:1, C:12, N:14, O:16)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Dilute $HNO_3$ nitrates phenol to a mixture of <strong>o-nitrophenol</strong> and <strong>p-nitrophenol</strong>. Only <em>ortho</em>-nitrophenol is <strong>steam volatile</strong> (intramolecular H-bonding lowers its boiling point; the para isomer forms intermolecular H-bonds and is non-volatile). So <strong>X = o-nitrophenol</strong>, $C_6H_4(OH)(NO_2)$.</p><p><strong>% oxygen in phenol</strong> ($C_6H_6O$, $M = 94$):</p>$$\dfrac{16}{94}\times 100 = 17.02\%$$<p><strong>% oxygen in o-nitrophenol</strong> ($C_6H_5NO_3$, $M = 72+5+14+48 = 139$; O mass $= 3\times16 = 48$):</p>$$\dfrac{48}{139}\times 100 = 34.53\%$$<p><strong>Increase:</strong></p>$$34.53 - 17.02 = 17.51\%$$<p>Expressed as $\times 10^{-1}\%$: $\;17.51 / 0.1 = 175.1 \approx \mathbf{175}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 175</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

Given below are two statements :

**Statement I:** The condensation reaction between $CH_3-CH=O$ and $H_2N-NH-C(=O)-NH_2$ under optimum pH will produce $CH_3-CH=N-NH-C(=O)-NH_2$.

**Statement II:** The molecule $Ph-CH(O-H)(O-CH_3)$ will generate $Ph-CH=O$ in the presence of dilute acid.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Semicarbazide ($H_2N\text{–}NH\text{–}CO\text{–}NH_2$) is a nitrogen nucleophile that condenses with a carbonyl group, losing water to form a <strong>semicarbazone</strong>:</p>$$CH_3CHO + H_2N\text{–}NH\text{–}CO\text{–}NH_2 \xrightarrow{\text{opt. pH}} CH_3CH{=}N\text{–}NH\text{–}CO\text{–}NH_2 + H_2O$$<p>This is exactly the product written. <strong>True.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> $Ph\text{–}CH(OH)(OCH_3)$ has both an $-OH$ and an $-OR$ on the same carbon — it is a <strong>hemiacetal</strong>. Hemiacetals are unstable and, in dilute acid, revert to the parent aldehyde and alcohol:</p>$$Ph\text{–}CH(OH)(OCH_3) \xrightarrow{H^+} Ph\text{–}CHO + CH_3OH$$<p>So it does generate benzaldehyde. <strong>True.</strong></p><p>Both statements are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> Both Statement I and Statement II are true</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

‘$x$’ is the product which is obtained from benzene by reacting it with carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of cuprous chloride. ‘$y$’ is the major product obtained from the benzene by reacting it with ethanoyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous $AlCl_3$. Product (major) obtained by heating $x$ and $y$ in the presence of alkali is $z$. Total number of $\pi$ (pi) electrons in $z$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Identify x:</strong> benzene $+ CO + HCl$ with $CuCl$ is the <strong>Gattermann–Koch</strong> reaction → <strong>benzaldehyde</strong>, $C_6H_5CHO$ (no $\alpha$-H).</p><p><strong>Identify y:</strong> benzene $+ CH_3COCl / \text{anhyd. }AlCl_3$ is <strong>Friedel–Crafts acylation</strong> → <strong>acetophenone</strong>, $C_6H_5COCH_3$ (has $\alpha$-H).</p><p><strong>Form z:</strong> heating with alkali drives a <strong>cross aldol condensation</strong> — the $\alpha$-carbon of acetophenone attacks benzaldehyde, then dehydration gives <strong>chalcone</strong>:</p>$$C_6H_5CHO + C_6H_5COCH_3 \xrightarrow{\text{OH}^-,\,\Delta} C_6H_5\text{–}CH{=}CH\text{–}CO\text{–}C_6H_5$$<p><strong>Count $\pi$ electrons in chalcone:</strong></p><table><thead><tr><th>Source</th><th>$\pi$ bonds</th><th>$\pi$ electrons</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Benzene ring 1</td><td>3</td><td>6</td></tr><tr><td>Benzene ring 2</td><td>3</td><td>6</td></tr><tr><td>$C=C$ (bridge)</td><td>1</td><td>2</td></tr><tr><td>$C=O$ (carbonyl)</td><td>1</td><td>2</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Total</strong></td><td><strong>8</strong></td><td><strong>16</strong></td></tr></tbody></table><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 16</p></div></details>
