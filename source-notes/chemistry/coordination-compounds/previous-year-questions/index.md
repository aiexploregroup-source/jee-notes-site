**List-II (Calculated spin only magnetic moment (BM)):** I. 3.87; II. 5.92; III. 4.90; IV. 1.73

Match the LIST-I with LIST-II.

**LIST-I (Electronic configuration of tetrahedral metal ion):** A. $d^2$; B. $d^4$; C. $d^6$; D. $d^8$

**LIST-II (Crystal Field Stabilization Energy $\Delta_t$):** I. $-0.6$; II. $-0.8$; III. $-1.2$; IV. $-0.4$

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>In a tetrahedral field the $e$ orbitals lie lower ($-0.6\,\Delta_t$ each) and the $t_2$ orbitals higher ($+0.4\,\Delta_t$ each). Tetrahedral complexes are high spin, and the $e$ set (2 orbitals) fills first, then $t_2$ (3 orbitals).</p>$$\text{A: } d^2 = e^2 \Rightarrow 2(-0.6) = -1.2\,\Delta_t \Rightarrow \text{III}$$

$$\text{B: } d^4 = e^2 t_2^2 \Rightarrow 2(-0.6)+2(+0.4) = -0.4\,\Delta_t \Rightarrow \text{IV}$$

$$\text{C: } d^6 = e^3 t_2^3 \Rightarrow 3(-0.6)+3(+0.4) = -0.6\,\Delta_t \Rightarrow \text{I}$$

$$\text{D: } d^8 = e^4 t_2^4 \Rightarrow 4(-0.6)+4(+0.4) = -0.8\,\Delta_t \Rightarrow \text{II}$$<p>So A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C (A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

Which of the following are true about the energy of the given d-orbitals of a tetrahedral complex?

A. $d_{xy} = d_{xz} > d_{x^2-y^2}$; B. $d_{xy} = d_{yz} > d_{z^2}$; C. $d_{x^2-y^2} > d_{z^2} > d_{xz}$; D. $d_{x^2-y^2} = d_{z^2} < d_{xz}$

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>In a tetrahedral field the splitting is inverted relative to octahedral: the $t_2$ set $(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz})$ is <strong>higher</strong> in energy, while the $e$ set $(d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2})$ is <strong>lower</strong>. Within each set the orbitals are degenerate.</p>$$e\text{-set: } d_{x^2-y^2} = d_{z^2} \quad (\text{lower}), \qquad t_2\text{-set: } d_{xy} = d_{yz} = d_{xz} \quad (\text{higher})$$<ul><li><strong>A.</strong> $d_{xy} = d_{xz}\ (t_2) &gt; d_{x^2-y^2}\ (e)$ — TRUE.</li><li><strong>B.</strong> $d_{xy} = d_{yz}\ (t_2) &gt; d_{z^2}\ (e)$ — TRUE.</li><li><strong>C.</strong> claims $d_{x^2-y^2} &gt; d_{z^2}$, but these are degenerate ($=$), so FALSE.</li><li><strong>D.</strong> $d_{x^2-y^2} = d_{z^2}\ (e) &lt; d_{xz}\ (t_2)$ — TRUE.</li></ul><p>So A, B and D are correct.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (A, B and D only)</p></div></details>

5.33 g of CrCl$_3\cdot$6H$_2$O, which is a 1 : 3 electrolyte, is dissolved in water and is passed through a cation exchanger. The chloride ions in the eluted solution, on treatment with AgNO$_3$, result in 8.61 g of AgCl. The ratio of moles of complex reacted and moles of AgCl formed is __________ $\times 10^{-2}$ (Nearest integer).

[Molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$: Cr = 52, Ag = 108, Cl = 35.5, H = 1, O = 16]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>A 1 : 3 electrolyte means all three chlorides are ionizable: the complex is $[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3$.</p><p>Molar mass of CrCl$_3\cdot$6H$_2$O:</p>$$M = 52 + 3(35.5) + 6(18) = 52 + 106.5 + 108 = 266.5\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$$<p>Moles of complex:</p>$$n_{\text{complex}} = \frac{5.33}{266.5} = 0.02\ \text{mol}$$<p>Passing through a cation exchanger retains the cation $[Cr(H_2O)_6]^{3+}$; the eluate carries the $3\,Cl^-$ (as HCl). These precipitate as AgCl ($M = 108 + 35.5 = 143.5$):</p>$$n_{\text{AgCl}} = \frac{8.61}{143.5} = 0.06\ \text{mol}$$<p>Required ratio:</p>$$\frac{n_{\text{complex}}}{n_{\text{AgCl}}} = \frac{0.02}{0.06} = 0.333 = 33.3 \times 10^{-2}$$<p>Nearest integer $= 33$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 33</p></div></details>

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>In all three, nickel is $\text{Ni}^{2+} = d^8$.</p><ul><li><strong>A</strong> $[Ni(en)_3]^{2+}$: octahedral $d^8$ gives $t_{2g}^6 e_g^2$ regardless of field strength $\Rightarrow$ <strong>2</strong> unpaired.</li><li><strong>B</strong> $[NiCl_4]^{2-}$: tetrahedral $d^8$ gives $e^4 t_2^4 \Rightarrow$ <strong>2</strong> unpaired.</li><li><strong>C</strong> $[Ni(NH_3)_6]^{2+}$: octahedral $d^8 \Rightarrow$ <strong>2</strong> unpaired.</li></ul><p>So the count is 2, 2, 2.</p><p>Frequency of absorption $\propto \Delta$. Octahedral $\Delta_o &gt; \Delta_t$, and field strength follows $en &gt; NH_3 &gt; Cl^-$:</p>$$\Delta:\quad [Ni(en)_3]^{2+} &gt; [Ni(NH_3)_6]^{2+} &gt; [NiCl_4]^{2-}$$<p>Hence frequency order $(A) &gt; (C) &gt; (B)$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (2, 2, 2 and (A) &gt; (C) &gt; (B))</p></div></details>

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Coordination isomerism arises when <strong>both</strong> the cation and the anion are complex ions and the ligands can be interchanged between the two <strong>different</strong> metal centres to give a genuinely different compound.</p><ul><li><strong>A.</strong> $[Ag(NH_3)_2][Ag(CN)_2]$: same metal (Ag/Ag) $\Rightarrow$ interchange gives an identical compound $\Rightarrow$ NO.</li><li><strong>B.</strong> $[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]$: Co and Cr differ $\Rightarrow$ isomer $[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$ $\Rightarrow$ YES.</li><li><strong>C.</strong> $[Co(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$: same metal in the same oxidation state (Co(III)/Co(III)) $\Rightarrow$ interchange gives the same compound $\Rightarrow$ NO.</li><li><strong>D.</strong> $[Fe(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$: Fe and Co differ $\Rightarrow$ YES.</li><li><strong>E.</strong> $[Co(NH_3)_6][Fe(CN)_6]$: Co and Fe differ $\Rightarrow$ YES.</li></ul><p>So B, D and E only.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B (B, D and E Only)</p></div></details>
