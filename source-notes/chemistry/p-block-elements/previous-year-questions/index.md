**Statement I:** The covalency of oxygen is generally two but it can exceed up to four. The oxidation state of oxygen in $\text{SO}_2$ is $-2$ and in $\text{OF}_2$ it is $+2$.

**Statement II:** The anomalous behaviour of oxygen when compared to the other elements of group 16 is due to its small size and high electronegativity.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Oxygen usually shows a covalency of two, but it can extend up to four (e.g. in $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ and coordinate-bonded species). Checking the oxidation states:</p><ul><li>In $\text{SO}_2$: oxygen is more electronegative than sulphur, so each O is $-2$ (and S is $+4$). **Statement I:** Aluminium is more electropositive than thallium as the standard electrode potential value of $E^\circ_{\text{Al}^{3+}/\text{Al}}$ is negative and $E^\circ_{\text{Tl}^{3+}/\text{Tl}}$ is positive.

**Statement II:** The sum of first three ionization enthalpies of boron is very high when compared to that of aluminium. Due to this reason boron forms covalent compounds only and aluminium forms $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ion.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> A more negative reduction potential means a more electropositive (more easily oxidised) metal. Here $E^\circ_{\text{Al}^{3+}/\text{Al}} = -1.66\ \text{V}$ (negative) while $E^\circ_{\text{Tl}^{3+}/\text{Tl}}$ is positive. Hence aluminium is more electropositive than thallium. <strong>True.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> The sum of the first three ionization enthalpies of boron ($\Delta_i H_1 + \Delta_i H_2 + \Delta_i H_3$) is much larger than that of aluminium because of boron’s very small size. This large energy cannot be compensated by lattice/hydration energy, so boron cannot form $\text{B}^{3+}$ and bonds covalently, whereas aluminium readily forms $\text{Al}^{3+}$. This is the standard NCERT reasoning. <strong>True.</strong></p><p>Both statements are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

**Statement (I):** Oxidising power of halogens decreases in the order $F_2 > Cl_2 > Br_2 > I_2$, which is the basis of the “Layer test”.

**Statement (II):** The “Layer test” to identify $Br_2$ and $I_2$ in aqueous solution involves the oxidation of bromide or iodide into $Br_2$ or $I_2$ respectively with $Cl_2$, which is a type of displacement redox reaction.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> The oxidising power (and standard reduction potential) of the halogens decreases down the group: $F_2 &gt; Cl_2 &gt; Br_2 &gt; I_2$. Because $Cl_2$ is a stronger oxidant than $Br_2$ and $I_2$, it can displace them from their salts — the principle used in the layer test. <strong>True.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> In the layer test, chlorine water is added to the solution containing bromide/iodide:</p>$$Cl_2 + 2Br^- \rightarrow 2Cl^- + Br_2 \qquad Cl_2 + 2I^- \rightarrow 2Cl^- + I_2$$<p>The liberated $Br_2$ (orange) or $I_2$ (violet) dissolves in the added organic layer (e.g. $CS_2/CCl_4$), giving a characteristic coloured layer. This is a displacement redox reaction (Cl is reduced, $Br^-/I^-$ is oxidised). <strong>True.</strong></p><p>Both statements are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

The electronegativity of a group 13 element $E$ is the same as that of Ge (on the Pauling scale and up to one decimal point). The CORRECT statements about $E^{3+}$ are:

A. It can act as a reducing agent.
B. It can act as an oxidizing agent.
C. $E^{3+}$ is more stable than $E^+$.
D. The standard electrode potential value for $E^{3+}/E$ is positive.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Germanium has a Pauling electronegativity of $2.0$ (to one decimal). Among the group 13 elements, <strong>thallium</strong> matches this ($\text{EN}_{\text{Tl}} \approx 2.0$), so $E = \text{Tl}$.</p><p>For thallium the <strong>inert-pair effect</strong> is strongest in the group: the $+1$ state is more stable than the $+3$ state, i.e. $\text{Tl}^+ $ is more stable than $\text{Tl}^{3+}$. Consequences for $E^{3+} = \text{Tl}^{3+}$:</p><ul><li>$\text{Tl}^{3+}$ tends to gain electrons and revert to the more stable $\text{Tl}^+$, so it is an <strong>oxidizing agent</strong> — statement <strong>B is correct</strong>; it is not a reducing agent, so <strong>A is wrong</strong>.</li><li>Since $E^+$ is the more stable state, $E^{3+}$ is <strong>not</strong> more stable than $E^+$ — <strong>C is wrong</strong>.</li><li>The strong oxidising tendency of $\text{Tl}^{3+}$ gives a <strong>positive</strong> $E^\circ_{E^{3+}/E}$ — <strong>D is correct</strong>.</li></ul><p>Correct statements: <strong>B and D</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

**Statement I:** $F_2O < H_2O < Cl_2O$ is the correct trend in terms of bond angle.

**Statement II:** $SiF_4$, $SnF_4$ and $PbF_4$ are ionic in nature.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Comparing the central-oxygen bond angles:</p>$$F_2O\ (\approx 103^\circ) &lt; H_2O\ (104.5^\circ) &lt; Cl_2O\ (\approx 111^\circ)$$<p>Fluorine is highly electronegative and pulls the bonding pairs away from O, reducing repulsion between bond pairs and compressing the angle; chlorine is larger and less electronegative, so bond pairs stay near O and, with added bp–bp / steric repulsion of the large Cl atoms, the angle opens up. The stated trend is <strong>true</strong>.</p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> $SiF_4$ is a covalent, molecular (gaseous) compound, not ionic. While the heavier $SnF_4$ and $PbF_4$ have considerable ionic character, grouping all three as ionic is incorrect. <strong>False.</strong></p><p>Statement I is true, Statement II is false.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

Treatment of a gas $X$ with a freshly prepared ferrous sulphate solution gives a compound $Y$ as a brown ring. The compounds $X$ and $Y$ are:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>This is the classic <strong>brown-ring test</strong> for nitrate/nitrite. The nitrate is first reduced by $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ in the presence of concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ to nitric oxide:</p>$$3\text{Fe}^{2+} + 4\text{H}^+ + \text{NO}_3^- \rightarrow 3\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{NO} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$<p>The liberated gas $X = \text{NO}$ then combines with fresh $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ (as $\text{FeSO}_4$) to form the brown nitrosyl-iron complex at the interface:</p>$$[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+} + \text{NO} \rightarrow [\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_5(\text{NO})]^{2+} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$<p>Written as the sulphate, $Y = [\text{Fe(NO)}]\text{SO}_4$, the brown ring.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

**Statement I:** Aluminium upon reaction with NaOH forms the $[Al(OH)_6]^{3-}$ ion.

**Statement II:** The geometry of $ICl_4^-$, $ClO_3^-$ and $IBr_2^-$ is square planar, pyramidal and linear respectively.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Aluminium dissolves in aqueous NaOH to give the <strong>tetrahydroxoaluminate(III)</strong> ion, not a hexahydroxo species:</p>$$2Al + 2NaOH + 6H_2O \rightarrow 2Na[Al(OH)_4] + 3H_2\uparrow$$<p>The product is $[Al(OH)_4]^-$ (tetrahedral), so the statement claiming $[Al(OH)_6]^{3-}$ is <strong>false</strong>.</p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> Apply VSEPR:</p><ul><li>$ICl_4^-$: I has 4 bond pairs + 2 lone pairs ($sp^3d^2$) $\Rightarrow$ <strong>square planar</strong>. Correct.</li><li>$ClO_3^-$: Cl has 3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair ($sp^3$) $\Rightarrow$ <strong>pyramidal</strong>. Correct.</li><li>$IBr_2^-$: I has 2 bond pairs + 3 lone pairs ($sp^3d$) $\Rightarrow$ <strong>linear</strong>. Correct.</li></ul><p>Statement II is <strong>true</strong>.</p><p>Statement I is false, Statement II is true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Find the correct statements related to group 15 hydrides:

A. Reducing nature increases from $NH_3$ to $BiH_3$.
B. Tendency to donate lone pair of electrons decreases from $NH_3$ to $BiH_3$.
C. The stability of hydrides decreases from $NH_3$ to $BiH_3$.
D. H–E–H bond angle decreases from $NH_3$ to $SbH_3$ (E = elements of group 15).

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Down group 15 the E–H bond becomes weaker (E gets larger, bond enthalpy falls):</p><ul><li><strong>A.</strong> As E–H bonds weaken, the hydrides more readily give up hydrogen, so <strong>reducing nature increases</strong> $NH_3 \rightarrow BiH_3$. <strong>Correct.</strong></li><li><strong>B.</strong> The lone pair becomes more diffuse and less available down the group, so the <strong>electron-donating (basic) tendency decreases</strong> $NH_3 \rightarrow BiH_3$ ($NH_3$ is the strongest donor). <strong>Correct.</strong></li><li><strong>C.</strong> With decreasing bond dissociation enthalpy, <strong>thermal stability decreases</strong> $NH_3 \rightarrow BiH_3$. <strong>Correct.</strong></li><li><strong>D.</strong> Bond angles fall as EN of the central atom drops (less bp–bp repulsion): $NH_3\,(107^\circ) &gt; PH_3\,(93.6^\circ) &gt; AsH_3\,(91.8^\circ) &gt; SbH_3\,(91.3^\circ)$, so the angle <strong>decreases</strong> $NH_3 \rightarrow SbH_3$. <strong>Correct.</strong></li></ul><p>All four statements A, B, C and D are correct.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>
