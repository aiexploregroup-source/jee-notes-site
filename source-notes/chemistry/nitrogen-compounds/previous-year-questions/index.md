PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

Product C of the following reaction sequence will be:

$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{water}} A \xrightarrow{\text{NaNO}_2/\text{HCl},\,273\text{-}278\,\text{K}} B \xrightarrow{\text{(i) HBF}_4\ \text{(ii) NaNO}_2,\,\text{Cu},\,\Delta} C$$

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — Bromination (A):</strong> Aniline is strongly activating, and with $\text{Br}_2$ in water (no catalyst) all three positions ortho/para to $-\text{NH}_2$ are brominated. The major product is <strong>2,4,6-tribromoaniline</strong>.</p><p><strong>Step 2 — Diazotization (B):</strong> $\text{NaNO}_2/\text{HCl}$ at $273\text{-}278\,\text{K}$ converts $-\text{NH}_2$ into the diazonium group, giving <strong>2,4,6-tribromobenzenediazonium chloride</strong> ($\text{Ar–N}_2^{+}$).</p><p><strong>Step 3 — Replacement of $-\text{N}_2^{+}$ by $-\text{NO}_2$ (C):</strong> Treatment with $\text{HBF}_4$ forms the diazonium fluoroborate; subsequent $\text{NaNO}_2/\text{Cu},\Delta$ replaces the diazonium group by a nitro group (nitro-de-diazoniation). The $-\text{NO}_2$ takes the position formerly held by $-\text{NH}_2$ (position 1), with the three bromines at 2, 4 and 6.</p><p>Numbering from the nitro group, the bromines sit at 3, 4-adjacent positions giving <strong>1,3,5-tribromo-2-nitrobenzene</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

Arrange the following compounds according to increasing order of boiling points:

$$n\text{-}C_4H_9OH\ (A),\quad n\text{-}C_4H_9NH_2\ (B),\quad n\text{-}C_4H_{10}\ (C),\quad C_2H_5NHC_2H_5\ (D)$$

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Boiling point is governed by the strength of intermolecular forces (mainly hydrogen bonding here), for molecules of comparable molar mass:</p><ul><li><strong>C — $n\text{-}C_4H_{10}$ (butane):</strong> only weak London dispersion forces, no H-bonding $\Rightarrow$ lowest bp ($\approx 0\,^\circ\text{C}$).</li><li><strong>D — $C_2H_5NHC_2H_5$ (diethylamine):</strong> a secondary amine with one $\text{N–H}$, so weak $\text{N}\cdots\text{H–N}$ hydrogen bonding ($\approx 56\,^\circ\text{C}$).</li><li><strong>B — $n\text{-}C_4H_9NH_2$ (butan-1-amine):</strong> a primary amine with two $\text{N–H}$ bonds, giving stronger H-bonding than a secondary amine ($\approx 78\,^\circ\text{C}$).</li><li><strong>A — $n\text{-}C_4H_9OH$ (butan-1-ol):</strong> $\text{O–H}$ is more polar than $\text{N–H}$, so alcohols H-bond most strongly $\Rightarrow$ highest bp ($\approx 118\,^\circ\text{C}$).</li></ul><p>Increasing order: $C &lt; D &lt; B &lt; A$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

The strongest conjugate acid will result from (protonation of the amine gives the conjugate acid; the strongest conjugate acid corresponds to the weakest base):

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For a base $B$ and its conjugate acid $BH^{+}$:</p>$$K_a(BH^{+}) \times K_b(B) = K_w \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{weaker base} \Rightarrow \text{stronger conjugate acid}.$$<p>So we need the <strong>weakest base</strong> among the substituted anilines:</p><ul><li><strong>$-\text{OCH}_3$ (para):</strong> electron-donating (by resonance) $\Rightarrow$ stronger base.</li><li><strong>$-\text{CH}_3$ (para):</strong> weakly electron-donating $\Rightarrow$ slightly stronger base than aniline.</li><li><strong>$-\text{H}$ (aniline):</strong> reference.</li><li><strong>$-\text{NO}_2$ (para):</strong> strongly electron-withdrawing ($-M,\,-I$), it delocalises the lone pair on $\text{N}$ and destabilises $BH^{+}$ $\Rightarrow$ <strong>weakest base</strong>.</li></ul><p>Since 4-nitroaniline is the weakest base, its conjugate acid ($4\text{-}O_2N\text{-}C_6H_4\text{-}NH_3^{+}$) is the strongest.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

**Statement I:** Heating benzamide with bromine in an ethanolic solution of sodium hydroxide will give benzylamine.

**Statement II:** Nitration of aniline with $\text{HNO}_3/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $288\,\text{K}$ produces m-nitroaniline in higher amount than o-nitroaniline.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I — FALSE.</strong> Benzamide undergoing the Hofmann bromamide degradation loses one carbon (the carbonyl carbon leaves as carbonate):</p>$$C_6H_5CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2,\,NaOH} C_6H_5NH_2\ (\text{aniline}).$$<p>The product is <strong>aniline</strong>, not benzylamine. (Benzylamine, $C_6H_5CH_2NH_2$, would require the same number of carbons, which the degradation does not give.)</p><p><strong>Statement II — TRUE.</strong> In the strongly acidic nitrating mixture, aniline is protonated to the anilinium ion $C_6H_5NH_3^{+}$. The $-\overset{+}{N}H_3$ group is deactivating and <strong>meta-directing</strong>, so a substantial fraction of meta product forms. The observed distribution is roughly $\approx 47\%$ meta, $\approx 51\%$ para and only $\approx 2\%$ ortho. Hence m-nitroaniline is formed in higher amount than o-nitroaniline.</p><p>Statement I is false and Statement II is true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Which statements are True?

A. In Hofmann bromamide degradation, 4 moles of NaOH and 2 moles of $\text{Br}_2$ are consumed per mole of an amide.

B. Hofmann bromamide reaction is not given by alkyl amides.

C. Primary amines can be synthesized by Hofmann bromamide degradation.

D. Secondary amide on reaction with $\text{Br}_2$ and NaOH will give secondary amine.

E. The by-products of Hofmann degradation are $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$, NaBr and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

Choose the correct answer:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The balanced Hofmann bromamide equation is:</p>$$R\text{-}CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4\,NaOH \longrightarrow R\text{-}NH_2 + Na_2CO_3 + 2\,NaBr + 2\,H_2O$$<p>Evaluating each statement:</p><ul><li><strong>A — FALSE.</strong> Per mole of amide, $4\ \text{mol NaOH}$ but only $1\ \text{mol Br}_2$ is consumed (not 2).</li><li><strong>B — FALSE.</strong> Alkyl amides do undergo the reaction (e.g. $CH_3CONH_2 \to CH_3NH_2$). It is not restricted to aryl amides.</li><li><strong>C — TRUE.</strong> The reaction converts a primary amide $R\text{-}CONH_2$ into a <strong>primary amine</strong> $R\text{-}NH_2$ (with one fewer carbon).</li><li><strong>D — FALSE.</strong> The rearrangement needs a primary amide ($-CONH_2$). N-substituted (secondary) amides lack the required $\text{N–H}$/migration pathway to give the analogous amine.</li><li><strong>E — TRUE.</strong> The by-products are exactly $Na_2CO_3$, $NaBr$ and $H_2O$, as shown in the balanced equation.</li></ul><p>Only <strong>C and E</strong> are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>
