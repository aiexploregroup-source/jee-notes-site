PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

The correct statements among the following are,

A. Mo(VI) and W(VI) are less stable than Cr(VI).
B. $\text{Ce}^{4+}$ and $\text{Tb}^{4+}$ are oxidant while $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ and $\text{Yb}^{2+}$ are reductant.
C. Cm and Am have seven unpaired electrons.
D. Actinoid contraction is greater from element to element than lanthanoid contraction.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Check each statement.</p><p><strong>A.</strong> Going down a group, the higher oxidation state becomes <em>more</em> stable. So Mo(VI) and W(VI) are <strong>more</strong> stable than Cr(VI), not less. <strong>Incorrect.</strong></p><p><strong>B.</strong> $\text{Ce}^{4+}$ and $\text{Tb}^{4+}$ tend to revert to the stable $+3$ state, so they act as <strong>oxidants</strong>; $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ and $\text{Yb}^{2+}$ tend to go to $+3$, so they act as <strong>reductants</strong>. <strong>Correct.</strong></p><p><strong>C.</strong> Configurations:</p>$$\text{Am}: [\text{Rn}]\,5f^{7}7s^{2}\ \Rightarrow\ 7\ \text{unpaired}$$<p></p>$$\text{Cm}: [\text{Rn}]\,5f^{7}6d^{1}7s^{2}\ \Rightarrow\ 7+1 = 8\ \text{unpaired}$$<p>Cm has 8 unpaired electrons, so “both have seven” is <strong>incorrect.</strong></p><p><strong>D.</strong> Because 5f electrons shield the nuclear charge even more poorly than 4f electrons, the contraction per element in the actinoids is <strong>greater</strong> than in the lanthanoids. <strong>Correct.</strong></p><p>Correct statements: <strong>B and D</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

Correct statements from the following are

A. Potassium dichromate is an oxidising agent and it oxidises $\text{FeSO}_4$ to $\text{Fe}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$ in acidic medium.
B. Sodium dichromate can be used as primary standard in volumetric estimation.
C. $\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$ and $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ are interconvertible in aqueous solution by varying the pH of the solution.
D. Cr-O-Cr bond angle in $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ is $126^\circ$.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>A.</strong> In acidic medium $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ oxidises $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$:</p>$$\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + 6\,\text{Fe}^{2+} + 14\,\text{H}^{+} \rightarrow 2\,\text{Cr}^{3+} + 6\,\text{Fe}^{3+} + 7\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$$<p>So $\text{FeSO}_4$ is oxidised to $\text{Fe}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$. <strong>Correct.</strong></p><p><strong>B.</strong> Sodium dichromate is <strong>deliquescent/hygroscopic</strong>, so it cannot be a primary standard. The primary standard is <strong>potassium</strong> dichromate. <strong>Incorrect.</strong></p><p><strong>C.</strong> The equilibrium</p>$$2\,\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + 2\,\text{H}^{+} \rightleftharpoons \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$<p>shifts with pH (yellow chromate in base, orange dichromate in acid), so they are interconvertible. <strong>Correct.</strong></p><p><strong>D.</strong> In the $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ ion the two tetrahedra share a corner oxygen with a Cr–O–Cr bridge angle of about $126^\circ$. <strong>Correct.</strong></p><p>Correct statements: <strong>A, C and D</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

Among $Fe^{2+}$, $Fe^{3+}$, $Cr^{2+}$ and $Zn^{2+}$, the ion that shows positive borax bead test and with highest ionisation enthalpy is :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The borax bead test gives a <strong>coloured</strong> bead only for ions with unpaired $d$-electrons.</p>$$\text{Zn}^{2+}: 3d^{10}\ (\text{colourless, no bead colour})$$<p></p>$$\text{Fe}^{2+}: 3d^{6},\quad \text{Fe}^{3+}: 3d^{5},\quad \text{Cr}^{2+}: 3d^{4}\ (\text{all coloured, positive test})$$<p>So $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ is eliminated. Among the remaining coloured ions we need the <strong>highest ionisation enthalpy</strong> — i.e. the ion formed by removing the most electrons / from the most positive species. Forming $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ (removal of the third electron) requires much more energy than forming the divalent ions $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ or $\text{Cr}^{2+}$.</p><p>Positive bead test + highest ionisation enthalpy → $\text{Fe}^{3+}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

The correct order of first ($\Delta_iH_1$) and second ($\Delta_iH_2$) ionisation enthalpy values of Cr and Mn are:
A. $\Delta_iH_1$: Cr > Mn
B. $\Delta_iH_2$: Cr > Mn
C. $\Delta_iH_1$: Mn > Cr
D. $\Delta_iH_2$: Mn > Cr
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Configurations: $\text{Cr} = [\text{Ar}]3d^{5}4s^{1}$, $\text{Mn} = [\text{Ar}]3d^{5}4s^{2}$.</p><p><strong>First ionisation ($\Delta_iH_1$):</strong> one $4s$ electron is removed from each. Mn’s $4s^2$ pair experiences a higher effective nuclear charge, so</p>$$\Delta_iH_1:\ \text{Mn} &gt; \text{Cr}\quad(717 &gt; 653\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1})$$<p>So statement <strong>C</strong> is correct (and A is wrong).</p><p><strong>Second ionisation ($\Delta_iH_2$):</strong></p>$$\text{Cr}^{+} = 3d^{5}\ (\text{stable half-filled — hard to remove}),\qquad \text{Mn}^{+} = 3d^{5}4s^{1}\ (\text{remove the loose }4s)$$<p>Removing an electron from the extra-stable $3d^5$ of $\text{Cr}^{+}$ needs more energy:</p>$$\Delta_iH_2:\ \text{Cr} &gt; \text{Mn}\quad(1592 &gt; 1509\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1})$$<p>So statement <strong>B</strong> is correct (and D is wrong).</p><p>Correct: <strong>B and C</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

Which of the following is NOT a physical or chemical characteristics of interstitial compounds?

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Interstitial compounds form when small atoms (H, C, N, B) occupy the interstitial holes of a transition-metal lattice. Their characteristic properties are:</p><ul><li>high melting points, often <strong>higher than the pure metal</strong> — a true property;</li><li>they <strong>retain metallic conductivity</strong> — a true property;</li><li>they are <strong>chemically inert</strong> and are usually <strong>non-stoichiometric</strong> — a true property;</li><li>they are <strong>very hard and rigid</strong> (not soft) and are essentially <strong>metallic/covalent, not ionic</strong>.</li></ul><p>Hence the statement “very soft and ionic in nature” is the one that is NOT correct.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

Given below are two statements :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I.</strong> Transition-metal catalysts use <strong>both their 3d and 4s electrons</strong> (their ability to adopt variable oxidation states and provide electrons) to form bonds with adsorbed reactants.
