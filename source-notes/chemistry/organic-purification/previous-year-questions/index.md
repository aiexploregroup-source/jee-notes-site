PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

2.0 g of a bromo hydrocarbon (X) was subjected to Carius analysis, gave 3.36 g of AgBr. The percentage of carbon in the compound (X) is 26.7%. Total number of carbon atoms in the empirical formula for compound (X) is _____.
(Given molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$ H:1, C:12, Br:80, Ag:108)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — mass and % of bromine from AgBr.</strong> Molar mass of $\text{AgBr} = 108 + 80 = 188$.</p>$$m_{\text{Br}} = 3.36 \times \frac{80}{188} = 1.430\ \text{g}$$<p></p>$$\%\,\text{Br} = \frac{1.430}{2.0}\times 100 = 71.5\%$$<p><strong>Step 2 — % of hydrogen by difference.</strong></p>$$\%\,\text{H} = 100 - \%\,\text{C} - \%\,\text{Br} = 100 - 26.7 - 71.5 = 1.8\%$$<p><strong>Step 3 — mole ratio.</strong></p>$$\text{C}:\text{H}:\text{Br} = \frac{26.7}{12} : \frac{1.8}{1} : \frac{71.5}{80} = 2.225 : 1.80 : 0.894$$<p>Divide by the smallest (0.894):</p>$$= 2.49 : 2.01 : 1.00 \;\xrightarrow{\times 2}\; 5 : 4 : 2$$<p><strong>Step 4 — empirical formula.</strong> $\text{C}_5\text{H}_4\text{Br}_2$ (M = 224). Check: $\%\text{C}=\tfrac{60}{224}\times100=26.8\%$, $\%\text{Br}=\tfrac{160}{224}\times100=71.4\%$ — matches the data.</p><p>Total carbon atoms in the empirical formula $= 5$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 5</p></div></details>

In sulphur estimation, $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ mol of an organic compound (X) (molar mass 76 g mol$^{-1}$) gave 0.4813 g of barium sulphate (molar mass 233 g mol$^{-1}$). The percentage of sulphur in the compound (X) is __________ $\times 10^{-1}$ % (Nearest integer)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — mass of compound taken.</strong></p>$$m_X = 2.0\times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \times 76\ \text{g mol}^{-1} = 0.152\ \text{g}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — sulphur in the BaSO$_4$ formed.</strong> Each mole of $\text{BaSO}_4$ contains 1 mole of S.</p>$$m_{\text{S}} = 0.4813 \times \frac{32}{233} = 0.06610\ \text{g}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — percentage of sulphur.</strong></p>$$\%\,\text{S} = \frac{0.06610}{0.152}\times 100 = 43.49\%$$<p><strong>Step 4 — express as $\times 10^{-1}\%$.</strong></p>$$43.49\% = 434.9 \times 10^{-1}\% \approx 435 \times 10^{-1}\%$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 435</p></div></details>

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — mass of carbon from $CO_2$.</strong></p>$$m_{\text{C}} = 0.25 \times \frac{12}{44} = 0.06818\ \text{g}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — relate to % carbon.</strong> Carbon is 25% of the sample mass $X$:</p>$$0.25 \, X = 0.06818 \implies X = \frac{0.06818}{0.25} = 0.2727\ \text{g}$$<p><strong>Cross-check with hydrogen.</strong> $m_{\text{H}} = 0.12 \times \tfrac{2}{18} = 0.01333\ \text{g}$; $X = \tfrac{0.01333}{0.0489} = 0.2727\ \text{g}$ — consistent.</p><p><strong>Step 3 — express as $\times 10^{-3}$ g.</strong></p>$$X = 0.2727\ \text{g} = 272.7\times 10^{-3}\ \text{g} \approx 273\times 10^{-3}\ \text{g}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 273</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

Consider the following reaction sequence starting from **para-methylnitrobenzene** (a benzene ring with $\text{CH}_3$ and $\text{NO}_2$ in para positions):

(i) Sn/HCl; $\text{OH}^-$   (ii) $(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$   (iii) $\text{Br}_2/\text{AlBr}_3$   (iv) $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$

gives Major Product (P). When the product (P) is subjected to Carius analysis using $\text{AgNO}_3$, 1.0 g of the product (P) will produce __________ g of the precipitate of AgBr. (Nearest Integer) (Given: molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$ C: 12, H: 1, O: 16, N: 14, Br: 80, Ag: 108)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Track the transformations:</strong></p><ul><li><strong>(i) Sn/HCl then $\text{OH}^-$:</strong> reduces $-\text{NO}_2$ to $-\text{NH}_2$ → <em>p</em>-toluidine (4-methylaniline).</li><li><strong>(ii) $(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$:</strong> acetylates the amine to the acetamide $-\text{NHCOCH}_3$ (protecting/moderating the strong activator so bromination is monosubstituted). Now the ring carries $-\text{NHCOCH}_3$ and, para to it, $-\text{CH}_3$.</li><li><strong>(iii) $\text{Br}_2/\text{AlBr}_3$:</strong> the amide nitrogen is the dominant $o,p$-director. <details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — sulphur contained in the BaSO$_4$.</strong> One mole of $\text{BaSO}_4$ carries one mole of S.</p>$$m_{\text{S}} = 0.6 \times \frac{32}{231} = 0.0831\ \text{g}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — percentage of sulphur in the sample.</strong></p>$$\%\,\text{S} = \frac{0.0831}{0.2}\times 100 = 41.56\% \approx 42\%$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 42</p></div></details>

Match **List-I** with **List-II**.

**List-I (Purification technique):** A. Simple distillation; B. Fractional distillation; C. Steam distillation; D. Distillation under reduced pressure

**List-II (Used to separate):** I. Steam volatile compound; II. Two liquids with large difference in boiling points; III. Liquid decomposing at its boiling point; IV. Two liquids with close boiling points

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Match each technique to the situation it is designed for:</p><ul><li><strong>A. Simple distillation</strong> — works when the components have a <strong>large difference in boiling points</strong> → <strong>II</strong>.</li><li><strong>B. Fractional distillation</strong> — needed when two liquids have <strong>close boiling points</strong> → <strong>IV</strong>.</li><li><strong>C.
