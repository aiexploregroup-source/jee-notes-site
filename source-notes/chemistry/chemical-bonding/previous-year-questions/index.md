PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

According to Lewis theory, the total number of $\sigma$ bond-pairs and lone pair of electrons around the central atom of $\text{XeO}_6^{4-}$ ion is _____.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Total valence electrons:</p>$$\underbrace{8}_{\text{Xe}} + \underbrace{6\times 6}_{6\,\text{O}} + \underbrace{4}_{\text{charge}} = 48\ e^-$$<p>In $\text{XeO}_6^{4-}$ the central Xe is bonded to <strong>six</strong> oxygen atoms in an octahedral arrangement. Each Xe–O linkage contributes one $\sigma$ bond, so there are $6$ $\sigma$ bond-pairs around Xe.</p><p>The steric number of Xe is $6$ (six $\sigma$ bonds, no lone pair), giving a regular octahedral geometry, so Xe carries <strong>zero</strong> lone pairs.</p>$$\sigma\text{ bond-pairs} + \text{lone pairs} = 6 + 0 = 6$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 6</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

The pairs among $A = [\text{SO}_3^{2-}, \text{CO}_3^{2-}]$, $B = [\text{O}_2^{2-}, \text{F}_2]$, $C = [\text{CN}^-, \text{CO}]$, $D = [\text{NH}_3, \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$ and $E = [\text{MnO}_4^{2-}, \text{CrO}_4^{2-}]$ that do not have similar Lewis dot structure are:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Two species have a “similar” Lewis dot structure only when they are <strong>isoelectronic</strong> (same number of valence electrons) and therefore share the same bonding pattern, lone-pair count, and geometry. Count the valence electrons for each pair:</p><ul><li><strong>A</strong> $[\text{SO}_3^{2-}, \text{CO}_3^{2-}]$: $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ has $4+3(6)+2 = 24$ valence electrons — trigonal planar ($sp^2$), central C carries <strong>no lone pair</strong>. $\text{SO}_3^{2-}$ has $6+3(6)+2 = 26$ valence electrons — pyramidal ($sp^3$), central S carries <strong>one lone pair</strong>. Different electron count, geometry and lone-pair count — <strong>NOT similar.</strong></li><li><strong>B</strong> $[\text{O}_2^{2-}, \text{F}_2]$: both have $14$ valence electrons — one single bond and three lone pairs on each atom. Isoelectronic — <strong>similar.</strong></li><li><strong>C</strong> $[\text{CN}^-, \text{CO}]$: both have $10$ valence electrons — a triple bond and one lone pair on each atom. Isoelectronic — <strong>similar.</strong></li><li><strong>D</strong> $[\text{NH}_3, \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$: both have $8$ valence electrons — pyramidal with one lone pair on the central atom. Isoelectronic — <strong>similar.</strong></li><li><strong>E</strong> $[\text{MnO}_4^{2-}, \text{CrO}_4^{2-}]$: both are tetrahedral $+6$ oxoanions, but they are <strong>not isoelectronic</strong> — $\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$ has a $d^0$ centre while $\text{MnO}_4^{2-}$ has one extra electron ($d^1$). The electron distributions of the two Lewis structures therefore differ — <strong>NOT similar.</strong></li></ul><p>Only pairs <strong>A</strong> and <strong>E</strong> fail to share a similar Lewis dot structure.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B (A and E)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

Given below are two statements:
Statement (I): The correct sequence of bond lengths in the following species is $O_2^+ < O_2 < O_2^- < O_2^{2-}$.
Statement (II): The correct sequence of number of unpaired electrons in the following species is $O_2 > O_2^+ > O_2^- > O_2^{2-}$.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Using molecular orbital theory, the bond order is:</p>$$O_2^+ : 2.5,\quad O_2 : 2.0,\quad O_2^- : 1.5,\quad O_2^{2-} : 1.0$$<p>Higher bond order $\Rightarrow$ shorter bond, so bond length increases as bond order falls:</p>$$O_2^+ &lt; O_2 &lt; O_2^- &lt; O_2^{2-}\quad\checkmark\ (\textbf{Statement I is true})$$<p>Number of unpaired electrons (from the $\pi^*$ occupation):</p>$$O_2 : 2,\quad O_2^+ : 1,\quad O_2^- : 1,\quad O_2^{2-} : 0$$<p>Statement II claims $O_2 &gt; O_2^+ &gt; O_2^- &gt; O_2^{2-}$, i.e. $2 &gt; 1 &gt; 1 &gt; 0$. Since $O_2^+$ and $O_2^-$ both have $1$ unpaired electron, the strict inequality $O_2^+ &gt; O_2^-$ is wrong.</p>$$\Rightarrow \textbf{Statement II is false}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

Consider the following molecules/species: (x) cycloheptatrienone (tropone) — a seven-membered carbon ring with conjugated double bonds bearing a $=O$ group; (y) acetone $(\mathrm{CH_3})(\mathrm{CH_3})\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{O}$; (z) acetate ion $\mathrm{CH_3-C(=O)-O^{(-)}}$. The correct order of carbon–oxygen double bond length is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Longer bond $\Leftrightarrow$ more single-bond (lower bond-order) character in the C–O linkage.</p><ul><li><strong>(y) acetone:</strong> an essentially pure, localized $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ double bond (no lengthening resonance). <strong>Shortest.</strong></li><li><strong>(z) acetate:</strong> the two C–O bonds are equivalent by resonance with bond order $1.5$, so the bond is longer than a pure double bond. <strong>Intermediate.</strong></li><li><strong>(x) tropone:</strong> resonance transfers electron density from $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ into the ring to generate the aromatic tropylium cation ($\mathrm{C{-}O^{-}}$ with a $6\pi$ aromatic ring). This gives the C–O bond the greatest single-bond character. <strong>Longest.</strong></li></ul>$$\text{Bond length: } x &gt; z &gt; y$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

$SF_4$ is isostructural with:
A. $BrF_4^{\ominus}$
B. $CH_4$
C. $IF_4^{\oplus}$
D. $XeF_4$
E. $XeO_2F_2$
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>$SF_4$ has steric number $5$ ($4$ bond pairs $+ 1$ lone pair, $AX_4E$, $sp^3d$) $\Rightarrow$ <strong>see-saw</strong> shape.</p><p>Check each option:</p><ul><li><strong>A. $BrF_4^{-}$:</strong> $4$ bonds $+ 2$ lone pairs ($AX_4E_2$) $\Rightarrow$ square planar. <strong>No.</strong></li><li><strong>B. $CH_4$:</strong> $4$ bonds, no lone pair ($AX_4$) $\Rightarrow$ tetrahedral. <strong>No.</strong></li><li><strong>C. $IF_4^{+}$:</strong> I contributes $7$ electrons, $-1$ for the charge, $4$ bonds $\Rightarrow$ $(7-1-4)/2 = 1$ lone pair, $AX_4E$ $\Rightarrow$ see-saw. <strong>Yes.</strong></li><li><strong>D. $XeF_4$:</strong> $4$ bonds $+ 2$ lone pairs $\Rightarrow$ square planar. <strong>No.</strong></li><li><strong>E. $XeO_2F_2$:</strong> Xe has $2$ $=O$, $2$ $-F$ and $1$ lone pair ($AX_4E$) $\Rightarrow$ see-saw. <strong>Yes.</strong></li></ul><p>Species isostructural with $SF_4$: <strong>C and E</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

Given below are two statements:
Statement I: The number of compounds among SO$_2$, SO$_3$, SF$_4$, SF$_6$ and H$_2$S in which sulphur does not obey the Octet rule is 3.
Statement II: Among [H$_2$O, ClF$_3$, SF$_4$], [NH$_3$, BrF$_5$, SF$_4$], [BrF$_5$, ClF$_3$, XeF$_4$] and [XeF$_4$, ClF$_3$, H$_2$O], the number of sets in which all the molecules have one lone pair of electrons on the central atom is 1.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I</strong> — electrons around S:</p><ul><li>$SO_2$: expanded octet ($10\,e^-$) — does not obey.</li><li>$SO_3$: $12\,e^-$ — does not obey.</li><li>$SF_4$: $10\,e^-$ — does not obey.</li><li>$SF_6$: $12\,e^-$ — does not obey.</li><li>$H_2S$: $2$ bonds $+ 2$ lone pairs $= 8\,e^-$ — obeys.</li></ul><p>So S does <strong>not</strong> obey the octet in $4$ compounds, not $3$. $\Rightarrow$ <strong>Statement I is false.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II</strong> — lone pairs on the central atom:</p>$$H_2O:2,\ \ NH_3:1,\ \ H_3O^+\text{—},\ \ ClF_3:2,\ \ SF_4:1,\ \ BrF_5:1,\ \ XeF_4:2$$<ul><li>$[H_2O, ClF_3, SF_4]$: $2,2,1$ — not all one.</li><li>$[NH_3, BrF_5, SF_4]$: $1,1,1$ — <strong>all one.</strong> $\checkmark$</li><li>$[BrF_5, ClF_3, XeF_4]$: $1,2,2$ — not all one.</li><li>$[XeF_4, ClF_3, H_2O]$: $2,2,2$ — not all one.</li></ul><p>Exactly $1$ set qualifies. $\Rightarrow$ <strong>Statement II is true.</strong></p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

The covalent radii of atoms A and B are $r_A$ and $r_B$, respectively. The covalent bond length and total length of AB molecule are respectively

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The <strong>covalent bond length</strong> is the internuclear A–B distance, which is the sum of the two covalent radii:</p>$$d_{A-B} = r_A + r_B$$<p>The <strong>total length</strong> of the AB molecule spans from the outer edge of A to the outer edge of B:</p>$$L = r_A + d_{A-B} + r_B = r_A + (r_A + r_B) + r_B = 2(r_A + r_B)$$

$$\Rightarrow\ \text{bond length} = (r_A + r_B),\qquad \text{total length} = 2(r_A + r_B)$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Bromine trifluoride autoionizes to form $BrF_2^{\oplus}$ and $BrF_4^{\ominus}$. The shapes of the cation and anion are respectively __________, and __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Autoionization: $\ 2\,BrF_3 \rightleftharpoons BrF_2^{+} + BrF_4^{-}$.</p><p><strong>Cation $BrF_2^{+}$:</strong> Br has $7$ electrons, $-1$ for the positive charge, $2$ used in bonding:</p>$$\text{lone pairs} = \frac{7 - 1 - 2}{2} = 2,\quad SN = 2 + 2 = 4\ (AX_2E_2)\ \Rightarrow\ \textbf{bent}$$<p><strong>Anion $BrF_4^{-}$:</strong> Br has $7$ electrons, $+1$ for the negative charge, $4$ used in bonding:</p>$$\text{lone pairs} = \frac{7 + 1 - 4}{2} = 2,\quad SN = 4 + 2 = 6\ (AX_4E_2)\ \Rightarrow\ \textbf{square planar}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>
