PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

A monoatomic anion ($A^-$) has 45 neutrons and 36 electrons. Atomic mass, group in the periodic table and physical state at room temperature of the element (A) respectively are

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The anion $A^-$ carries one extra electron, so the neutral atom has</p>$$Z = \text{electrons in } A^- - 1 = 36 - 1 = 35$$<p>An atomic number $Z = 35$ corresponds to <strong>bromine (Br)</strong>.</p>$$\text{Atomic mass} = Z + \text{neutrons} = 35 + 45 = 80$$<p>Bromine’s configuration is $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^{10}\,4s^2\,4p^5$, placing it in <strong>Group 17</strong> (halogens). Elemental bromine $\left(\text{Br}_2\right)$ is a reddish-brown <strong>liquid</strong> at room temperature.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (80, 17, liquid)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

Given below are two statements:

**Statement (I):** The first ionisation enthalpy of the elements Na, Mg, Cl and Ar follows the order Na > Mg > Cl > Ar.

**Statement (II):** Among Ca, Al, Fe and B, the third ionisation enthalpy is very high for Ca.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> First ionisation enthalpy increases across a period. Approximate values (kJ/mol):</p>$$\text{Na} = 496,\quad \text{Mg} = 737,\quad \text{Cl} = 1251,\quad \text{Ar} = 1521$$<p>So the true order is $\text{Ar} &gt; \text{Cl} &gt; \text{Mg} &gt; \text{Na}$, the <strong>reverse</strong> of what is stated. <strong>Statement I is false.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> Calcium is $[\text{Ar}]\,4s^2$. Removing two electrons gives $\text{Ca}^{2+} = [\text{Ar}]$, a stable noble-gas core. The third electron must come from this core, so</p>$$IE_3(\text{Ca}) \gg IE_3(\text{Al, Fe, B})$$<p><strong>Statement II is true.</strong></p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D (Statement I is false but Statement II is true)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

The 1$^{st}$ ionisation enthalpy for Mg is $+737$ kJ/mol. The most probable estimated value of the 2$^{nd}$ ionisation enthalpy of Mg is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Ionisation enthalpies are always <strong>endothermic (positive)</strong>, so the answer cannot be negative — this rules out $-906$ and $-856$.</p><p>The second ionisation removes an electron from a <strong>positive ion</strong> $\left(\text{Mg}^+\right)$, where the remaining electrons are held more tightly by the same nuclear charge. Hence</p>$$IE_2 &gt; IE_1 = 737 \text{ kJ/mol}$$<p>This rules out $+590$. The only value that is both positive and greater than $737$ is $+1450$ kJ/mol. (The experimental value of $IE_2$ for Mg is $\approx 1451$ kJ/mol.)</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($+1450$ kJ/mol)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

Given below are two statements:

**Statement I:** The second ionisation enthalpy of B, Al and Ga is in the order $B > Al > Ga$.

**Statement II:** The correct order in terms of first ionisation enthalpy is $Si < Ge < Pb < Sn$.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> After losing one electron, the second electron is removed from Group-13 cations. Approximate $IE_2$ values (kJ/mol):</p>$$\text{B} = 2427,\quad \text{Al} = 1817,\quad \text{Ga} = 1979$$<p>The true order is $\text{B} &gt; \text{Ga} &gt; \text{Al}$ (Ga’s higher value comes from poor shielding by the intervening $3d^{10}$ electrons). This is <strong>not</strong> $B &gt; Al &gt; Ga$, so <strong>Statement I is false.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> First ionisation enthalpies of Group-14 elements (kJ/mol):</p>$$\text{Si} = 786,\quad \text{Ge} = 762,\quad \text{Sn} = 709,\quad \text{Pb} = 716$$<p>The true order is $\text{Sn} &lt; \text{Pb} &lt; \text{Ge} &lt; \text{Si}$, not $Si &lt; Ge &lt; Pb &lt; Sn$. <strong>Statement II is false.</strong></p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B (Both Statement I and Statement II are false)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

In a period, the first ionisation enthalpy of the element at extreme left and the negative electron gain enthalpy of the extreme right element, except noble gases, are respectively.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Extreme left element</strong> of a period is an <strong>alkali metal</strong> (Group 1). It has a single loosely held $ns^1$ electron, so its first ionisation enthalpy is the <strong>lowest</strong> in the period.</p><p><strong>Extreme right element (excluding noble gases)</strong> is a <strong>halogen</strong> (Group 17). It needs just one electron to complete its octet, releasing a large amount of energy, so its negative electron gain enthalpy is the <strong>highest</strong> (most negative) in the period.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C (lowest and highest)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

Given below are two statements:

**Statement I:** The correct order of electronegativity of fluorine, oxygen and nitrogen is $F > O > N$.

**Statement II:** The oxidation state of oxygen in $OF_2$ is $+2$ and in $Na_2O$ is $-2$.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Electronegativity increases across Period 2. On the Pauling scale:</p>$$\text{F} = 4.0 &gt; \text{O} = 3.5 &gt; \text{N} = 3.0$$<p>So $F &gt; O &gt; N$ is correct. <strong>Statement I is true.</strong></p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> In $OF_2$, fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen, so F takes $-1$ each:</p>$$x + 2(-1) = 0 \implies x = +2$$<p>In $Na_2O$, sodium is $+1$ each:</p>$$2(+1) + y = 0 \implies y = -2$$<p>Both assignments are correct. <strong>Statement II is true.</strong></p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (Both Statement I and Statement II are true)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Match List-I with List-II ($n = 2$, i.e. Period 2):

**List-I (Electronic configuration of neutral atom):**

- A. $ns^2$
- B. $ns^2np^1$
- C. $ns^2np^3$
- D. $ns^2np^6$

**List-II (1st Ionisation Energy, kJ mol$^{-1}$):**

- I. 2080
- II. 899
- III. 800
- IV. 1402

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For $n = 2$, identify each element and its known first ionisation energy (kJ/mol):</p>$$\text{A: } 2s^2 = \text{Be} \;(899) \rightarrow \text{II}$$<p></p>$$\text{B: } 2s^2 2p^1 = \text{B} \;(800) \rightarrow \text{III}$$<p></p>$$\text{C: } 2s^2 2p^3 = \text{N} \;(1402) \rightarrow \text{IV}$$<p></p>$$\text{D: } 2s^2 2p^6 = \text{Ne} \;(2080) \rightarrow \text{I}$$<p>Note nitrogen’s half-filled $2p^3$ gives extra stability, raising its $IE_1$ above that of oxygen; neon (noble gas) has the highest value.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (A-II, B-III, C-IV, D-I)</p></div></details>
