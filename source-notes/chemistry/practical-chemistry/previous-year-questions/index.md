PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

A salt with few drops of conc. HCl gives apple green colour in flame test. The group precipitate of the salt is dissolved in acetic acid and treated with K$_2$CrO$_4$ to give yellow precipitate. When the sodium carbonate extract of the salt solution is heated with conc. HNO$_3$ and ammonium molybdate, it resulted a canary yellow precipitate. The cation and anion present in the salt are respectively,

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Read each test in turn:</p><p><strong>Flame test — apple green:</strong> An apple/grass-green flame is characteristic of $Ba^{2+}$. This eliminates $Ca^{2+}$ (brick-red flame) and $Mn^{2+}$.</p><p><strong>Group precipitate + acetic acid + $K_2CrO_4$ → yellow precipitate:</strong> $Ba^{2+}$ falls in Group V and its precipitate dissolves in acetic acid. Treatment with chromate gives yellow barium chromate:</p>$$Ba^{2+} + CrO_4^{2-} \longrightarrow BaCrO_4\!\downarrow \ (\text{yellow})$$<p>Note that $BaSO_4$ would <strong>not</strong> dissolve in acetic acid, so the salt’s anion is not sulphate.</p><p><strong>Sodium carbonate extract + conc. $HNO_3$ + ammonium molybdate → canary yellow precipitate:</strong> This is the confirmatory test for the phosphate ion, giving ammonium phosphomolybdate:</p>$$PO_4^{3-} + 12\,MoO_4^{2-} + 3\,NH_4^+ + 24\,H^+ \longrightarrow (NH_4)_3[PMo_{12}O_{40}]\!\downarrow \ (\text{canary yellow})$$<p>So the cation is $Ba^{2+}$ and the anion is $PO_4^{3-}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

Among Fe$^{3+}$, Pb$^{2+}$, Cu$^{2+}$ and Mn$^{2+}$, identify the one that gets precipitated out while passing H$_2$S in presence of NH$_4$OH as group reagent. The highest possible oxidation state of the corresponding metal is

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Passing $H_2S$ in the presence of $NH_4OH$ (alkaline medium) is the <strong>Group IV</strong> reagent, precipitating cations as sulphides.</p><p>Classify each cation by its analytical group:</p><ul><li>$Pb^{2+}$ and $Cu^{2+}$ → <strong>Group II</strong> (precipitated by $H_2S$ in acidic medium).</li><li>$Fe^{3+}$ → <strong>Group III</strong> (precipitated by $NH_4OH$/$NH_4Cl$ as hydroxide, not sulphide).</li><li>$Mn^{2+}$ → <strong>Group IV</strong>, precipitated by $H_2S$ in the presence of $NH_4OH$ as buff-coloured $MnS$:
$$Mn^{2+} + H_2S \xrightarrow{\ NH_4OH\ } MnS\!\downarrow + 2H^+$$<p></p></li></ul><p>So the ion precipitated here is $Mn^{2+}$. Manganese has electron configuration $[Ar]\,3d^5\,4s^2$, giving a highest oxidation state of $+7$ (as in $KMnO_4$).</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

Match the LIST-I with LIST-II

**List-I (Compound):** A. cyclohexanol; B. cyclohexylamine; C. cyclohexanecarbaldehyde; D. phenol

**List-II (Test):** I. Hinsberg’s reagent test; II. Phthalein dye test; III. Lucas test; IV. Tollen’s test

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Match each compound to the test that identifies its functional group:</p><ul><li><strong>A. Cyclohexanol</strong> is a secondary alcohol → the <strong>Lucas test</strong> (III) distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols by rate of turbidity with conc. HCl/$ZnCl_2$.</li><li><strong>B. Cyclohexylamine</strong> is a primary amine → <strong>Hinsberg’s reagent test</strong> (I) with benzenesulphonyl chloride gives a product soluble in alkali.</li><li><strong>C. Cyclohexanecarbaldehyde</strong> is an aldehyde → <strong>Tollen’s test</strong> (IV) gives a silver mirror with ammoniacal $AgNO_3$.</li><li><strong>D. Phenol</strong> → <strong>Phthalein dye test</strong> (II); phenol with phthalic anhydride gives phenolphthalein.</li></ul><p>Therefore: A-III, B-I, C-IV, D-II.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

Given below are two statements:

**Statement I:** Sodium dichromate and potassium dichromate are classified as primary standards in titrimetric analysis.

**Statement II:** Phenolphthalein is a weak base, therefore it dissociates in acidic medium.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>A primary standard must be a stable, high-purity solid of exactly known composition that is <strong>non-hygroscopic</strong>, so it can be weighed directly to make a standard solution.</p><p><strong>Statement I:</strong> Only <strong>potassium dichromate ($K_2Cr_2O_7$)</strong> meets this test — it is anhydrous and non-hygroscopic, so it is a primary standard. <strong>Sodium dichromate</strong>, however, is <strong>hygroscopic (deliquescent)</strong> and is normally obtained as the dihydrate $Na_2Cr_2O_7\cdot 2H_2O$; it absorbs moisture and cannot be weighed to an exactly known composition, so it is <strong>not</strong> a primary standard. Because the statement lumps <em>both</em> dichromates together as primary standards, Statement I is <strong>false</strong>.</p><p><strong>Statement II:</strong> Phenolphthalein is a <strong>weak organic acid</strong> (not a base). It stays colourless and essentially <strong>un-ionised in acidic medium</strong>, and dissociates (turning pink) only in basic medium, pH $\approx 8.3$–$10$. So calling it a weak base that dissociates in acidic medium is <strong>false</strong> on both counts. Statement II is <strong>false</strong>.</p><p>Hence both Statement I and Statement II are false.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

A paper dipped in a dil. H$_2$SO$_4$ solution of ‘X’ upon treatment with SO$_2$ gas turns into green. The compound ‘X’ is :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>$SO_2$ is a reducing gas. A paper that turns <strong>green</strong> on exposure to $SO_2$ contains acidified dichromate: the orange $Cr_2O_7^{2-}$ (Cr in $+6$) is reduced to green $Cr^{3+}$:</p>$$Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3SO_2 + 2H^+ \longrightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 3SO_4^{2-} + H_2O$$<p>The orange-to-green colour change is characteristic of $K_2Cr_2O_7$.</p><p>Checking the others: KI-starch turns blue-black with oxidising gases (not $SO_2$); acidified $KMnO_4$ is decolourised (purple → colourless), not green; $Pb(CH_3COO)_2$ paper detects $H_2S$ (turns black), not $SO_2$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>
