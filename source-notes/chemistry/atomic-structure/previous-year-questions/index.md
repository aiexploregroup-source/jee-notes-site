PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

What is the ratio of wave number of first line (lowest energy line) of Balmer series of H atomic spectrum to first line of its Brackett series?

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Wave number: $\bar{\nu} = R_H Z^2 \left(\dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$, with $Z = 1$ for H.</p><p><strong>Balmer first line</strong> ($n_1 = 2,\ n_2 = 3$):</p>$$\bar{\nu}_B = R_H\left(\dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2}\right) = R_H\left(\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9}\right) = R_H \cdot \dfrac{5}{36}$$<p><strong>Brackett first line</strong> ($n_1 = 4,\ n_2 = 5$):</p>$$\bar{\nu}_{Br} = R_H\left(\dfrac{1}{4^2} - \dfrac{1}{5^2}\right) = R_H\left(\dfrac{1}{16} - \dfrac{1}{25}\right) = R_H \cdot \dfrac{9}{400}$$<p>Ratio:</p>$$\dfrac{\bar{\nu}_B}{\bar{\nu}_{Br}} = \dfrac{5/36}{9/400} = \dfrac{5}{36}\cdot\dfrac{400}{9} = \dfrac{2000}{324} = 6.17$$<p>Writing as $5 : x$: $\ x = \dfrac{5}{6.17} = 0.81$, so the ratio is $5 : 0.81$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

If shortest wavelength of hydrogen atom in Lyman series is $x$, then longest wavelength in Balmer series of $\text{He}^+$ is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Shortest wavelength, Lyman (H):</strong> transition $n = \infty \to 1$, $Z = 1$:</p>$$\dfrac{1}{x} = R\cdot 1^2\left(\dfrac{1}{1^2} - 0\right) = R \quad\Rightarrow\quad R = \dfrac{1}{x}$$<p><strong>Longest wavelength, Balmer ($\text{He}^+$):</strong> smallest energy gap $n = 3 \to 2$, $Z = 2$:</p>$$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R\cdot 2^2\left(\dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2}\right) = 4R\left(\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9}\right) = 4R\cdot\dfrac{5}{36} = \dfrac{5R}{9}$$<p>Therefore:</p>$$\lambda = \dfrac{9}{5R} = \dfrac{9}{5}\cdot\dfrac{1}{R} = \dfrac{9x}{5}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $\dfrac{9x}{5}$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

First and second ionization enthalpies of lithium are 520 kJ mol$^{-1}$ and 7297 kJ mol$^{-1}$ respectively. Energy required to convert 3.5 mg lithium (g) into $\text{Li}^{2+}$ (g) $[\text{Li}(g) \to \text{Li}^{2+}(g)]$ is ________ kJ mol$^{-1}$. (nearest integer) [Molar mass of Li = 7 g mol$^{-1}$]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The process $\text{Li}(g) \to \text{Li}^{2+}(g)$ removes two electrons, so the molar energy is the sum of the first two ionization enthalpies:</p>$$\Delta E = IE_1 + IE_2 = 520 + 7297 = 7817\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p>The requested unit is <strong>kJ mol$^{-1}$</strong> (a per-mole quantity), so the given mass of 3.5 mg does not change the molar answer.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 7817</p></div></details>

What is the energy (in J atom$^{-1}$) required for the following process?

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>$\text{Li}^{2+}$ is a single-electron (hydrogen-like) species with $Z = 3$, electron in the ground state ($n = 1$). Ionization energy scales as $Z^2$:</p>$$IE = 2.18\times10^{-18}\cdot Z^2 = 2.18\times10^{-18}\times 3^2$$<p></p>$$= 2.18\times10^{-18}\times 9 = 1.962\times10^{-17}\ \text{J atom}^{-1}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $1.962 \times 10^{-17}$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

The surface of sodium metal is irradiated with radiation of wavelength $x$ nm. The kinetic energy of ejected electrons is $2.8 \times 10^{-20}$ J. The work function of sodium is 2.3 eV. The value of $x$ is __________ $\times 10^2$ nm. (Nearest integer) (Given : $h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J s; 1 eV $= 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J; $c = 3.0 \times 10^8$ m s$^{-1}$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Photoelectric equation: $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = W_0 + KE$.</p><p>Work function in joules:</p>$$W_0 = 2.3\times 1.6\times10^{-19} = 3.68\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$<p>Photon energy:</p>$$E = W_0 + KE = 3.68\times10^{-19} + 2.8\times10^{-20} = 3.96\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$<p>Wavelength:</p>$$\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E} = \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}\times 3.0\times10^{8}}{3.96\times10^{-19}} = \dfrac{1.98\times10^{-25}}{3.96\times10^{-19}} = 5.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}$$

$$\lambda = 500\ \text{nm} = 5\times10^{2}\ \text{nm}$$<p>So $x = 5$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 5</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

Identify the correct statements from the following :

A. Heisenberg uncertainty principle is applicable to electrons.
B. The size of $2p_x$ orbital is less than the size of $3p_x$ orbital.
C. The energy of $2s$ orbital of H atom is equal to the energy of $2s$ orbital of Li.
D. The electronic configuration of Cr is [Ar] $3d^5 4s^1$.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>A.</strong> True — Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle applies to microscopic particles such as electrons.</p><p><strong>B.</strong> True — a higher principal quantum number means a larger orbital, so $2p_x &lt; 3p_x$ in size.</p><p><strong>C.</strong> False — orbital energies depend on nuclear charge. For a given shell, energy $\propto Z^2$ (H-like) or is shifted by $Z_{\text{eff}}$ in multi-electron atoms, so the $2s$ energy of Li is much lower (more negative) than that of H.</p><p><strong>D.</strong> True — Cr shows the stable half-filled configuration $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^5\,4s^1$.</p><p>Correct statements: A, B and D.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

Which of the following statement(s) is/are true ?
A. If two orbitals have the same value of $(n + l)$, the orbital with lower value of $n$ will have lower energy.
B. Energies of the orbitals in the same subshell increase with increase in atomic number.
C. The size of $2p_x$ orbital is less than the size of $3p_x$ orbital.
D. Among 5f, 6s, 4d, 5p and 5d orbitals, none of the orbitals have 2 radial nodes.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>A.</strong> True — for equal $(n+l)$, the orbital with lower $n$ has lower energy (a corollary of the Aufbau rule).</p><p><strong>B.</strong> False — as atomic number increases, the increased nuclear attraction makes orbital energies <strong>more negative</strong> (they decrease, not increase).</p><p><strong>C.</strong> True — $3p_x$ is larger than $2p_x$ due to the higher principal quantum number.</p><p><strong>D.</strong> Radial nodes $= n - l - 1$:</p>$$5f:\ 5-3-1=1,\quad 6s:\ 6-0-1=5,\quad 4d:\ 4-2-1=1,\quad 5p:\ 5-1-1=3,\quad 5d:\ 5-2-1=2$$<p>The $5d$ orbital has 2 radial nodes, so the claim “none have 2 radial nodes” is <strong>False</strong>.</p><p>Correct statements: A and C only.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Consider two radiations of wavelengths

1. $\lambda_1 = 2000$ Å
2. $\lambda_2 = 6000$ Å
   The ratio of the energies of these two radiations $\left(\frac{E_1}{E_2}\right)$ is __________ (Nearest integer).

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Photon energy is inversely proportional to wavelength: $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, so $E \propto \dfrac{1}{\lambda}$.</p>$$\dfrac{E_1}{E_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \dfrac{6000}{2000} = 3$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 3</p></div></details>
