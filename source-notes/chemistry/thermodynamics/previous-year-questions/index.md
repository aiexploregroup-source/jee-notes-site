PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

Given below are two statements:

**Statement I:** For an ideal gas, heat capacity at constant volume is always greater than the heat capacity at constant pressure.

**Statement II:** In a constant volume process, no work is produced and all the heat withdrawn goes into the chaotic motion and is reflected by a temperature increase of the ideal gas.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For an ideal gas the Mayer relation holds:</p>$$C_p = C_V + R$$<p>Since $R &gt; 0$, we always have $C_p &gt; C_V$. So Statement I (claiming $C_V &gt; C_p$) is <strong>false</strong>.</p><p>At constant volume, $w = -p_{ext}\,\Delta V = 0$, so the first law gives:</p>$$q_V = \Delta U = nC_V\,\Delta T$$<p>All the heat added raises the internal (chaotic) molecular energy and hence the temperature. Statement II is <strong>true</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

Arrange the following isothermal processes in order of the magnitude of the work $(p\text{-}V)$ involved between states 1 and 2.

A. Expansion in single stage $w_A$; B. Expansion in multi stages $w_B$; C. Compression in single stage $w_C$; D. Compression in multi stages $w_D$. Choose the correct option.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For an isothermal gas moving between the same two states, the work magnitude depends on the external pressure profile.</p><p><strong>Expansion (1 → 2, gas pushes out):</strong></p><ul><li>Single stage against the low final pressure $p_2$: $|w_A| = p_2(V_2 - V_1)$ — smallest expansion work.</li><li>Multi stage / reversible (external pressure tracks the gas): $|w_B| = nRT\ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}$ — larger.</li></ul><p><strong>Compression (2 → 1, surroundings push in):</strong></p><ul><li>Multi stage / reversible: $|w_D| = nRT\ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1} = |w_B|$ — minimum compression work.</li><li>Single stage against the high pressure $p_1$: $|w_C| = p_1(V_2 - V_1)$ — maximum.</li></ul><p>So the ranking is:</p>$$|w_C| &gt; |w_B| = |w_D| &gt; |w_A|$$<p>The single-stage compression is largest and the single-stage expansion is smallest. Among the choices, this matches:</p>$$|w_C| &gt; |w_D| &gt; |w_B| &gt; |w_A|$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

Consider the reaction $X \rightleftharpoons Y$ at 300 K. If $\Delta H^\theta$ and K are 28.40 kJ mol$^{-1}$ and $1.8 \times 10^{-7}$ at the same temperature, then the magnitude of $\Delta S^\theta$ for the reaction in J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$ is ________. (Nearest integer)

(Given: R = 8.3 J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$, ln 10 = 2.3, log 3 = 0.48, log 2 = 0.30)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>First find $\Delta G^\theta$ from the equilibrium constant:</p>$$\Delta G^\theta = -RT\ln K$$<p>Evaluate $\ln K = 2.303\log(1.8\times10^{-7})$ using the given logs:</p>$$\log 1.8 = \log 2 + 2\log 3 - 1 = 0.30 + 0.96 - 1 = 0.26$$

$$\log K = 0.26 - 7 = -6.74 \implies \ln K = 2.3 \times (-6.74) = -15.5$$

$$\Delta G^\theta = -(8.3)(300)(-15.5) = 38{,}600 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$$<p>Now use $\Delta G^\theta = \Delta H^\theta - T\Delta S^\theta$:</p>$$\Delta S^\theta = \frac{\Delta H^\theta - \Delta G^\theta}{T} = \frac{28{,}400 - 38{,}600}{300} = -34 \text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$$<p>Magnitude $= 34$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 34</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

Consider the following data (values are heat released, so each $\Delta H$ is negative):

(i) $2\mathrm{Al}(s) + 6\mathrm{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow \mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{Cl}_6(aq) + 3\mathrm{H}_2(g) + 1200$ kJ/mol

(ii) $\mathrm{H}_2(g) + \mathrm{Cl}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{HCl}(g) + 164$ kJ/mol

(iii) $\mathrm{HCl}(g) + aq \rightarrow \mathrm{HCl}(aq) + 83$ kJ/mol

(iv) $\mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{Cl}_6(s) + aq \rightarrow \mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{Cl}_6(aq) + 663$ kJ/mol

The enthalpy of formation of anhydrous solid Al$_2$Cl$_6$ is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Target reaction:</p>$$2\mathrm{Al}(s) + 3\mathrm{Cl}_2(g) \rightarrow \mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{Cl}_6(s)$$<p>Assign $\Delta H$ values (heat released ⇒ negative):</p>$$\Delta H_1=-1200,\quad \Delta H_2=-164,\quad \Delta H_3=-83,\quad \Delta H_4=-663 \text{ kJ}$$<p>Combine: take (i), add $3\times$(ii) to make 6 HCl(g), add $6\times$(iii) to convert HCl(g) → HCl(aq), and reverse (iv) to precipitate the solid. The aqueous $\mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{Cl}_6(aq)$, $3\mathrm{H}_2$, HCl(g) and HCl(aq) species all cancel, leaving the target:</p>$$\Delta H_f = \Delta H_1 + 3\Delta H_2 + 6\Delta H_3 - \Delta H_4$$

$$\Delta H_f = -1200 + 3(-164) + 6(-83) - (-663)$$

$$= -1200 - 492 - 498 + 663 = -1527 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $-1527$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ (D)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

At the transition temperature T, $A \rightleftharpoons B$ and $\Delta G^0 = 105 - 35\log T$ where A and B are two states of substance X. The transition temperature in °C when pressure is 1 atm is __________. (Nearest integer)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>At the transition temperature the two states are in equilibrium, so $\Delta G^0 = 0$:</p>$$105 - 35\log T = 0 \implies \log T = 3 \implies T = 10^{3} = 1000 \text{ K}$$<p>Convert to Celsius:</p>$$t = 1000 - 273 = 727\ ^\circ\text{C}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 727</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

If 3.365 g of ethanol ($l$) is burnt completely in a bomb calorimeter at 298.15 K, the heat produced is 99.472 kJ. The $|\Delta H_f^\ominus|$ of ethanol at 298.15 K is __________ $\times 10^2$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. (Nearest integer)

Given: Standard enthalpy for combustion of graphite $= -393.5$ kJ mol$^{-1}$; Standard enthalpy of formation of water(l) $= -285.8$ kJ mol$^{-1}$; Molar masses of C, H, O are 12, 1 and 16 g mol$^{-1}$.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Molar mass of C$_2$H$_5$OH $= 46$ g mol$^{-1}$, so moles burnt:</p>$$n = \frac{3.365}{46} = 0.07315 \text{ mol}$$<p>A bomb calorimeter is a constant-volume system, so the measured heat is $\Delta U_c$:</p>$$\Delta U_c = \frac{-99.472}{0.07315} = -1359.8 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p>Convert to $\Delta H_c$ for $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}(l) + 3\mathrm{O_2}(g) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{CO_2}(g) + 3\mathrm{H_2O}(l)$, with $\Delta n_g = 2-3 = -1$:</p>$$\Delta H_c = \Delta U_c + \Delta n_g RT = -1359.8 + (-1)(8.314\times10^{-3})(298.15) \approx -1362.3 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p>Apply Hess’s law, $\Delta H_c = 2\Delta H_f(\mathrm{CO_2}) + 3\Delta H_f(\mathrm{H_2O}) - \Delta H_f(\text{ethanol})$:</p>$$\Delta H_f(\text{ethanol}) = 2(-393.5) + 3(-285.8) - (-1362.3) = -282.1 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

$$|\Delta H_f^\ominus| = 282.1 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1} = 2.82\times10^2 \approx 3\times10^2$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 3</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

Gas ‘A’ undergoes change from state ‘X’ to state ‘Y’. In this process, the heat absorbed and work done by the gas is 10 J and 18 J respectively. Now gas is brought back to state ‘X’ by another process during which 6 J of heat is evolved. In the reverse process of ‘Y’ to ‘X’:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Internal energy is a state function. For X → Y (using $\Delta U = q - w_{by}$):</p>$$\Delta U_{X\to Y} = q - w_{by} = 10 - 18 = -8 \text{ J}$$<p>For the return Y → X, the change reverses sign:</p>$$\Delta U_{Y\to X} = +8 \text{ J}, \qquad q = -6 \text{ J (evolved)}$$<p>Apply the first law again:</p>$$\Delta U_{Y\to X} = q - w_{by} \implies 8 = -6 - w_{by} \implies w_{by} = -14 \text{ J}$$<p>A negative $w_{by}$ means 14 J of work is done <strong>on</strong> the gas by the surroundings.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

Match List - I with List - II. Given $V_1$ and $V_2$ are initial and final volumes respectively.

**List - I (Isothermal process):** A. Reversible expansion; B. Free expansion; C. Irreversible Compression; D. Cyclic reversible.

**List - II (Expression):** I. $q=0$; II. $q=nRT\ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}$; III. $w=-p_{ext}(V_1-V_2)$; IV. $\dfrac{q_{rev}}{T}=0$.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><ul><li><strong>A. Reversible isothermal expansion:</strong> $\Delta U = 0$, so $q = -w = nRT\ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}$ → <strong>II</strong>.</li><li><strong>B. Free expansion:</strong> $p_{ext}=0 \Rightarrow w=0$; isothermal ideal gas has $\Delta U=0$, so $q=0$ → <strong>I</strong>.</li><li><strong>C. Irreversible compression:</strong> work against constant external pressure, $w=-p_{ext}(V_2-V_1)=-p_{ext}(V_1-V_2)$ as written → <strong>III</strong>.</li><li><strong>D. Cyclic reversible:</strong> entropy is a state function, so over a cycle $\displaystyle\oint \dfrac{q_{rev}}{T}=0$ → <strong>IV</strong>.</li></ul><p>So A-II, B-I, C-III, D-IV.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

Consider the following data for the reaction $X_2(g) + Y_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2XY(g)$ at 600 K. The $\Delta_r G^\circ$ (in kJ mol$^{-1}$) for the reaction is:

| Compound | $\Delta_f H^\circ_{600K}$ (kJ mol$^{-1}$) | $S^\circ_{600K}$ (J mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| XY(g) | 42 | 200 |
| X$_2$(g) | 8 | 140 |
| Y$_2$(g) | 80 | 250 |

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Enthalpy of reaction:</p>$$\Delta_r H^\circ = 2(42) - (8 + 80) = 84 - 88 = -4 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p>Entropy of reaction:</p>$$\Delta_r S^\circ = 2(200) - (140 + 250) = 400 - 390 = 10 \text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$$<p>Gibbs free energy at 600 K:</p>$$\Delta_r G^\circ = \Delta_r H^\circ - T\Delta_r S^\circ = -4 - 600\times\frac{10}{1000} = -4 - 6 = -10 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $-10$ (B)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

The correct order of molar heat capacities measured at 298 K and 1 bar is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Compare typical molar heat capacities at 298 K:</p><ul><li><strong>Helium(g)</strong> — monatomic ideal gas, $C_{p,m} = \tfrac{5}{2}R \approx 20.8$ J mol$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ (only translational modes).</li><li><strong>Copper(s)</strong> — solid, $C_{p,m} \approx 24.4$ J mol$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ (Dulong–Petit, $\approx 3R$).</li><li><strong>Bromine(l)</strong> — a polyatomic liquid with many active vibrational/rotational modes, $C_{p,m} \approx 75.7$ J mol$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, the highest.</li></ul><p>So the order is:</p>$$\text{Bromine(l)} &gt; \text{Copper(s)} &gt; \text{Helium(g)}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Consider the reaction

$$2H_2S(g) + 3O_2(g) \rightarrow 2H_2O(l) + 2SO_2(g)$$

The magnitude of enthalpy change for the reaction in kJ mol$^{-1}$ is __________. (Nearest integer)

Given: $\Delta_f H^{\ominus}(H_2S) = -20.1$ kJ mol$^{-1}$, $\Delta_f H^{\ominus}(H_2O) = -286.0$ kJ mol$^{-1}$, $\Delta_f H^{\ominus}(SO_2) = -297.0$ kJ mol$^{-1}$.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Using $\Delta_r H = \sum \Delta_f H_{\text{products}} - \sum \Delta_f H_{\text{reactants}}$, with $\Delta_f H(\mathrm{O_2}) = 0$:</p>$$\Delta_r H = \big[2(-286.0) + 2(-297.0)\big] - \big[2(-20.1) + 3(0)\big]$$

$$= (-572.0 - 594.0) - (-40.2) = -1166.0 + 40.2 = -1125.8 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$<p>Magnitude $= 1125.8 \approx 1126$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 1126</p></div></details>
