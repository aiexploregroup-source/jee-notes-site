PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 1

A non-volatile, non-electrolyte solid solute when dissolved in 40 g of a solvent, the vapour pressure of the solvent decreased from 760 mm Hg to 750 mm Hg. If the same solution boils at 320 K, then the number of moles of the solvent present in the solution is _____. (Nearest integer)
[Given: boiling point of the pure solvent = 319.5 K, $K_b$ of the solvent = 0.3 K kg mol$^{-1}$]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — Moles of solute from boiling point elevation.</strong></p>$$\Delta T_b = T_b - T_b^\circ = 320 - 319.5 = 0.5\ \text{K}.$$<p></p>$$\Delta T_b = K_b\, m \implies m = \frac{0.5}{0.3} = \frac{5}{3}\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}.$$<p>With solvent mass $= 40\ \text{g} = 0.040\ \text{kg}$:</p>$$n_{\text{solute}} = m \times 0.040 = \frac{5}{3}\times 0.040 = 0.0667\ \text{mol}.$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Moles of solvent from relative lowering of vapour pressure.</strong></p>$$\frac{\Delta p}{p^\circ} = x_{\text{solute}} = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{n_{\text{solute}}+n_{\text{solvent}}}.$$<p></p>$$\frac{760-750}{760} = \frac{10}{760} = \frac{1}{76} = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{n_{\text{solute}}+n_{\text{solvent}}}.$$<p></p>$$n_{\text{solute}}+n_{\text{solvent}} = 76\,n_{\text{solute}} \implies n_{\text{solvent}} = 75\,n_{\text{solute}}.$$<p></p>$$n_{\text{solvent}} = 75 \times 0.0667 = 5.0\ \text{mol}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 5</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 6, Shift 1

When 0.25 moles of a non-volatile, non-ionizable solute was dissolved in 1 mole of a solvent the vapor pressure of solution was $x$ % of vapor pressure of pure solvent. What is $x$ %?

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>By Raoult’s law for a non-volatile solute, the vapour pressure of the solution equals the mole fraction of the solvent times the pure-solvent vapour pressure:</p>$$p_{\text{soln}} = x_{\text{solvent}}\, p^\circ.$$<p></p>$$x_{\text{solvent}} = \frac{n_{\text{solvent}}}{n_{\text{solvent}}+n_{\text{solute}}} = \frac{1}{1+0.25} = \frac{1}{1.25} = 0.80.$$<p></p>$$\frac{p_{\text{soln}}}{p^\circ} = 0.80 = 80\%.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D (80%)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 1

19.5 g of fluoro acetic acid (molar mass = 78 g mol$^{-1}$) is dissolved in 500 g of water at 298 K. The depression in the freezing point of water was 1°C. What is $K_a$ of fluoro acetic acid? (For water, $K_f = 1.86$ K kg mol$^{-1}$). Assume molarity and molality to have same values.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — Molality of the acid.</strong></p>$$n_{\text{acid}} = \frac{19.5}{78} = 0.25\ \text{mol},\qquad m = \frac{0.25}{0.500\ \text{kg}} = 0.5\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}.$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Van’t Hoff factor from freezing point depression.</strong></p>$$\Delta T_f = i\,K_f\,m \implies i = \frac{\Delta T_f}{K_f\,m} = \frac{1}{1.86 \times 0.5} = \frac{1}{0.93} = 1.0753.$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Degree of dissociation.</strong> For a weak monobasic acid $\mathrm{HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-}$, $i = 1+\alpha$:</p>$$\alpha = i - 1 = 0.0753.$$<p><strong>Step 4 — Dissociation constant.</strong> With $C = m = 0.5\ \text{M}$:</p>$$K_a = \frac{C\alpha^2}{1-\alpha} = \frac{0.5 \times (0.0753)^2}{1 - 0.0753} = \frac{0.5 \times 0.00567}{0.9247} = \frac{0.002835}{0.9247} \approx 3.07\times10^{-3}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D ($3 \times 10^{-3}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 2

Solution A is prepared by dissolving 1 g of a protein (molar mass = 50000 g mol$^{-1}$) in 0.5 L of water at 300 K. Its osmotic pressure is $x$ bar. Solution B is made by dissolving 2 g of same protein in 1 L of water at 300 K. Osmotic pressure of solution B is $y$ bar. Entire solution of A is mixed with entire solution of B at same temperature. The osmotic pressure of resultant solution is $z$ bar. $x$, $y$ and $z$ respectively are : ($R = 0.083$ L bar mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Use $\pi = \dfrac{n}{V}RT$.</p><p><strong>Solution A:</strong> $n_A = \dfrac{1}{50000} = 2\times10^{-5}\ \text{mol}$, $V_A = 0.5\ \text{L}$.</p>$$x = \frac{2\times10^{-5}}{0.5}(0.083)(300) = 4\times10^{-5}\times 24.9 = 9.96\times10^{-4}\ \text{bar}.$$<p><strong>Solution B:</strong> $n_B = \dfrac{2}{50000} = 4\times10^{-5}\ \text{mol}$, $V_B = 1\ \text{L}$.</p>$$y = \frac{4\times10^{-5}}{1}(0.083)(300) = 4\times10^{-5}\times 24.9 = 9.96\times10^{-4}\ \text{bar}.$$<p><strong>After mixing:</strong> total moles $= 6\times10^{-5}$, total volume $= 1.5\ \text{L}$.</p>$$z = \frac{6\times10^{-5}}{1.5}(0.083)(300) = 4\times10^{-5}\times 24.9 = 9.96\times10^{-4}\ \text{bar}.$$<p>All three osmotic pressures equal $9.96\times10^{-4}$ bar.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A ($9.96 \times 10^{-4}$; $9.96 \times 10^{-4}$; $9.96 \times 10^{-4}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 1

What is the mole fraction of water in 10% by weight (w/w) of aqueous urea solution?
[Given: Molar mass of H, O, C and N are 1, 16, 12 and 14 g mol$^{-1}$ respectively.]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Take 100 g of solution: 10 g urea and 90 g water.
Molar mass of urea $\mathrm{(NH_2)_2CO} = 2(14+2) + 12 + 16 = 60\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.</p>$$n_{\text{urea}} = \frac{10}{60} = 0.1667\ \text{mol},\qquad n_{\text{water}} = \frac{90}{18} = 5\ \text{mol}.$$<p></p>$$x_{\text{water}} = \frac{n_{\text{water}}}{n_{\text{water}}+n_{\text{urea}}} = \frac{5}{5 + 0.1667} = \frac{5}{5.1667} = 0.967.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D ($0.967$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 2

20 g hemoglobin in a 1 L aqueous solution (A) at 300 K is separated from pure water by semi permeable membrane. At equilibrium the height of solution in a tube dipped in a solution (A) is found to be 80.0 mm higher than the tube dipped in water. The molar mass of hemoglobin is __________ kg mol$^{-1}$. (Nearest integer)
(Given : $g = 10$ m s$^{-2}$, $R = 8.3$ kPa dm$^3$ K$^{-1}$mol$^{-1}$, density of solution = 1000 kg m$^{-3}$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — Osmotic pressure from the height of the liquid column.</strong></p>$$\pi = \rho g h = 1000 \times 10 \times (80.0\times10^{-3}) = 800\ \text{Pa} = 0.8\ \text{kPa}.$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Relate to molar mass via $\pi V = \dfrac{w}{M}RT$.</strong></p>$$M = \frac{wRT}{\pi V} = \frac{20 \times 8.3 \times 300}{0.8 \times 1} = \frac{49800}{0.8} = 62250\ \text{g mol}^{-1}.$$<p></p>$$M = 62.25\ \text{kg mol}^{-1} \approx 62\ \text{kg mol}^{-1}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 62</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 8, Shift 2

Given below are two statements :

Given : Molar mass of C, H, O, Cl are 12, 1, 16 and 35.5 g mol$^{-1}$, respectively

Statement I : In 30% (w/w) solution of methanol in CCl$_4$ (at T K), the mole fraction of CCl$_4$ is equal to 0.33.

Statement II : Mixture of methanol and CCl$_4$ shows positive deviation from Raoult’s law.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Statement I.</strong> In 100 g solution: 30 g methanol, 70 g CCl$_4$.
Molar masses: $\mathrm{CH_3OH} = 32$, $\mathrm{CCl_4} = 12 + 4(35.5) = 154\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.</p>$$n_{\text{methanol}} = \frac{30}{32} = 0.9375,\qquad n_{\mathrm{CCl_4}} = \frac{70}{154} = 0.4545.$$<p></p>$$x_{\mathrm{CCl_4}} = \frac{0.4545}{0.9375 + 0.4545} = \frac{0.4545}{1.392} = 0.327 \approx 0.33.\ \checkmark$$<p><strong>Statement II.</strong> Pure methanol is strongly H-bonded. Adding non-polar CCl$_4$ breaks these H-bonds, weakening intermolecular attractions, so the escaping tendency (vapour pressure) exceeds the ideal value — a <strong>positive deviation</strong> from Raoult’s law. $\checkmark$</p><p>Both statements are true.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (Both Statement I and Statement II are true)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 8, Shift 2

Which of the following statements are not correct ?
A. For water, magnitude of $K_b$ is more than the magnitude of $K_f$.
B. The elevation in boiling point of water when a non-volatile solute is added to it is larger in magnitude than its depression in freezing point.
C. Osmotic pressure measurement is preferred over any other colligative property to determine molar mass of proteins and polymers.
D. The dimerised form of benzoic acid in benzene is $C_6H_5-\overset{\overset{O}{\|}}{C}-OH \cdots O=\overset{\overset{OH}{|}}{C}-C_6H_5$

Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For water, $K_b = 0.52\ \text{K kg mol}^{-1}$ and $K_f = 1.86\ \text{K kg mol}^{-1}$, so $K_f &gt; K_b$.</p><ul><li><strong>A.</strong> Claims $K_b &gt; K_f$. Since $K_f &gt; K_b$, statement A is <strong>not correct</strong>.</li><li><strong>B.</strong> For the same molality, $\Delta T_b = K_b m$ and $\Delta T_f = K_f m$; because $K_f &gt; K_b$, the freezing-point depression is larger, so B (boiling elevation larger) is <strong>not correct</strong>.</li><li><strong>C.</strong> Osmotic pressure is measurable at low concentrations and gives large, precise values, making it the preferred method for the high molar masses of proteins and polymers — <strong>correct</strong>.</li><li><strong>D.</strong> Benzoic acid dimerises in benzene through two O$\cdots$H–O hydrogen bonds between the carboxyl groups; the structure shown is <strong>correct</strong>.</li></ul><p>Not correct: A and B.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (A and B only)</p></div></details>
