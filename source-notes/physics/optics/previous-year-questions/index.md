PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 1

A slit of width $a$ is illuminated by light of wavelength $\lambda$. The linear separation between the $1^{\text{st}}$ and $3^{\text{rd}}$ minima in the diffraction pattern produced on a screen placed at a distance $D$ from the slit system is _____.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For single-slit diffraction, the $n^{\text{th}}$ minimum occurs at</p>$$a\sin\theta = n\lambda \implies y_n = \frac{n\lambda D}{a}\quad(\text{small angles}).$$<p>Positions of the $1^{\text{st}}$ and $3^{\text{rd}}$ minima:</p>$$y_1 = \frac{\lambda D}{a},\qquad y_3 = \frac{3\lambda D}{a}.$$<p>Linear separation:</p>$$\Delta y = y_3 - y_1 = \frac{3\lambda D}{a} - \frac{\lambda D}{a} = \frac{2D\lambda}{a}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($2\frac{D\lambda}{a}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 1

A telescope with objective diameter $R$ is used to observe a distant star emitting light of wavelength 500 nm, at a resolution of $5 \times 10^{-7}$ radian. The value of $R$ is _____ cm.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The angular resolution (limit) of a telescope is</p>$$\theta = \frac{1.22\,\lambda}{R} \implies R = \frac{1.22\,\lambda}{\theta}.$$<p>Substitute $\lambda = 500\times10^{-9}\ \text{m}$ and $\theta = 5\times10^{-7}\ \text{rad}$:</p>$$R = \frac{1.22 \times 500\times10^{-9}}{5\times10^{-7}} = \frac{6.1\times10^{-7}}{5\times10^{-7}} = 1.22\ \text{m}.$$

$$R = 1.22\ \text{m} = 122\ \text{cm}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B (122)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 6, Shift 1

In an interference experiment the path difference between two interfering waves at a point $A$ on the screen is $\lambda/3$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of these waves, and at another point $B$ the path difference is $\lambda/6$. The ratio of intensities at points $A$ and $B$ is ________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Phase difference from path difference: $\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\Delta x$, and for two equal-amplitude waves</p>$$I = 4I_0\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right).$$<p>At $A$: $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda}{3} \Rightarrow \phi_A = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} \Rightarrow \dfrac{\phi_A}{2} = 60^\circ$</p>$$I_A = 4I_0\cos^2 60^\circ = 4I_0 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4} = I_0.$$<p>At $B$: $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda}{6} \Rightarrow \phi_B = \dfrac{\pi}{3} \Rightarrow \dfrac{\phi_B}{2} = 30^\circ$</p>$$I_B = 4I_0\cos^2 30^\circ = 4I_0 \cdot \tfrac{3}{4} = 3I_0.$$<p>Ratio:</p>$$\frac{I_A}{I_B} = \frac{I_0}{3I_0} = \frac{1}{3}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($1/3$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 6, Shift 1

An unpolarized light of intensity $I_0$ passes through a polarizer and then through a certain optically active solution and finally through an analyser. If the angle between analyser and polariser is $0^\circ$ and the intensity of light emerged from the analyser is $\dfrac{3}{8}I_0$, the angle of rotation of the light by the solution with respect to the analyser is ________ degrees.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Unpolarized light through the polarizer:</p>$$I_1 = \frac{I_0}{2}.$$<p>The optically active solution rotates the plane of polarization by angle $\theta$. Since the analyser is parallel to the polarizer ($0^\circ$), the angle between the (rotated) polarization plane and the analyser axis is $\theta$. By Malus’s law:</p>$$I = I_1\cos^2\theta = \frac{I_0}{2}\cos^2\theta.$$<p>Set equal to the given output:</p>$$\frac{I_0}{2}\cos^2\theta = \frac{3}{8}I_0 \implies \cos^2\theta = \frac{3}{4} \implies \cos\theta = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}.$$

$$\theta = 30^\circ.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 30</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 1

For a thin symmetric prism made of glass (refractive index 1.5), the ratio of the incident angle and the minimum deviation will be __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For a thin prism, the minimum deviation is</p>$$\delta_m = (\mu - 1)A = (1.5 - 1)A = 0.5\,A.$$<p>At minimum deviation the ray passes symmetrically, so the incident angle is</p>$$i = \frac{A + \delta_m}{2} = \frac{A + 0.5A}{2} = 0.75\,A.$$<p>Therefore the required ratio:</p>$$\frac{i}{\delta_m} = \frac{0.75A}{0.5A} = \frac{3}{2}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($3 : 2$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 1

In a single slit diffraction pattern, the wavelength of light used is 628 nm and the slit width is 0.2 mm. The angular width of the central maximum is $\alpha \times 10^{-2}$ degrees. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The full angular width of the central maximum is</p>$$\Delta\theta = \frac{2\lambda}{a}.$$<p>Substitute $\lambda = 628\times10^{-9}\ \text{m}$ and $a = 0.2\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}$:</p>$$\Delta\theta = \frac{2 \times 628\times10^{-9}}{0.2\times10^{-3}} = 6.28\times10^{-3}\ \text{rad}.$$<p>Convert to degrees:</p>$$\Delta\theta = 6.28\times10^{-3} \times \frac{180}{\pi} \approx 0.36^\circ = 36 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{degrees}.$$

$$\alpha = 36.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 36</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 2

A convex lens is made from glass material having refractive index 1.4 with the same radius of curvature on both sides. The ratio of its focal length to radius of curvature is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Equiconvex lens: $R_1 = +R$, $R_2 = -R$. Lens maker’s formula:</p>$$\frac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right) = (\mu - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R} + \frac{1}{R}\right) = \frac{2(\mu - 1)}{R}.$$<p>Hence</p>$$\frac{f}{R} = \frac{1}{2(\mu - 1)} = \frac{1}{2(1.4 - 1)} = \frac{1}{0.8} = 1.25.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D (1.25)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 2

An unpolarized light of certain intensity passes through a combination of two polarizers whose transmission axes are at 30° and 90°, respectively, with respect to the horizontal axis. A third polarizer with its transmission axis at 60° with the horizontal axis is placed between the two existing polarizers. The ratio of the output intensities with and without the third polarizer is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Let the intensity after the first polarizer (at $30^\circ$) be $I_1 = \dfrac{I}{2}$.</p><p><strong>Without the third polarizer</strong> (axes $30^\circ \to 90^\circ$, i.e. $60^\circ$ apart):</p>$$I_{\text{out}} = I_1\cos^2 60^\circ = \frac{I}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{I}{8}.$$<p><strong>With the third polarizer at $60^\circ$</strong> ($30^\circ \to 60^\circ \to 90^\circ$, each step $30^\circ$):</p>$$I_{\text{out}}' = I_1\cos^2 30^\circ \cos^2 30^\circ = \frac{I}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9I}{32}.$$<p>Ratio (with $:$ without):</p>$$\frac{I_{\text{out}}'}{I_{\text{out}}} = \frac{9I/32}{I/8} = \frac{9}{32}\times 8 = \frac{9}{4}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C (9/4)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 2

In a double slit experiment, when one of the slits is covered by a transparent mica sheet of refractive index 1.56, the central fringe shifts to the position of the $7^{th}$ bright fringe obtained with both slits uncovered. If the light source wavelength is 450 nm, the thickness of the mica sheet is $\alpha \times 10^{-9}$ m. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Introducing a sheet of refractive index $\mu$ and thickness $t$ adds an extra path $(\mu - 1)t$. The central fringe shifts by $n$ fringes when</p>$$(\mu - 1)t = n\lambda.$$<p>Here $n = 7$, $\mu = 1.56$, $\lambda = 450\times10^{-9}\ \text{m}$:</p>$$t = \frac{n\lambda}{\mu - 1} = \frac{7 \times 450\times10^{-9}}{1.56 - 1} = \frac{3150\times10^{-9}}{0.56}.$$

$$t = 5625\times10^{-9}\ \text{m}.$$

$$\alpha = 5625.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 5625</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 2

In a Young’s double slit experiment, the intensity at some point on the screen is found to be $\dfrac{3}{4}$ times the maximum of the interference pattern. The path difference between the interfering waves at this point is $\dfrac{\lambda}{x}$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the incident light. The value of $x$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For two equal sources, $I = I_{\max}\cos^2\!\left(\dfrac{\phi}{2}\right)$. Given $I = \dfrac{3}{4}I_{\max}$:</p>$$\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right) = \frac{3}{4} \implies \cos\frac{\phi}{2} = \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \implies \frac{\phi}{2} = 30^\circ \implies \phi = 60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}.$$<p>Path difference:</p>$$\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,\phi = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\lambda}{6}.$$<p>Comparing with $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda}{x}$ gives $x = 6$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 6</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 2

If sunlight is focused on a paper using a convex lens, it starts burning the paper in the shortest time when the lens is kept at 30 cm above the paper. If the radius of curvature of the lens is 60 cm, then the refractive index of the lens material is $\dfrac{\alpha}{10}$. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Sunlight is effectively parallel, so it converges at the focal point. The shortest burning time occurs when the paper is at the focus:</p>$$f = 30\ \text{cm}.$$<p>For an equiconvex lens with both surfaces of radius $R = 60\ \text{cm}$:</p>$$\frac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R} + \frac{1}{R}\right) = \frac{2(\mu - 1)}{R}.$$<p>Substitute $f = 30$, $R = 60$:</p>$$\frac{1}{30} = \frac{2(\mu - 1)}{60} = \frac{\mu - 1}{30} \implies \mu - 1 = 1 \implies \mu = 2.$$<p>So $\dfrac{\alpha}{10} = 2 \implies \alpha = 20$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 20</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 6, Shift 2

The angle of minimum deviation is equal to half of the angle of the prism in an equilateral prism. The refractive index of the prism is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For an equilateral prism, $A = 60^\circ$ and given $\delta_m = \dfrac{A}{2} = 30^\circ$.</p><p>Prism formula:</p>$$\mu = \frac{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{A + \delta_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)} = \frac{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{60^\circ + 30^\circ}{2}\right)}{\sin 30^\circ} = \frac{\sin 45^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ}.$$

$$\mu = \frac{1/\sqrt2}{1/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt2} = \sqrt2 \approx 1.414.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($\sqrt{2}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 6, Shift 2

A concave mirror of focal length 10 cm forms an image which is double the size of the object when the object is placed at two different positions. The distance between the two positions of the object is __________ cm.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>A concave mirror can give a magnified image of magnitude $2$ in two ways: a real inverted image ($m = -2$) and a virtual erect image ($m = +2$).</p><p>Using $m = \dfrac{f}{f - u}$ (with $f = -10\ \text{cm}$, distances measured with sign):</p><p><strong>Real image</strong> $m = -2$: $\;-2 = \dfrac{-10}{-10 - u} \Rightarrow -10 - u = 5 \Rightarrow u = -15\ \text{cm}$ (object at 15 cm).</p><p><strong>Virtual image</strong> $m = +2$: $\;+2 = \dfrac{-10}{-10 - u} \Rightarrow -10 - u = -5 \Rightarrow u = -5\ \text{cm}$ (object at 5 cm).</p><p>Distance between the two object positions:</p>$$|{-15}| - |{-5}| = 15 - 5 = 10\ \text{cm}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 10</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 1

In Young’s double slit experiment, the fringe width of the interference pattern produced on the screen is 2.4 µm. If the experiment is carried out in another medium having refractive index 1.2, the fringe width will be ______ µm.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Fringe width $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$. In a medium the wavelength reduces to $\lambda' = \dfrac{\lambda}{\mu}$, so</p>$$\beta' = \frac{\beta}{\mu} = \frac{2.4\ \mu\text{m}}{1.2} = 2\ \mu\text{m}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($2$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 1

A ray of light passing through an equilateral prism has a velocity $2.12 \times 10^8$ m/s in the prism material. The minimum angle of deviation is ________ degrees.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Refractive index of the prism material:</p>$$\mu = \frac{c}{v} = \frac{3\times10^8}{2.12\times10^8} \approx 1.414 = \sqrt2.$$<p>For an equilateral prism $A = 60^\circ$:</p>$$\mu = \frac{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{A + \delta_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)} \implies \sin\!\left(\frac{60^\circ + \delta_m}{2}\right) = \mu\sin 30^\circ = \sqrt2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}.$$

$$\frac{60^\circ + \delta_m}{2} = 45^\circ \implies 60^\circ + \delta_m = 90^\circ \implies \delta_m = 30^\circ.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($30$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 1

A compound microscope is designed with two symmetric biconvex lenses. The objective lens is cut vertically, creating two identical plano-convex lenses. One of them is used in place of the original objective lens. To retain the same magnification keeping the object distance unchanged, the tube length has to be

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For a symmetric biconvex lens (both radii $R$):</p>$$\frac{1}{f_{\text{bi}}} = (\mu - 1)\frac{2}{R}.$$<p>Cutting it vertically gives a plano-convex lens (one flat face, $R \to \infty$):</p>$$\frac{1}{f_{\text{pc}}} = (\mu - 1)\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{2f_{\text{bi}}} \implies f_{\text{pc}} = 2f_{\text{bi}}.$$<p>The objective’s linear magnification is $m_o \approx \dfrac{L}{f_o}$, where $L$ is the tube length. To keep $m_o$ (hence the overall magnification) unchanged with the object distance fixed, when $f_o$ doubles the tube length must also double:</p>$$\frac{L'}{f_{\text{pc}}} = \frac{L}{f_{\text{bi}}} \implies L' = \frac{f_{\text{pc}}}{f_{\text{bi}}}L = 2L.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (increased two times)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 2

A thin convex lens and a thin concave lens are kept in contact and are co-axial. Which of the following statements is correct for this combination of two lenses?

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For lenses in contact, powers add:</p>$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{f_{\text{convex}}} - \frac{1}{|f_{\text{concave}}|}.$$<p>The combination behaves as a <strong>concave</strong> lens when $\dfrac{1}{f} &lt; 0$, i.e.</p>$$\frac{1}{f_{\text{convex}}} &lt; \frac{1}{|f_{\text{concave}}|} \implies |f_{\text{convex}}| &gt; |f_{\text{concave}}|.$$<p>(Interchanging the order of two thin lenses in contact does not change the combined focal length, so that option is incorrect.)</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (behaves as concave lens if $|f_{convex}| &gt; |f_{concave}|$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 2

An object $AB$ is placed 15 cm to the left of a convex lens $P$ of focal length 10 cm. Another convex lens $Q$ is now placed 15 cm to the right of lens $P$. If the focal length of lens $Q$ is 15 cm, the final image is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Lens $P$</strong> ($f = +10$, $u = -15$):</p>$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{15} = \frac{1}{30} \implies v = +30\ \text{cm}.$$<p>Image forms 30 cm to the right of $P$; $m_P = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{30}{-15} = -2$.</p><p><strong>Lens $Q$</strong> is 15 cm right of $P$, so this image lies $30 - 15 = 15\ \text{cm}$ to the right of $Q$ — a virtual object, $u = +15$, $f = +15$:</p>$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{15} = \frac{2}{15} \implies v = +7.5\ \text{cm}.$$<p>Real image, 7.5 cm right of $Q$; $m_Q = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{7.5}{15} = +0.5$.</p><p>Total magnification:</p>$$m = m_P \cdot m_Q = (-2)(0.5) = -1.$$<p>Image is <strong>real, 7.5 cm right of $Q$, same size as $AB$</strong> (inverted).</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B (real, formed at 7.5 cm right of lens $Q$, with a size same as that of $AB$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 2

The maximum intensity in a Young’s double slit experiment is $I_0$. The distance between the slits ($d$) is $5\lambda$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light used. The intensity of the fringe exactly opposite to one of the slits on a screen placed at $D = 10d$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>A point exactly opposite one slit is at $y = \dfrac{d}{2}$ from the centre. Path difference:</p>$$\Delta x = \frac{d\,y}{D} = \frac{d\cdot (d/2)}{10d} = \frac{d}{20} = \frac{5\lambda}{20} = \frac{\lambda}{4}.$$<p>Phase difference:</p>$$\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$<p>Intensity:</p>$$I = I_0\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right) = I_0\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = I_0\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{I_0}{2}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($\dfrac{I_0}{2}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 8, Shift 2

A thin biconvex lens is prepared from glass ($\mu = 1.5$), both curved surfaces of which have equal radii of 20 cm each. The left side surface of the lens is silvered from outside to make it reflecting. To have the position of image and object at the same place, the object should be placed, from the lens, at a distance of __________ cm.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>A silvered lens behaves as an equivalent mirror. Light passes through the lens, reflects off the silvered surface, and passes through the lens again, so</p>$$P_{\text{eq}} = 2P_{\text{lens}} + P_{\text{mirror}}.$$<p><strong>Lens power</strong> (biconvex, $R = 20$):</p>$$P_{\text{lens}} = (\mu-1)\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{1}{R}\right) = 0.5\cdot\frac{2}{20} = \frac{1}{20}\ \text{cm}^{-1}.$$<p><strong>Mirror power</strong> (silvered surface, $R = 20$, so $f_m = R/2 = 10$):</p>$$P_{\text{mirror}} = \frac{1}{f_m} = \frac{2}{R} = \frac{1}{10}\ \text{cm}^{-1}.$$<p>Equivalent power and focal length:</p>$$P_{\text{eq}} = 2\cdot\frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{1}{10}+\frac{1}{10} = \frac{1}{5} \implies f_{\text{eq}} = 5\ \text{cm}.$$<p>For a mirror, object and image coincide when the object is at the centre of curvature, i.e. at $2f_{\text{eq}}$:</p>$$u = 2f_{\text{eq}} = 2\times 5 = 10\ \text{cm}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (10)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 8, Shift 2

A distant star is to be observed by a telescope of objective lens diameter $a$, at an angular resolution of $3.0 \times 10^{-7}$ radian. If the wavelength of light from the star reaching the telescope is 500 nm, the minimum diameter of the objective lens of the telescope is __________ cm. (nearest integer)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The angular resolution limit of a telescope is</p>$$\theta = \frac{1.22\,\lambda}{a} \implies a = \frac{1.22\,\lambda}{\theta}.$$<p>Substitute $\lambda = 500\times10^{-9}\ \text{m}$ and $\theta = 3.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{rad}$:</p>$$a = \frac{1.22 \times 500\times10^{-9}}{3.0\times10^{-7}} = \frac{6.1\times10^{-7}}{3.0\times10^{-7}} \approx 2.033\ \text{m}.$$

$$a \approx 203.3\ \text{cm} \approx 203\ \text{cm}.$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 203</p></div></details>
