PYQ · Source verification pending

The time taken by a block of mass $m$ to slide down from the highest point to the lowest point on a rough inclined plane is 50 % more compared to the time taken by the same block on identical inclined smooth plane. Both inclined planes are at $45^\circ$ with the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the rough inclined surface and block is _____.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Both planes cover the same slope length $L$ starting from rest, so $L = \tfrac{1}{2}a t^2$, which gives $t \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a}}$.</p><p><strong>Accelerations</strong> ($\theta = 45^\circ$, so $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$):</p>$$a_{\text{smooth}} = g\sin\theta, \qquad a_{\text{rough}} = g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta$$<p><strong>Time ratio</strong> (rough time is 50 % more, i.e. $t_{\text{rough}} = 1.5\,t_{\text{smooth}}$):</p>$$\frac{t_{\text{rough}}}{t_{\text{smooth}}} = \sqrt{\frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}}} = 1.5 \implies \frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}} = \frac{9}{4}$$<p><strong>Solve for $\mu$</strong> (dividing by $g\cos\theta$ with $\tan 45^\circ = 1$):</p>$$\frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta} = \frac{1}{1-\mu} = \frac{9}{4}$$<p></p>$$1 - \mu = \frac{4}{9} \implies \mu = \frac{5}{9}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> μ = 5/9</p></div></details>

PYQ · Source verification pending

At $t = 0$, a body of mass 100 g starts moving under the influence of a force $\left(5\hat{i} + 10\hat{j}\right)\,\mathrm{N}$. After 2 s its position is $\left(2x\hat{i} + 5y\hat{j}\right)\,\mathrm{m}$. The ratio $x : y$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Mass $m = 100\ \text{g} = 0.1\ \text{kg}$. The body starts from rest at the origin.</p><p><strong>Acceleration</strong> from $\vec{a} = \vec{F}/m$:</p>$$\vec{a} = \frac{5\hat{i} + 10\hat{j}}{0.1} = \left(50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}\right)\ \text{m/s}^2$$<p><strong>Displacement</strong> after $t = 2$ s, $\vec{s} = \tfrac{1}{2}\vec{a}\,t^2 = 2\vec{a}$:</p>$$\vec{s} = 2\left(50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}\right) = \left(100\hat{i} + 200\hat{j}\right)\ \text{m}$$<p><strong>Match components</strong> with $\left(2x\hat{i} + 5y\hat{j}\right)$:</p>$$2x = 100 \implies x = 50, \qquad 5y = 200 \implies y = 40$$

$$x : y = 50 : 40 = 5 : 4$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 5 : 4</p></div></details>

PYQ · Source verification pending

A block takes $t$ time to slide down a plane inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. If the surface is made smooth (frictionless), the block takes time $\dfrac{t}{2}$ to slide down the plane. The coefficient of friction between the block and the inclined plane is $\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{100}\right)$. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Same slope length $L$ from rest, so $t \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a}}$.</p><p><strong>Accelerations</strong> ($\theta = 45^\circ$):</p>$$a_{\text{rough}} = g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta, \qquad a_{\text{smooth}} = g\sin\theta$$<p><strong>Time ratio</strong> ($t_{\text{smooth}} = t/2$, so rough time is twice the smooth time):</p>$$\frac{t_{\text{rough}}}{t_{\text{smooth}}} = \sqrt{\frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}}} = 2 \implies \frac{a_{\text{smooth}}}{a_{\text{rough}}} = 4$$<p><strong>Solve for $\mu$</strong> (with $\tan 45^\circ = 1$):</p>$$\frac{1}{1-\mu} = 4 \implies 1 - \mu = \frac{1}{4} \implies \mu = \frac{3}{4} = 0.75 = \frac{75}{100}$$<p>Hence $\alpha = 75$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 75</p></div></details>

A car moving with a speed of 54 km/h takes a turn of radius 20 m. A simple pendulum is suspended from the ceiling of the car. Determine the angle made by the string of the pendulum with the vertical during the turning. (Take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Convert the speed: $v = 54\ \text{km/h} = 54 \times \dfrac{5}{18} = 15\ \text{m/s}$.</p><p>In the car’s (non-inertial) frame, the bob experiences a pseudo (centrifugal) force $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ horizontally outward and gravity $mg$ downward. At equilibrium the string tilts by angle $\theta$ from the vertical:</p>$$\tan\theta = \frac{mv^2/r}{mg} = \frac{v^2}{rg}$$<p><strong>Substitute values:</strong></p>$$\tan\theta = \frac{(15)^2}{20 \times 10} = \frac{225}{200} = 1.125$$

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.125)$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> θ = tan⁻¹(1.125)</p></div></details>

## JEE Main-style practice questions (original)

These are original practice questions written by JEE Notes, not from any past paper. Take g = 10 m/s² throughout.

**P1.** A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal floor (μs = 0.4, μk = 0.3). A horizontal force of 15 N acts on it. What is the friction force?

(A) 20 N (B) 15 N (C) 12 N (D) 0

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The maximum static friction is μs·mg = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N. Since 15 N &lt; 20 N, the block stays at rest and static friction balances the applied force: f = 15 N.</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P2.** The same block is now pushed with 30 N. Find its acceleration.

(A) 2 m/s² (B) 3 m/s² (C) 4 m/s² (D) 6 m/s²

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>30 N &gt; 20 N, so the block slides and kinetic friction applies: f = 0.3 × 50 = 15 N. Then a = (30 − 15)/5 = 3 m/s².</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P3.** Masses of 3 kg and 2 kg hang over an ideal (light, frictionless) pulley. Find the acceleration and the string tension.

(A) 2 m/s², 24 N (B) 2 m/s², 20 N (C) 4 m/s², 24 N (D) 2 m/s², 30 N

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂) = 10/5 = 2 m/s². For the 2 kg mass, T − 20 = 2 × 2, so T = 24 N. Check with the 3 kg mass: 30 − T = 3 × 2, which also gives T = 24 N.</p><p><strong>Answer (A).</strong></p></div></details>

**P4.** A 4 kg block on a smooth table is tied by a string over a light pulley at the table’s edge to a hanging 1 kg mass. Find the tension.

(A) 10 N (B) 8 N (C) 2 N (D) 5 N

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂) = 10/5 = 2 m/s². For the block on the table, T = 4 × 2 = 8 N. Check with the hanging mass: 10 − 8 = 1 × 2.</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P5.** A block slides down a 30° incline with μk = 1/(2√3). Find its acceleration.

(A) 5 m/s² (B) 2.5 m/s² (C) 3.75 m/s² (D) 1.25 m/s²

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>a = g(sinθ − μcosθ) = 10(½ − (1/(2√3))(√3/2)) = 10(0.5 − 0.25) = 2.5 m/s².</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P6.** What is the minimum coefficient of static friction needed to keep a block at rest on a 37° incline? (Use sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8.)

(A) 0.6 (B) 0.8 (C) 0.75 (D) 1.33

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>At the limit, mg sinθ = μ mg cosθ, so μ = tanθ = 0.6/0.8 = 0.75.</p><p><strong>Answer (C).</strong></p></div></details>

**P7.** A 60 kg person stands on a scale in a lift accelerating upward at 2 m/s². What does the scale read?

(A) 480 N (B) 600 N (C) 720 N (D) 120 N

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>N − mg = ma, so N = 60(10 + 2) = 720 N.</p><p><strong>Answer (C).</strong></p></div></details>

**P8.** A small block is pressed against the vertical front face of a cart, on the outside. The cart accelerates forward (horizontally) and pushes the block ahead of it. μs between the block and the face is 0.5. What minimum acceleration keeps the block from sliding down?

(A) 5 m/s² (B) 10 m/s² (C) 20 m/s² (D) 15 m/s²

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The face pushes the block forward, giving a normal force N horizontally. The block moves with the cart, so N = ma (ground frame). Equivalently, in the cart’s frame the backward pseudo force ma presses the block onto the face. Static friction acts upward and must balance gravity: f ≤ μN, so mg ≤ μma and a ≥ g/μ = 10/0.5 = 20 m/s². Check: at a = 20 m/s², N = 20m and μN = 10m = mg, which is exactly the limit.</p><p><strong>Answer (C).</strong></p></div></details>

**P9.** A bob hangs from the roof of a train accelerating horizontally at 7.5 m/s². What angle does the string make with the vertical? (Use tan37° = 0.75.)

(A) 30° (B) 37° (C) 45° (D) 53°

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>In the train’s frame, tanθ = a/g = 0.75, so θ = 37°.</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P10.** A car takes a flat (unbanked) turn of radius 50 m with μs = 0.2. What is its maximum safe speed?

(A) 10 m/s (B) 20 m/s (C) 5 m/s (D) 14 m/s

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Friction supplies the centripetal force: mv²/r ≤ μmg, so v ≤ √(μrg) = √(0.2 × 50 × 10) = √100 = 10 m/s (36 km/h).</p><p><strong>Answer (A).</strong></p></div></details>

**P11.** A coin sits 10 cm from the centre of a horizontal turntable, with μs = 0.4. What is the maximum angular speed before it slips?

(A) 2 rad/s (B) 2√10 rad/s (C) 4 rad/s (D) 20 rad/s

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>mω²r ≤ μmg, so ω ≤ √(μg/r) = √(4/0.1) = √40 = 2√10 ≈ 6.32 rad/s.</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P12.** Blocks of 2 kg and 3 kg touch each other on a smooth floor. A 20 N horizontal force pushes the 2 kg block. What force does the 2 kg block exert on the 3 kg block?

(A) 8 N (B) 20 N (C) 12 N (D) 4 N

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>a = 20/5 = 4 m/s². For the 3 kg block alone, N = 3 × 4 = 12 N.</p><p><strong>Answer (C).</strong></p></div></details>

**P13.** A 3 kg box lies on a 9 kg plank, and the plank rests on smooth (frictionless) ice. μs between the box and the plank is 0.3. A horizontal rope pulls the box, not the plank. What is the largest pull for which the box and the plank move together without slipping?

(A) 9 N (B) 12 N (C) 27 N (D) 36 N

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Now the plank is accelerated only by friction from the box, so the limit is set by the plank, not the box. Maximum friction is μs·m(box)·g = 0.3 × 30 = 9 N, so the plank’s maximum acceleration is 9/9 = 1 m/s². Moving together, F = (3 + 9) × 1 = 12 N. Check with the box: F − 9 = 3 × 1, so F = 12 N.</p><p><strong>Answer (B).</strong></p></div></details>

**P14.** A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds along the same line at 8 m/s. Contact lasts 0.02 s. What is the average force from the wall?

(A) 50 N (B) 100 N (C) 450 N (D) 900 N

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The change in momentum is 0.5 × (8 − (−10)) = 9 kg·m/s, so F = 9/0.02 = 450 N.</p><p><strong>Answer (C).</strong></p></div></details>

**P15.** At what banking angle can a car take a 40 m radius turn at 20 m/s with no friction needed?

(A) 30° (B) 37° (C) 45° (D) 60°

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>tanθ = v²/(rg) = 400/400 = 1, so θ = 45°.</p><p><strong>Answer (C).</strong></p></div></details>
