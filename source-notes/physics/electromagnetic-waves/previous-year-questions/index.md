PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

A point light source emits E.M. waves in free space. A detector, placed at a distance of $L$ m, measures the intensity as $I_0$. The detector is now shifted to another location on the same spherical surface ensuring the angle between original location and new location as $45^\circ$. The measured intensity at new location will be ________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For an isotropic point source, the emitted power $P$ spreads uniformly over a sphere, so the intensity depends only on the distance $r$ from the source:</p>$$I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$$<p>Both the original and the new detector positions lie on the <strong>same spherical surface</strong>, meaning they are at the same distance $r = L$ from the source. The angular separation of $45^\circ$ only moves the detector along the sphere and does not change $r$.</p>$$I_{\text{new}} = \frac{P}{4\pi L^2} = I_0$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $I_0$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

An electromagnetic wave travelling in $x$-direction is described by field equation $E_y = 300\sin\omega\left(t - \dfrac{x}{c}\right)$. If the electron is restricted to move in $y$-direction only with speed of $1.5 \times 10^6$ m/s then ratio of maximum electric and magnetic forces acting on the electron is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The peak electric field is $E_0 = 300\ \text{V/m}$, and the peak magnetic field of the wave is:</p>$$B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}$$<p><strong>Maximum electric force</strong> on the electron (charge $q$):</p>$$F_E = qE_0$$<p><strong>Maximum magnetic force</strong> on the electron moving with speed $v$:</p>$$F_B = qvB_0 = qv\cdot\frac{E_0}{c}$$<p>Taking the ratio, $E_0$ and $q$ cancel:</p>$$\frac{F_E}{F_B} = \frac{qE_0}{qv\,E_0/c} = \frac{c}{v} = \frac{3\times10^8}{1.5\times10^6} = 200$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 200</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

A magnetic field vector in an electromagnetic wave is represented by $\vec{B} = B_0 \sin\left(2\pi vt - \dfrac{2\pi x}{\lambda}\right)\hat{j}$. Its associated electric field vector is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The phase $\left(2\pi vt - \dfrac{2\pi x}{\lambda}\right)$ shows the wave propagates along $+x$, so the propagation direction is $\hat{i}$.</p><p>For an EM wave, $\vec{E}$, $\vec{B}$ and the propagation direction $\hat{n}$ satisfy $\hat{E}\times\hat{B} = \hat{n}$. With $\vec{B}\parallel\hat{j}$ and $\hat{n}=\hat{i}$, we need $\hat{E}$ such that:</p>$$\hat{E}\times\hat{j} = \hat{i}$$<p>Testing $\hat{E} = -\hat{k}$: $\;(-\hat{k})\times\hat{j} = -(\hat{k}\times\hat{j}) = -(-\hat{i}) = \hat{i}$ ✓</p><p>So the electric field points along $-\hat{k}$. Its magnitude uses $E_0 = cB_0$, and since $c = v\lambda$:</p>$$\vec{E} = -v\lambda B_0 \sin\left(2\pi vt - \frac{2\pi x}{\lambda}\right)\hat{k}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $\vec{E} = -v\lambda B_0 \sin\left(2\pi vt - \dfrac{2\pi x}{\lambda}\right)\hat{k}$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

An electromagnetic wave travels in free space along the $x$-direction. At a particular point in space and time, $\vec{B} = 2 \times 10^{-7}\,\hat{j}$ T is associated with this wave. The value of corresponding electric field $\vec{E}$ at this point is __________ V/m.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The magnitude of the electric field is:</p>$$E = cB = (3\times10^8)(2\times10^{-7}) = 60\ \text{V/m}$$<p>The propagation is along $+x$ ($\hat{i}$), and $\vec{B}\parallel\hat{j}$. Using $\hat{E}\times\hat{B} = \hat{n}$:</p>$$\hat{E}\times\hat{j} = \hat{i} \implies \hat{E} = -\hat{k}$$<p>since $(-\hat{k})\times\hat{j} = \hat{i}$.</p>$$\vec{E} = -60\,\hat{k}\ \text{V/m}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $-60\,\hat{k}$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

For an electromagnetic wave propagating through vacuum, $\vec{k}$, $\vec{E}$ and $\omega$ represent propagation vector, electric field and angular frequency, respectively. The magnetic field associated with this wave is represented by:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For a plane EM wave, the magnetic field is obtained from $\vec{B} = \dfrac{\hat{k}\times\vec{E}}{c}$, where $\hat{k}$ is the unit propagation vector.</p><p>Since $\hat{k} = \dfrac{\vec{k}}{k}$ and $c = \dfrac{\omega}{k}$:</p>$$\vec{B} = \frac{1}{c}\left(\frac{\vec{k}}{k}\right)\times\vec{E} = \frac{k}{\omega}\cdot\frac{1}{k}\,(\vec{k}\times\vec{E}) = \frac{\vec{k}\times\vec{E}}{\omega}$$<p>This correctly gives $\vec{B}$ perpendicular to both $\vec{k}$ and $\vec{E}$, with $\hat{E}\times\hat{B}$ pointing along $\vec{k}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> $\dfrac{\vec{k}\times\vec{E}}{\omega}$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

A displacement current of 4.0 A can be set up in the space between two parallel plates of 6 µF capacitor. The rate of change of potential difference across the plates of the capacitor is nearly $\alpha \times 10^6$ V/s. The value of $\alpha$ is ________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The displacement current equals the conduction current charging the capacitor:</p>$$I_d = C\,\frac{dV}{dt}$$<p>Solving for the rate of change of potential difference:</p>$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{I_d}{C} = \frac{4.0}{6\times10^{-6}} = 0.667\times10^{6}\ \text{V/s}$$<p>Comparing with $\alpha\times10^6$ V/s gives $\alpha \approx 0.67$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> $0.67$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

Given below are two statements: one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other is labelled as Reason (R).

**Assertion (A):** The electromagnetic wave exerts pressure on the surface on which they are allowed to fall.

**Reason (R):** There is no mass associated with the electromagnetic waves.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> EM waves carry linear momentum. When they fall on a surface, momentum is transferred, producing <strong>radiation pressure</strong>. For a fully absorbing surface, $P_{\text{rad}} = \dfrac{I}{c}$. So (A) is <strong>true</strong>.</p><p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> Although photons have zero <em>rest mass</em>, an EM wave still carries momentum $p = \dfrac{E}{c}$ and an associated effective (relativistic) mass $m = \dfrac{E}{c^2}$. It is precisely this momentum that causes the pressure. The blanket claim “there is no mass associated with EM waves” is misleading/incorrect and, moreover, does not explain why pressure arises. So (R) is <strong>false</strong>.</p><p>Therefore, (A) is true but (R) is false.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> (A) is true but (R) is false</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

A monochromatic source of light operating at 15 kW emits $2.5 \times 10^{22}$ photons/s. The region of an electromagnetic spectrum to which the emitted electromagnetic radiation belongs to __________. (Take $h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J.s and $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s).

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Energy per photon</strong> = total power ÷ number of photons per second:</p>$$E = \frac{P}{N} = \frac{15\times10^{3}}{2.5\times10^{22}} = 6\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$<p><strong>Wavelength</strong> from $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$:</p>$$\lambda = \frac{hc}{E} = \frac{(6.6\times10^{-34})(3\times10^{8})}{6\times10^{-19}} = 3.3\times10^{-7}\ \text{m} = 330\ \text{nm}$$<p>The visible range is roughly $400$–$700$ nm. Since $330\ \text{nm} &lt; 400\ \text{nm}$, this radiation lies in the <strong>ultraviolet</strong> region.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> Ultraviolet</p></div></details>
