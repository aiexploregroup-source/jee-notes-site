PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

An insulated wire is wound so that it forms a flat coil with $N = 200$ turns. The radius of the innermost turn is $r_1 = 3$ cm, and of the outermost turn $r_2 = 6$ cm. If 20 mA current flows in it then the magnetic moment will be $\alpha \times 10^{-2}\ \text{A.m}^2$. The value of $\alpha$ is _____.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Each turn contributes $I\pi r^2$ to the magnetic moment. The radii vary uniformly from $r_1$ to $r_2$ across the $N$ turns, so the total moment is</p>$$M = NI\pi \langle r^2\rangle = NI\pi\cdot\frac{r_1^2 + r_1 r_2 + r_2^2}{3}$$<p>Substituting $N = 200$, $I = 20\times10^{-3}$ A, $r_1 = 0.03$ m, $r_2 = 0.06$ m:</p>$$r_1^2 + r_1 r_2 + r_2^2 = 0.0009 + 0.0018 + 0.0036 = 0.0063\ \text{m}^2$$

$$M = 200 \times 0.02 \times \pi \times \frac{0.0063}{3} = 4\pi \times 0.0021 = 2.64\times10^{-2}\ \text{A.m}^2$$<p>Hence $\alpha = 2.64$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($2.64$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

A small cube of side 1 mm is placed at the centre of a circular loop of radius 10 cm carrying a current of 2 A. The magnetic energy stored inside the cube is $\alpha \times 10^{-14}$ J. The value of $\alpha$ is ________. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ Tm/A, $\pi = 3.14$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Magnetic field at the centre of the loop:</p>$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} = \frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times 2}{2\times 0.10} = 4\pi\times10^{-6} = 1.256\times10^{-5}\ \text{T}$$<p>Magnetic energy density:</p>$$u = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} = \frac{(4\pi\times10^{-6})^2}{2\times 4\pi\times10^{-7}} = \frac{16\pi^2\times10^{-12}}{8\pi\times10^{-7}} = 2\pi\times10^{-5}\ \text{J/m}^3$$<p>Volume of the cube: $V = (10^{-3})^3 = 10^{-9}\ \text{m}^3$. Energy stored:</p>$$U = uV = 2\pi\times10^{-5}\times10^{-9} = 2\pi\times10^{-14} = 6.28\times10^{-14}\ \text{J}$$<p>Hence $\alpha = 6.28$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A ($6.28$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

1 µC charge moving with velocity $\vec{v} = \left(\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}\right)$ m/s in the region of magnetic field $\vec{B} = \left(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}\right)$ T. The magnitude of force acting on it is $\sqrt{\alpha} \times 10^{-6}$ N. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The magnetic force is $\vec{F} = q\,\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$. Compute the cross product:</p>$$\vec{v}\times\vec{B} = \begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} &amp; \hat{j} &amp; \hat{k}\\ 1 &amp; -2 &amp; 3\\ 2 &amp; 3 &amp; -5\end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \hat{i}\big[(-2)(-5) - (3)(3)\big] - \hat{j}\big[(1)(-5) - (3)(2)\big] + \hat{k}\big[(1)(3) - (-2)(2)\big]$$

$$= \hat{i}(10 - 9) - \hat{j}(-5 - 6) + \hat{k}(3 + 4) = \hat{i} + 11\hat{j} + 7\hat{k}$$<p>Magnitude:</p>$$|\vec{v}\times\vec{B}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 11^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{1 + 121 + 49} = \sqrt{171}$$<p>So $\vec{F} = 10^{-6}\sqrt{171}\ \text{N}$, giving $\sqrt{\alpha} = \sqrt{171}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 171</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

A solenoid has a core made of material with relative permeability 400. The magnetic field produced in the interior of solenoid is 1.0 T. The magnetic intensity in SI units is $\alpha \times 10^5$. The value of $\alpha$ is __________. (Free space permeability $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ SI units.)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The magnetic field inside a medium relates to the magnetic intensity by</p>$$B = \mu_0 \mu_r H \quad\Rightarrow\quad H = \frac{B}{\mu_0 \mu_r}$$<p>Substituting $B = 1.0$ T, $\mu_r = 400$, $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$:</p>$$H = \frac{1.0}{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times 400} = \frac{1}{1600\pi\times10^{-7}} = \frac{10^7}{1600\pi}\ \text{A/m}$$

$$H = \frac{10^5}{16\pi} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\times10^5\ \text{A/m}$$<p>Hence $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{16\pi}$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($\dfrac{1}{16\pi}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

A circular coil of radius 2 cm and 125 turns carries a current of 1 A. The coil is placed in a uniform magnetic field of magnitude 0.4 T. The axis of the coil makes an angle of 30° with the direction of the magnetic field. The torque acting on the coil is $\alpha \times 10^{-4}$ N.m. The value of $\alpha$ is __________. ($\pi = 3.14$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The magnetic moment of the coil is</p>$$m = NIA = NI\pi r^2 = 125 \times 1 \times \pi \times (0.02)^2 = 125\pi\times 4\times10^{-4}$$

$$m = 0.05\pi = 0.157\ \text{A.m}^2$$<p>The angle between the coil’s axis (direction of $\vec{m}$) and $\vec{B}$ is $30°$, so the torque is</p>$$\tau = mB\sin\theta = 0.157 \times 0.4 \times \sin 30° = 0.157 \times 0.4 \times 0.5$$

$$\tau = 0.0314\ \text{N.m} = 314\times10^{-4}\ \text{N.m}$$<p>Hence $\alpha = 314$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 314</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

A particle having charge $10^{-9}$ C moving in $x$-$y$ plane in fields of $0.4\,\hat{j}$ N/C and $4 \times 10^{-3}\,\hat{k}$ T experiences a force of $\left(4\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}\right) \times 10^{-10}$ N. The velocity of the particle at that instant is __________ m/s.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The Lorentz force is $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v}\times\vec{B})$ with $\vec{v} = v_x\hat{i} + v_y\hat{j}$, $\vec{E} = 0.4\hat{j}$, $\vec{B} = 4\times10^{-3}\hat{k}$.</p>$$\vec{v}\times\vec{B} = (v_x\hat{i} + v_y\hat{j})\times(4\times10^{-3}\hat{k}) = v_y(4\times10^{-3})\hat{i} - v_x(4\times10^{-3})\hat{j}$$<p>Equate components of $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v}\times\vec{B})$ with $q = 10^{-9}$:</p>$$F_x = q\,v_y(4\times10^{-3}) = 4\times10^{-10} \Rightarrow v_y = \frac{4\times10^{-10}}{10^{-9}\times4\times10^{-3}} = 100$$

$$F_y = q\big(0.4 - v_x(4\times10^{-3})\big) = 2\times10^{-10}$$

$$0.4 - v_x(4\times10^{-3}) = \frac{2\times10^{-10}}{10^{-9}} = 0.2 \Rightarrow v_x(4\times10^{-3}) = 0.2 \Rightarrow v_x = 50$$<p>So $\vec{v} = 50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$ m/s.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A ($50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

A current of 30 A each flows in opposite directions in two conducting wires, placed parallel to each other at a distance of 8 cm. The magnetic field at the mid point between the two wires is __________ $\mu$T. $\left(\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}=10^{-7}\text{ N/A}^2\right)$

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The midpoint is at distance $r = 4$ cm $= 0.04$ m from each wire. Field due to one long straight wire:</p>$$B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{2I}{r} = 10^{-7}\times\frac{2\times 30}{0.04} = 10^{-7}\times 1500 = 1.5\times10^{-4}\ \text{T}$$<p>The currents are antiparallel, so at the midpoint both fields point the same way and add:</p>$$B_{\text{net}} = 2B = 3\times10^{-4}\ \text{T} = 300\ \mu\text{T}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A ($300$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

The charged particle moving in a uniform magnetic field of $(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j})$ T has an acceleration $\left( 4\hat{i} - \frac{x}{2}\hat{j} \right)$ m/s$^2$. The value of $x$ is

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The magnetic force $\vec{F} = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$ is always perpendicular to $\vec{B}$. Since $\vec{a} = \vec{F}/m$, the acceleration must also be perpendicular to $\vec{B}$:</p>$$\vec{a}\cdot\vec{B} = 0$$

$$\left(4\hat{i} - \frac{x}{2}\hat{j}\right)\cdot(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}) = 0$$

$$4\times 3 + \left(-\frac{x}{2}\right)\times 2 = 12 - x = 0$$<p>Hence $x = 12$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 12</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

A particle of charge $q$ and mass $m$ is projected from origin with an initial velocity $\vec{v} = \left(\dfrac{v_0}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{x} + \dfrac{v_0}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{y}\right)$. There exists a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = B_0\hat{z}$ and a space varying electric field $\vec{E} = E_0 e^{-\lambda x}\hat{x}$ within the region $0 \leq x \leq L$. After travelling a distance such that $x$-coordinate has changed from $x = 0$ to $x = L$, the change in the kinetic energy is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>By the work-energy theorem, the change in kinetic energy equals the total work done. The magnetic force does no work (it is always perpendicular to velocity), so only the electric force contributes:</p>$$\Delta KE = W_E = \int \vec{F}_E\cdot d\vec{r} = \int_{x=0}^{x=L} qE_0 e^{-\lambda x}\,dx$$<p>Only the $x$-displacement matters since $\vec{E}$ points along $\hat{x}$. Integrating:</p>$$\Delta KE = qE_0\left[\frac{-e^{-\lambda x}}{\lambda}\right]_0^{L} = \frac{qE_0}{\lambda}\left[1 - e^{-\lambda L}\right]$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A ($\dfrac{q E_0}{\lambda}\left[1 - e^{-\lambda L}\right]$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

A current carrying circular loop of radius 2 cm with unit normal $\hat{n} = \frac{\hat{k} + \hat{i}}{\sqrt{2}}$ is placed in a magnetic field, $\vec{B} = B_0(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{k})$. If $B_0 = 4 \times 10^{-3}$ T and current $I = 100\sqrt{2}$ A, the torque experienced by the loop is __________ Wb.A. ($\pi = 3.14$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The magnetic moment is $\vec{m} = IA\,\hat{n}$ with $A = \pi r^2 = \pi(0.02)^2 = 4\pi\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2$.</p>$$\vec{m} = 100\sqrt{2}\times 4\pi\times10^{-4}\times\frac{\hat{k}+\hat{i}}{\sqrt{2}} = 100\times 4\pi\times10^{-4}(\hat{i}+\hat{k}) = 0.04\pi(\hat{i}+\hat{k})$$<p>The torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{m}\times\vec{B}$ with $\vec{B} = 4\times10^{-3}(3\hat{i}+2\hat{k}) = (0.012\hat{i} + 0.008\hat{k})$:</p>$$\vec{\tau} = 0.04\pi(\hat{i}+\hat{k})\times(0.012\hat{i}+0.008\hat{k})$$

$$\hat{i}\times\hat{k} = -\hat{j},\quad \hat{k}\times\hat{i} = \hat{j}$$

$$\vec{\tau} = 0.04\pi\big[0.008(\hat{i}\times\hat{k}) + 0.012(\hat{k}\times\hat{i})\big] = 0.04\pi\big[-0.008\hat{j} + 0.012\hat{j}\big]$$

$$\vec{\tau} = 0.04\pi\times 0.004\,\hat{j} = 1.6\times10^{-4}\pi\,\hat{j} = 5.024\times10^{-4}\,\hat{j} = 5024\times10^{-7}\,\hat{j}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D ($5024 \times 10^{-7}\,\hat{j}$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

A 5 mg particle carrying a charge of $5\pi \times 10^{-6}$ C is moving with velocity of $(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{k}) \times 10^{-2}$ m/s in a region having magnetic field $\vec{B} = 0.1\hat{k}$ Wb/m$^2$. It moves a distance of $\alpha$ meter along $\hat{k}$ when it completes 5 revolutions. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The velocity component along $\hat{k}$ (parallel to $\vec{B}$) is unaffected by the field and produces the drift along the axis; the perpendicular component causes circular motion, giving helical motion.</p><p>Period of one revolution:</p>$$T = \frac{2\pi m}{qB} = \frac{2\pi\times 5\times10^{-6}}{5\pi\times10^{-6}\times 0.1} = \frac{2\pi\times 5\times10^{-6}}{5\pi\times10^{-7}} = 20\ \text{s}$$<p>Pitch (axial distance per revolution) using $v_z = 2\times10^{-2}$ m/s:</p>$$\text{pitch} = v_z\,T = 2\times10^{-2}\times 20 = 0.4\ \text{m}$$<p>Distance along $\hat{k}$ in 5 revolutions:</p>$$\alpha = 5\times 0.4 = 2\ \text{m}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 2</p></div></details>
