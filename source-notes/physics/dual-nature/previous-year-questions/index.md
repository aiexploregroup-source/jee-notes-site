PYQ · 2026 Apr 2, Shift 1

For a certain metal, when monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda$ is incident, the stopping potential for photoelectrons is $3V_o$. When the same metal is illuminated by light of wavelength $2\lambda$, then the stopping potential becomes $V_o$. The threshold wavelength for photoelectric emission for the given metal is $\alpha\lambda$. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Einstein’s photoelectric equation: $eV_s = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi$.</p><p><strong>Case 1</strong> (wavelength $\lambda$, stopping potential $3V_o$):</p>$$3eV_o = \frac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi$$<p><strong>Case 2</strong> (wavelength $2\lambda$, stopping potential $V_o$):</p>$$eV_o = \frac{hc}{2\lambda} - \phi$$<p><strong>Subtract:</strong></p>$$2eV_o = \frac{hc}{\lambda} - \frac{hc}{2\lambda} = \frac{hc}{2\lambda} \implies eV_o = \frac{hc}{4\lambda}$$<p><strong>Work function</strong> (from Case 2):</p>$$\phi = \frac{hc}{2\lambda} - eV_o = \frac{hc}{2\lambda} - \frac{hc}{4\lambda} = \frac{hc}{4\lambda}$$<p><strong>Threshold wavelength:</strong> $\phi = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0}$</p>$$\lambda_0 = \frac{hc}{\phi} = \frac{hc}{hc/4\lambda} = 4\lambda \implies \alpha = 4$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($\alpha = 4$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 2

Match the LIST-I with LIST-II

**List-I:** A. Planck’s constant; B. Stopping potential; C. Work function; D. Threshold frequency

**List-II:** I. $\mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-2}}$; II. $\mathrm{T^{-1}}$; III. $\mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-1}}$; IV. $\mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-3}\,A^{-1}}$

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>A. Planck’s constant</strong> — $E = h\nu \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{E}{\nu}$, units $\mathrm{J\,s}$:</p>$$[h] = \frac{\mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-2}}}{\mathrm{T^{-1}}} = \mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-1}} \;\to\; \text{III}$$<p><strong>B. Stopping potential</strong> — a potential (volt) $= \dfrac{\text{energy}}{\text{charge}}$:</p>$$[V] = \frac{\mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-2}}}{\mathrm{A\,T}} = \mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-3}\,A^{-1}} \;\to\; \text{IV}$$<p><strong>C. Work function</strong> — an energy:</p>$$[\phi] = \mathrm{M\,L^2\,T^{-2}} \;\to\; \text{I}$$<p><strong>D. Threshold frequency</strong> — a frequency:</p>$$[\nu_0] = \mathrm{T^{-1}} \;\to\; \text{II}$$<p>Hence <strong>A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> A</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 4, Shift 2

The de Broglie wavelength associated with an electron accelerated through a potential difference $V$ is $\lambda_e$ and the de Broglie wavelength associated with a proton accelerated through the same potential difference is $\lambda_p$. If their corresponding masses are $m_e$ and $m_p$, respectively, then the ratio of their de Broglie wavelengths $\left(\dfrac{\lambda_e}{\lambda_p}\right)$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>A charge $q$ accelerated through $V$ gains kinetic energy $qV = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$, so momentum $p = \sqrt{2mqV}$.</p>$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}$$<p>The electron and proton carry the same charge magnitude $e$ and are accelerated through the same $V$:</p>$$\frac{\lambda_e}{\lambda_p} = \frac{h/\sqrt{2m_e eV}}{h/\sqrt{2m_p eV}} = \sqrt{\frac{m_p}{m_e}}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A $\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}}\right)$</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 6, Shift 2

The de Broglie wavelength for an electron accelerated through the potential difference of $V_1$ volt is $\lambda_1$. When the potential difference is changed to $V_2$ volt, the associated de Broglie wavelength is increased by 50%. If $\left(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2}\right)=\dfrac{9}{\alpha}$, then the value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For an electron accelerated through $V$:</p>$$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}} \implies \lambda \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$$<p>A 50% increase means $\lambda_2 = 1.5\,\lambda_1$:</p>$$\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \sqrt{\frac{V_1}{V_2}} = 1.5$$<p>Square both sides:</p>$$\frac{V_1}{V_2} = (1.5)^2 = 2.25 = \frac{9}{4}$$<p>Comparing with $\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{9}{\alpha}$ gives $\alpha = 4$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 4</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 1

An electron of mass $m$ is moving in an electric field $\vec{E} = -2E_0\hat{i}$ ($E_0$ = constant $> 0$), with an initial velocity $\vec{V} = v_0\hat{i}$ ($v_0$ = constant $> 0$). If $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{h}{4mv_0}$, its de Broglie wavelength at time $t$ is ______.
($e$ = charge of electron)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Force on the electron</strong> (charge $-e$):</p>$$\vec{F} = (-e)\vec{E} = (-e)(-2E_0\hat{i}) = 2eE_0\,\hat{i}$$<p>The force is along $+\hat{i}$, the same direction as the initial velocity, so the electron speeds up.</p><p><strong>Acceleration:</strong></p>$$a = \frac{2eE_0}{m}$$<p><strong>Velocity at time $t$:</strong></p>$$v = v_0 + at = v_0 + \frac{2eE_0}{m}t = v_0\left(1 + \frac{2E_0 e}{m}\frac{t}{v_0}\right)$$<p><strong>de Broglie wavelength:</strong> $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv}$. Using $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{h}{4mv_0} \Rightarrow \dfrac{h}{m} = 4\lambda_0 v_0$:</p>$$\lambda = \frac{h/m}{v} = \frac{4\lambda_0 v_0}{v_0\left(1 + \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right)} = \frac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 + \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 1

Light source having wavelength $331$ nm is used to generate photo-electrons whose stopping potential is $0.2$ V. The work function of the used metal in the experiment is $\alpha \times 10^{-19}$ J. The value of $\alpha$ is ______.
($h = 6.62 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C and $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Einstein’s equation: $\phi = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - eV_s$.</p><p><strong>Photon energy:</strong></p>$$\frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{(6.62\times10^{-34})(3\times10^{8})}{331\times10^{-9}} = \frac{1.986\times10^{-25}}{3.31\times10^{-7}} = 6.0\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$<p><strong>Energy carried by stopping potential:</strong></p>$$eV_s = (1.6\times10^{-19})(0.2) = 0.32\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$<p><strong>Work function:</strong></p>$$\phi = 6.0\times10^{-19} - 0.32\times10^{-19} = 5.68\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$<p>So $\alpha = 5.68$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($5.68$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 5, Shift 2

An electron is travelling with a velocity $v$ in free space and when it enters a medium, its velocity is reduced by 20%. The de Broglie wavelength of electron in the medium is $\alpha\lambda_0$, where $\lambda_0$ is its de Broglie wavelength in free space. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>de Broglie wavelength: $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv} \implies \lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{v}$ (mass constant).</p><p>Velocity reduced by 20%: $v' = 0.8\,v$.</p>$$\frac{\lambda'}{\lambda_0} = \frac{v}{v'} = \frac{v}{0.8\,v} = \frac{1}{0.8} = 1.25$$<p>So $\lambda' = 1.25\,\lambda_0 \implies \alpha = 1.25$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C (1.25)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 Apr 8, Shift 2

$K_1$ and $K_2$ be the maximum kinetic energies of photoelectrons emitted from a surface of a given material for the light of wavelength $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, respectively. If $\lambda_1 = 2\lambda_2$ then the work function of material is given by :

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Einstein’s equation gives the maximum kinetic energy $K = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi$:</p>$$K_1 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_1} - \phi, \qquad K_2 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_2} - \phi$$<p>Since $\lambda_1 = 2\lambda_2$, we have $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} = \dfrac{hc}{2\lambda_2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_2}$.</p><p>From the second equation, $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_2} = K_2 + \phi$, so:</p>$$K_1 = \frac{1}{2}(K_2 + \phi) - \phi = \frac{K_2}{2} - \frac{\phi}{2}$$<p>Multiply by 2 and rearrange:</p>$$2K_1 = K_2 - \phi \implies \phi = K_2 - 2K_1$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D ($K_2 - 2K_1$)</p></div></details>
