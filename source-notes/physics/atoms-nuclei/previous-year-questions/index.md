PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

Two nuclei of mass number 3 combine with another nucleus of mass number 4 to yield a nucleus of mass number 10. If the binding energy per nucleon for the mass numbers 3, 4 and 10 are $5.6$ MeV, $7.4$ MeV and $6.1$ MeV, respectively, then in the process, $\Delta M c^2 = $ _____ MeV.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Reaction:</strong> two nuclei of $A=3$ plus one nucleus of $A=4$ give one nucleus of $A=10$.</p><p><strong>Step 1 — Total binding energy of reactants:</strong></p>$$BE_{\text{react}} = 2(3 \times 5.6) + (4 \times 7.4) = 33.6 + 29.6 = 63.2\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Binding energy of product:</strong></p>$$BE_{\text{prod}} = 10 \times 6.1 = 61.0\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Mass-energy change:</strong> The bound-mass difference corresponds to</p>$$\Delta M c^2 = \left| BE_{\text{prod}} - BE_{\text{react}} \right| = |61.0 - 63.2| = 2.2\ \text{MeV}$$<p>Since the product is <em>less</em> tightly bound, the reaction is endothermic; the magnitude $\Delta M c^2 = 2.2$ MeV.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

The ratio of momentum of the photons of the $1^{st}$ and $2^{nd}$ line of Balmer series of Hydrogen atoms is $\alpha/\beta$. The possible values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are:-

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Photon momentum $p = E/c \propto \bar{\nu}$ (wavenumber). For the Balmer series ($n_f = 2$):</p>$$\bar{\nu} = R\left(\frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$<p><strong>1st line</strong> ($n = 3$):</p>$$\bar{\nu}_1 = R\left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{9}\right) = R\cdot\frac{5}{36}$$<p><strong>2nd line</strong> ($n = 4$):</p>$$\bar{\nu}_2 = R\left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{16}\right) = R\cdot\frac{3}{16}$$<p><strong>Ratio:</strong></p>$$\frac{p_1}{p_2} = \frac{5/36}{3/16} = \frac{5 \times 16}{36 \times 3} = \frac{80}{108} = \frac{20}{27}$$<p>So $\alpha = 20,\ \beta = 27$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

The energy released when $\dfrac{7}{17.13}$ kg of $_3^7\text{Li}$ is converted into $_2^4\text{He}$ by proton bombardment is $\alpha \times 10^{32}$ eV. The value of $\alpha$ is ________. (Nearest integer) (Mass of $_3^7\text{Li} = 7.0183$ u, mass of $_2^4\text{He} = 4.004$ u, mass of proton = 1.008 u and 1 u = 931 MeV/c$^2$ and Avogadro number = $6.0 \times 10^{23}$)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Reaction:</strong> $_3^7\text{Li} + {}_1^1\text{p} \rightarrow 2\,{}_2^4\text{He}$</p><p><strong>Step 1 — Mass defect per reaction:</strong></p>$$\Delta m = (7.0183 + 1.008) - 2(4.004) = 8.0263 - 8.008 = 0.0183\ \text{u}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Energy per reaction:</strong></p>$$E_1 = 0.0183 \times 931 = 17.04\ \text{MeV} = 1.704 \times 10^{7}\ \text{eV}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Number of Li nuclei</strong> in $\dfrac{7}{17.13}\ \text{kg} = 408.64\ \text{g}$:</p>$$N = \frac{408.64}{7.0183} \times 6.0 \times 10^{23} \approx 3.49 \times 10^{25}$$<p><strong>Step 4 — Total energy</strong> (one Li consumed per reaction):</p>$$E = N \times E_1 = 3.49 \times 10^{25} \times 1.704 \times 10^{7} \approx 5.95 \times 10^{32}\ \text{eV}$$<p>So $\alpha \approx 5.95 \Rightarrow 6$ (nearest integer).</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> 6</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 1

Angular momentum of an electron in a hydrogen atom is $\dfrac{3h}{\pi}$, then the energy of the electron is __________ eV.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — Find $n$ from Bohr quantization:</strong></p>$$L = \frac{nh}{2\pi} = \frac{3h}{\pi} \implies \frac{n}{2} = 3 \implies n = 6$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Energy of the level:</strong></p>$$E_n = -\frac{13.6}{n^2}\ \text{eV} = -\frac{13.6}{36} = -0.378\ \text{eV} \approx -0.38\ \text{eV}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

In Rutherford’s alpha-particle scattering experiment, only a few alpha particles rebound back because

A. The size of gold nucleus is very small as compared to the size of gold atom.
B. Alpha particle and gold nucleus have equal charge.
C. The impact parameter is minimum for a few alpha particles.
D. A few alpha particles have very high kinetic energy.
E. Only a few alpha particles undergo head-on collision with the nuclei.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Evaluate each statement:</p><ul><li><strong>A — True.</strong> The nucleus occupies a tiny fraction of the atom, so most $\alpha$-particles pass undeflected and only a few come close enough to rebound.</li><li><strong>B — False.</strong> The gold nucleus has charge $+79e$; the $\alpha$-particle has $+2e$. Their charges are not equal.</li><li><strong>C — True.</strong> Large-angle (rebound) scattering occurs only for the few particles aimed nearly at the nucleus, i.e. with <strong>minimum impact parameter</strong>.</li><li><strong>D — False.</strong> The incident $\alpha$-particles all have essentially the same kinetic energy; rebound is not caused by a few having higher energy.</li><li><strong>E — True.</strong> Only the few particles undergoing near head-on collisions are turned back through large angles.</li></ul><p>Correct statements: <strong>A, C, E</strong>.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> D</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

The binding energy per nucleon of $^{209}_{83}Bi$ is __________ MeV. [Take $m\left(^{209}_{83}Bi\right) = 208.980388\,u$, $m_p = 1.007825\,u$, $m_n = 1.008665\,u$, $1\,u = 931\,\text{MeV/c}^2$]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For $^{209}_{83}\text{Bi}$: $Z = 83$ protons, $N = 209 - 83 = 126$ neutrons.</p><p><strong>Step 1 — Mass defect</strong> (using $m_p$ = hydrogen-atom mass, electron masses cancel):</p>$$\Delta m = 83(1.007825) + 126(1.008665) - 208.980388$$<p></p>$$\Delta m = 83.649475 + 127.09179 - 208.980388 = 1.760877\ \text{u}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Total binding energy:</strong></p>$$BE = 1.760877 \times 931 = 1639.4\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Binding energy per nucleon:</strong></p>$$\frac{BE}{A} = \frac{1639.4}{209} = 7.84\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

Using Bohr’s model, calculate the ratio of the magnetic fields generated due to the motion of the electrons in the $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$ orbits of hydrogen atom __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The electron orbiting at radius $r$ with speed $v$ acts as a current loop producing a field at the nucleus:</p>$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2r}, \qquad I = \frac{e v}{2\pi r} \implies B = \frac{\mu_0 e v}{4\pi r^2}$$<p><strong>Step 1 — Bohr scaling</strong> ($v \propto 1/n$, $r \propto n^2$):</p>$$B \propto \frac{v}{r^2} \propto \frac{1/n}{n^4} = \frac{1}{n^5}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Take the ratio for $n = 2$ and $n = 4$:</strong></p>$$\frac{B_2}{B_4} = \frac{n_4^5}{n_2^5} = \left(\frac{4}{2}\right)^5 = 2^5 = 32$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 32</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

The energy released if hydrogen atoms are combined to form $^4_2\text{He}$ is __________ MeV. (Take binding energies per nucleon of $^2_1\text{H}$ and $^4_2\text{He}$ as $1.1$ MeV and $7.2$ MeV, respectively)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The given binding energy per nucleon for $^2_1\text{H}$ (deuterium) tells us the fusion is:</p>$$2\,{}^2_1\text{H} \rightarrow {}^4_2\text{He}$$<p><strong>Step 1 — Binding energy of reactants</strong> (two deuterons, 2 nucleons each):</p>$$BE_{\text{react}} = 2 \times (2 \times 1.1) = 4.4\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Binding energy of product</strong> ($^4_2\text{He}$, 4 nucleons):</p>$$BE_{\text{prod}} = 4 \times 7.2 = 28.8\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Energy released:</strong></p>$$Q = BE_{\text{prod}} - BE_{\text{react}} = 28.8 - 4.4 = 24.4\ \text{MeV}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 1

In the hydrogen atom, the electron makes a transition from the higher orbit ($i$) to a lower orbit ($f$). The ratio of the radius of the orbits is given by $r_i : r_f = 16 : 4$. The wavelength of photon emitted due to this transition is ________ nm.
(Given Rydberg constant $= 1.0973 \times 10^7$ /m)

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p><strong>Step 1 — Find the orbit numbers</strong> using $r \propto n^2$:</p>$$r_i \propto n_i^2 = 16 \implies n_i = 4, \qquad r_f \propto n_f^2 = 4 \implies n_f = 2$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Apply the Rydberg formula:</strong></p>$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\right) = 1.0973 \times 10^7\left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{16}\right)$$<p></p>$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = 1.0973 \times 10^7 \times \frac{3}{16} = 2.057 \times 10^6\ \text{m}^{-1}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Wavelength:</strong></p>$$\lambda = \frac{1}{2.057 \times 10^6} = 4.86 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m} = 486\ \text{nm}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

Assuming the experimental mass of ${}^{12}_{6}C$ as $12$ u, the mass defect of ${}^{12}_{6}C$ atom is __________ MeV/$c^2$. (Mass of proton = $1.00727$ u, mass of neutron = $1.00866$ u, $1$ u = $931.5$ MeV/$c^2$ and $c$ is the speed of the light in vacuum).

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>$^{12}_{6}\text{C}$ has $6$ protons and $6$ neutrons.</p><p><strong>Step 1 — Mass defect:</strong></p>$$\Delta m = 6\,m_p + 6\,m_n - m(^{12}_6\text{C})$$<p></p>$$\Delta m = 6(1.00727) + 6(1.00866) - 12 = 6.04362 + 6.05196 - 12 = 0.09558\ \text{u}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — Convert to MeV/$c^2$:</strong></p>$$\Delta m = 0.09558 \times 931.5 = 89.03\ \text{MeV}/c^2$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 8 Apr, Shift 2

Two radioactive substances A and B of mass numbers 200 and 212 respectively, shows spontaneous $\alpha$-decay with same Q value of $1$ MeV. The ratio of energies of $\alpha$-rays produced by A and B is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>When a parent (mass number $A$) at rest emits an $\alpha$-particle ($A_\alpha = 4$), momentum conservation gives the $\alpha$-particle kinetic energy:</p>$$K_\alpha = Q \cdot \frac{A - 4}{A}$$<p><strong>Step 1 — For A</strong> ($A = 200$):</p>$$K_A = Q \cdot \frac{196}{200}$$<p><strong>Step 2 — For B</strong> ($A = 212$):</p>$$K_B = Q \cdot \frac{208}{212}$$<p><strong>Step 3 — Ratio</strong> (same $Q$):</p>$$\frac{K_A}{K_B} = \frac{196/200}{208/212} = \frac{196 \times 212}{200 \times 208} = \frac{41552}{41600} = \frac{2597}{2600}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C</p></div></details>
