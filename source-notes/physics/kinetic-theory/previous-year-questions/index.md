PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 1

One gas of $n_1$ mole of molecules at temperature $T_1$, volume $V_1$, and pressure $P_1$, and another gas of $n_2$ mole of molecules at temperature $T_2$, volume $V_2$, and pressure $P_2$, are mixed resulting in pressure $P$ and volume $V$ of the mixture. The temperature of the mixture is _____.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The total number of moles is conserved when the two gases are mixed:</p>$$n = n_1 + n_2$$<p>Using the ideal gas law $PV = nRT$ for each component and for the mixture:</p>$$n_1 = \frac{P_1 V_1}{R T_1}, \qquad n_2 = \frac{P_2 V_2}{R T_2}, \qquad n = \frac{P V}{R T}$$<p>Substituting into $n = n_1 + n_2$:</p>$$\frac{P V}{R T} = \frac{P_1 V_1}{R T_1} + \frac{P_2 V_2}{R T_2}$$<p>Cancelling $R$ and combining the right side over a common denominator:</p>$$\frac{P V}{T} = \frac{P_1 V_1 T_2 + P_2 V_2 T_1}{T_1 T_2}$$<p>Solving for the mixture temperature $T$:</p>$$T = \frac{T_1 T_2 \, P V}{T_2 P_1 V_1 + T_1 P_2 V_2}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> B ($T_1 T_2 PV/(T_2 P_1 V_1 + T_1 P_2 V_2)$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 1

Two closed vessels of same volume are joined through a narrow tube and both vessels are filled with air of pressure 90 kPa and temperature 400 K. Keeping the temperature of one vessel constant at 400 K the second vessel temperature is raised to 500 K. The final pressure in the vessels is ________ kPa.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>Let each vessel have volume $V$. Initially both are at $P_0 = 90\,\text{kPa}$ and $T = 400\,\text{K}$, so the total number of moles is:</p>$$n = \frac{P_0 V}{R (400)} + \frac{P_0 V}{R (400)} = \frac{2 P_0 V}{400\,R}$$<p>The vessels stay connected, so after heating they share a common final pressure $P$. Vessel 1 remains at 400 K and vessel 2 is raised to 500 K:</p>$$n = \frac{P V}{400\,R} + \frac{P V}{500\,R}$$<p>Since the total moles are conserved, equate the two expressions and cancel $V/R$:</p>$$\frac{2 P_0}{400} = \frac{P}{400} + \frac{P}{500}$$

$$\frac{2(90)}{400} = P\left(\frac{1}{400} + \frac{1}{500}\right) = P\left(\frac{9}{2000}\right)$$

$$0.45 = P \times \frac{9}{2000} \quad\Rightarrow\quad P = 0.45 \times \frac{2000}{9} = 100\,\text{kPa}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A (100)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 4 Apr, Shift 2

Given below are two statements: one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R

Assertion A: If the average kinetic energy of H$_2$ and O$_2$ molecules, kept in two different sized containers are same, then their temperatures will be same.

Reason R: The r.m.s. speed of H$_2$ and O$_2$ molecules are same at same temperature.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>The average translational kinetic energy of a gas molecule depends only on temperature:</p>$$\langle KE \rangle = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$$<p>It is independent of the molecular mass and of the container size. So if H$_2$ and O$_2$ have the same average kinetic energy, they must be at the same temperature. <strong>Assertion A is true.</strong></p><p>The root-mean-square speed does depend on the molecular mass:</p>$$v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3 k_B T}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 R T}{M}}$$<p>At the same temperature, since $M_{\text{H}_2} = 2\,\text{g/mol} \ne M_{\text{O}_2} = 32\,\text{g/mol}$, the lighter H$_2$ molecules move much faster than O$_2$. Their rms speeds are <strong>not</strong> the same. <strong>Reason R is false.</strong></p><p>Therefore A is true but R is false.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C (A is true but R is false)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 2 Apr, Shift 2

A mixture of carbon dioxide and oxygen has volume $8310\,\text{cm}^3$, temperature 300 K, pressure 100 kPa and mass 13.2 g. The number of moles of carbon dioxide and oxygen gases in the mixture respectively are __________. (Assume both carbon dioxide and oxygen gases behave like ideal gases) [$R = 8.31\,\text{J/mol.K}$]

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>First find the total number of moles from the ideal gas law $PV = nRT$, with $P = 100\times10^3\,\text{Pa}$, $V = 8310\,\text{cm}^3 = 8310\times10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$:</p>$$n = \frac{PV}{RT} = \frac{(100\times10^3)(8310\times10^{-6})}{(8.31)(300)} = \frac{831}{2493} = \frac{1}{3}\,\text{mol}$$<p>Let $a$ = moles of CO$_2$ (molar mass $44\,\text{g/mol}$) and $b$ = moles of O$_2$ (molar mass $32\,\text{g/mol}$). Then:</p>$$a + b = \frac{1}{3}, \qquad 44a + 32b = 13.2$$<p>Substitute $b = \tfrac{1}{3} - a$ into the mass equation:</p>$$44a + 32\left(\frac{1}{3} - a\right) = 13.2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 12a + \frac{32}{3} = 13.2$$

$$12a = 13.2 - 10.667 = 2.533 \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = 0.211 \approx 0.21$$

$$b = \frac{1}{3} - 0.211 = 0.122 \approx 0.12$$<p>So there are about $0.21$ mol of CO$_2$ and $0.12$ mol of O$_2$.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($0.21$ and $0.12$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 6 Apr, Shift 2

If 2 mole of an ideal monoatomic gas at temperature $T$, is mixed with 6 mole of another ideal monoatomic gas at temperature $2T$ then the temperature of mixture is:

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>When the two gases are mixed without any external work or heat exchange, the total internal energy is conserved. For an ideal monoatomic gas $U = \dfrac{3}{2} n R T$, so:</p>$$U_1 + U_2 = U_{\text{mix}}$$

$$\frac{3}{2} n_1 R T_1 + \frac{3}{2} n_2 R T_2 = \frac{3}{2}(n_1 + n_2) R T_{\text{mix}}$$<p>Cancelling $\dfrac{3}{2}R$ throughout:</p>$$n_1 T_1 + n_2 T_2 = (n_1 + n_2) T_{\text{mix}}$$<p>Substitute $n_1 = 2,\ T_1 = T,\ n_2 = 6,\ T_2 = 2T$:</p>$$(2)(T) + (6)(2T) = (2 + 6)\, T_{\text{mix}}$$

$$2T + 12T = 8\, T_{\text{mix}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 14T = 8\, T_{\text{mix}}$$

$$T_{\text{mix}} = \frac{14T}{8} = \frac{7}{4}T$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> D ($\dfrac{7}{4}T$)</p></div></details>

PYQ · 2026 5 Apr, Shift 2

An ideal gas at pressure $P$ and temperature $T$ is expanding such that $PT^3 = $ constant. The coefficient of volume expansion of the gas is __________.

<details class="solution"><p>**Solution / Answer**</p><summary class="solution-summary"><span class="solution-btn">Show solution</span></summary><div class="solution-body"><p>For an ideal gas, $PV = nRT$, so the pressure can be written as:</p>$$P = \frac{nRT}{V}$$<p>Substitute this into the given condition $PT^3 = \text{constant}$:</p>$$\frac{nRT}{V}\, T^3 = \text{constant} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{T^4}{V} = \text{constant}$$<p>This means $V \propto T^4$, so we can write $V = c\,T^4$ for some constant $c$. The coefficient of volume expansion is defined as:</p>$$\gamma = \frac{1}{V}\frac{dV}{dT}$$<p>Differentiating $V = c\,T^4$:</p>$$\frac{dV}{dT} = 4 c\, T^3 = \frac{4V}{T}$$<p>Therefore:</p>$$\gamma = \frac{1}{V}\cdot\frac{4V}{T} = \frac{4}{T}$$<p><strong>Answer:</strong> C ($\dfrac{4}{T}$)</p></div></details>
