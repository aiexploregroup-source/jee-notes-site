> Week 5 of Mission JEE 2027 · Spin City — Rotation is JEE's favourite mechanics chapter; thermo is chemistry's; trigonometry is everywhere. See the full plan .

Ride your scooter at walking pace and it wobbles like it’s had too much chai; take it past twenty and it holds a line like it’s on rails. That stability is a spinning wheel’s angular momentum resisting whatever tries to tip it - rotational motion, doing crowd control on two wheels.

Park it in the sun for an hour and touch the engine casing - thermodynamics, specifically the first law refusing to let that heat just vanish, it has to go somewhere, usually your palm. The lean angle you take into a turn is a trig identity you’ve been solving by feel since you were twelve.

You already know this stuff in your hands. Prove it on paper - today’s three questions are waiting.

## Rotational Motion

JEE Main 2025
Evening

A solid sphere and a hollow sphere of the same mass and of same radius are rolled on an inclined plane. Let the time taken to reach the bottom by the solid sphere and the hollow sphere be $t_1$ and $t_2$, respectively, then

- **A.** $t_1< t_2$

- **B.** $t_1=2 t_2$

- **C.** $t_1 >t_2$

- **D.** $t_1=t_2$

**Solution / Answer**

![](https://app-content.cdn.examgoal.net/fly/@width/image/jaoe38c1m792n65f/b4bb6efe-8fc9-4114-9a23-d252f33db80f/4fb72330-ed30-11ef-9fee-319e7f181762/file-jaoe38c1m792n65g.png)

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathrm{t}=\sqrt{\frac{2 \ell}{\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{cm}}}} \\ & \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{cm}}=\frac{\mathrm{g} \sin \theta}{1+\frac{\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{cm}}}{\mathrm{MR}^2}} \\ & \mathrm{a}_1=\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{cm}_1}=\frac{5 \mathrm{~g} \sin \theta}{7} \ldots . . \text { Solid } \\ & \mathrm{a}_2=\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{cm}_2}=\frac{3 \mathrm{~g} \sin \theta}{5} \ldots . \text { Hollow } \\ & \mathrm{a}_1>\mathrm{a}_2 \\ & \mathrm{t}_1<\mathrm{t}_2 \end{aligned}$$

**Correct option:** A

## Thermodynamics

The formation enthalpies, $\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\ominus}$ for $\mathrm{H}_{(\mathrm{g})}$ and $\mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{g})}$ are 220.0 and $250.0 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$, respectively, at 298.15 K , and $\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\ominus}$ for $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{g})}$ is $-242.0 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$ at the same temperature. The average bond enthalpy of the $\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{H}$ bond in water at 298.15 K is _______ $\mathrm{kJ} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$ (nearest integer).

Numerical answer — work it out, then check the solution.

**Solution / Answer**

$$\begin{array}{ll} \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \rightarrow \mathrm{H}(\mathrm{~g}) \quad ;\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} \mathrm{H}\left(\mathrm{H}_{(\mathrm{g})}\right)=220 \mathrm{KJ} / \mathrm{mol} \\ \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \rightarrow \mathrm{O}(\mathrm{~g}) \quad ; \Delta_{\mathrm{f}} \mathrm{H}\left(\mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{g})}\right)=250 \mathrm{KJ} / \mathrm{mol} \end{array}$$

## Trigonometry

JEE Main 2026
8 Apr, Shift 2

Let $\alpha = 3\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{6}{11}\right)$ and $\beta = 3\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{4}{9}\right)$, where inverse trigonometric functions take only the principal values.

Given below are two statements :

Statement I : $\cos(\alpha + \beta) > 0$.

Statement II : $\cos(\alpha) < 0$.

In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

- **A.** Both Statement I and Statement II are true

- **B.** Both Statement I and Statement II are false

- **C.** Statement I is true but Statement II is false

- **D.** Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**Solution / Answer**

**Statement II.** Since $\dfrac{6}{11} > \dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$? Check the size directly: $\sin^{-1}\dfrac{6}{11} \approx 33.06^\circ$, so

$$\alpha = 3\sin^{-1}\frac{6}{11} \approx 99.2^\circ.$$

Because $90^\circ < \alpha < 180^\circ$, we get $\cos\alpha < 0$. **Statement II is true.**

**Statement I.** Also $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{4}{9} \approx 63.6^\circ$, so

$$\beta = 3\cos^{-1}\frac{4}{9} \approx 190.8^\circ,\qquad \alpha + \beta \approx 290.0^\circ.$$

An angle near $290^\circ$ lies in the fourth quadrant, where cosine is positive, so $\cos(\alpha+\beta) > 0$. **Statement I is true.**

Both statements are true.

**Answer:** A

**Correct option:** A
