> Week 3 of Mission JEE 2027 · Forces of Habit — Newton's laws, periodic trends, and patterns in sequences — the week of rules that repeat. See the full plan .

Take a sharp turn in an auto and your bag slides across the seat, while everything hanging tilts outward like it has an opinion. That tilt is not decoration — it is a pendulum settling at exactly the angle where the forces balance inside a turning frame.

Today’s physics puts a pendulum in a car doing 54 km/h around a 20 m turn and asks for precisely that angle. Week 3, day 3: chemistry estimates magnesium’s second ionisation enthalpy from its first — the periodic table’s habits at work — and maths interleaves an A.P. with an increasing G.P.

Three questions. Hold the handle, find the angle.

Physics

## Laws of Motion

JEE Main 2026
8 Apr, Shift 2

A car moving with a speed of 54 km/h takes a turn of radius 20 m. A simple pendulum is suspended from the ceiling of the car. Determine the angle made by the string of the pendulum with the vertical during the turning. (Take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)

- **A.** $\tan^{-1}(0.5)$

- **B.** $\tan^{-1}(0.75)$

- **C.** $\tan^{-1}(1.125)$

- **D.** $\tan^{-1}(0.25)$

**Solution / Answer**

Convert the speed: $v = 54\ \text{km/h} = 54 \times \dfrac{5}{18} = 15\ \text{m/s}$.

In the car’s (non-inertial) frame, the bob experiences a pseudo (centrifugal) force $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ horizontally outward and gravity $mg$ downward. At equilibrium the string tilts by angle $\theta$ from the vertical:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{mv^2/r}{mg} = \frac{v^2}{rg}$$

**Substitute values:**

$$\tan\theta = \frac{(15)^2}{20 \times 10} = \frac{225}{200} = 1.125$$

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.125)$$

**Answer:** C ($\tan^{-1}(1.125)$)

**Correct option:** C

Chemistry

## Classification of Elements and Periodicity

JEE Main 2026
4 Apr, Shift 2

The 1$^{st}$ ionisation enthalpy for Mg is $+737$ kJ/mol. The most probable estimated value of the 2$^{nd}$ ionisation enthalpy of Mg is __________.

- **A.** $-906$ kJ/mol

- **B.** $-856$ kJ/mol

- **C.** $+1450$ kJ/mol

- **D.** $+590$ kJ/mol

**Solution / Answer**

Ionisation enthalpies are always **endothermic (positive)**, so the answer cannot be negative — this rules out $-906$ and $-856$.

The second ionisation removes an electron from a **positive ion** $\left(\text{Mg}^+\right)$, where the remaining electrons are held more tightly by the same nuclear charge. Hence

$$IE_2 > IE_1 = 737 \text{ kJ/mol}$$

This rules out $+590$. The only value that is both positive and greater than $737$ is $+1450$ kJ/mol. (The experimental value of $IE_2$ for Mg is $\approx 1451$ kJ/mol.)

**Answer:** C ($+1450$ kJ/mol)

**Correct option:** C

## Sequences and Series

JEE Main 2026
2 Apr, Shift 2

Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ be an A.P. and $g_1 = a_1, g_2, g_3, \ldots$ be an increasing G.P. If $a_1 = a_2 + g_2 = 1$ and $a_3 + g_3 = 4$, then $a_{10} + g_5$ is equal to :

- **A.** $81$

- **B.** $76$

- **C.** $62$

- **D.** $55$

**Solution / Answer**

Let the A.P. have first term $a_1 = 1$ and common difference $d$, and the G.P. have $g_1 = 1$ and ratio $r$ (with $r > 1$ since it is increasing).

From $a_2 + g_2 = 1$:

$$(1 + d) + r = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; d = -r.$$

From $a_3 + g_3 = 4$:

$$(1 + 2d) + r^2 = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; 1 - 2r + r^2 = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; r^2 - 2r - 3 = 0.$$

So $(r - 3)(r + 1) = 0$, giving $r = 3$ (taking the increasing root). Then $d = -3$.

$$a_{10} = 1 + 9d = 1 - 27 = -26, \qquad g_5 = r^4 = 81.$$

$$a_{10} + g_5 = -26 + 81 = 55.$$

**Answer:** D ($55$)

**Correct option:** D
